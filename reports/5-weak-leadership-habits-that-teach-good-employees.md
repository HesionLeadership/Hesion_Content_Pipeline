# 5 Weak Leadership Habits That Teach Good Employees to Stay Quiet, Wait, and Give Less

**Source:** Inc  
**Published:** Sat, 05 Sep 2026 05:30:00 -0400  
**URL:** [https://www.inc.com/marcel-schwantes/5-weak-leadership-habits/91400763](https://www.inc.com/marcel-schwantes/5-weak-leadership-habits/91400763)

## Summary
The article identifies 5 weak leadership habits that cause good employees to disengage: (1) making decisions without explanation, (2) avoiding difficult conversations, (3) rewarding visibility over value, (4) responding to feedback with defensiveness, and (5) being inconsistent between words and actions. These behaviors erode trust and teach high performers to stay quiet, wait passively, and eventually reduce their effort or leave.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
These habits directly undermine psychological safety, procedural justice, and trust—core constructs in organizational psychology that predict employee voice behavior, engagement, and retention. When leaders fail to explain decisions or respond defensively to feedback, they create conditions where employee silence becomes a rational adaptive response.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should audit leadership behaviors for these trust-eroding patterns, particularly decision transparency, feedback receptivity, and consistency between stated values and actions. Building structured feedback mechanisms and training managers in procedural justice practices can prevent the quiet disengagement that precedes turnover of top talent.
