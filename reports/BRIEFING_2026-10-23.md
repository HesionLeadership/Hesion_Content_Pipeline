# Morning Briefing — October 23, 2026

## This Week's Themes
The dominant theme this week is the gap between AI rhetoric and operational reality: **AI is everywhere except in the GDP numbers — here is what triggers the break** (Fortune), **Twilio CMO: We shrank 2 weeks of work down to one day with AI. Then the unsexy work began** (Fortune), and **The Hidden Upside of Tech Challenges** (MIT Sloan Review) all point to the same truth—tools alone don't transform organizations; unglamorous infrastructure work and cognitive framing do. A second theme is workforce anxiety and trust erosion amid AI-driven disruption, running through **AI is killing 10,000-plus jobs per month and 9% of jobs are at risk, analysts say** (Fortune), **Leaders Need to Grapple with Employee Ambivalence on AI** (HBR), and **Is an 'AI war' complicating your job search?** (BBC Business). A third theme is governance and power asymmetry, visible in **Mark Zuckerberg's creative plan to pay California's proposed billionaire tax without losing control of Meta** (TechCrunch) and echoed in **Harvard Business School's former dean has studied CEOs for three decades. He says the role remains remarkably unchanged** (Fast Company). Finally, a human-sustainability thread runs through **The hidden cost of being the 'reliable one' at work** (Fast Company) and **Recognition is a talent strategy** (Fast Company), both pointing to how organizations under- or mis-invest in the people who hold things together.

## Top Story
**AI is everywhere except in the GDP numbers — here is what triggers the break** (Fortune) is the most compelling story because it reframes the AI productivity debate with hard data: 95% of enterprise AI deployments show zero P&L impact. For CHROs and COOs, this is the clearest evidence yet that bolting AI onto existing structures doesn't work—only organizations willing to redesign workflows, incentives, and roles will see the payoff, and the window to be in that 5% is now.

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**AI is killing 10,000-plus jobs per month and 9% of jobs are at risk, analysts say** (Fortune) has the deepest org psych angle and the most material for a 500+ word piece. It's not just a jobs-numbers story—it's a case study in how organizations manage collective anxiety during disruption. Pete could unpack: why displacement concentrated in accounting, advertising, admin, and legal creates a credibility crisis for leaders who claim "no jobs at risk"; how Goldman's 9% displacement estimate forces CHROs to choose between reactive layoffs and proactive reskilling pathways; and how trust erodes organization-wide—even among employees not directly affected—when transitions are handled opaquely. This pairs naturally with the GDP/productivity piece (same underlying J-curve dynamic) but focuses squarely on the human and trust dimension, which is Pete's core expertise. There's enough factual grounding here (specific sectors, specific percentages, the construction counter-trend from data center buildouts) to build a substantive argument without speculating beyond the source.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**Mark Zuckerberg's creative plan to pay California's proposed billionaire tax without losing control of Meta** (TechCrunch) is a strong live illustration of board capture and asymmetric power dynamics—a real, named, verifiable example (SEC filings, reported threats since July) that Pete can use to open a discussion on governance independence versus leader accommodation. It's vivid enough to hold a room: a CEO reportedly threatening to sell his home and trigger a media firestorm to get board action on his personal tax exposure is a gift for illustrating how "independent" boards can quietly become reactive to founder pressure.
