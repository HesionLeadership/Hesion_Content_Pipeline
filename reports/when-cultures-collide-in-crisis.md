## Summary
The article examines how cultural missteps in crisis communication—such as language choice, tone, and timing—can turn operational responses into reputational disasters, using Air Canada CEO Michael Rousseau's English-only statement after a fatal 2026 LaGuardia crash (which preceded his resignation) and a similar 2013 Lac-Mégantic rail disaster as parallel examples.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story illustrates how crisis leadership effectiveness depends heavily on perceived cultural attunement and symbolic communication (language, presence, humility) rather than just factual accuracy or operational competence, tying into theories of identity-based trust and intergroup perception during high-stakes events.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should build cross-cultural crisis communication protocols in advance—including language parity, local representation, and culturally attuned tone—since in moments of crisis, symbolic acts of respect and presence can outweigh operational clarity in sustaining stakeholder trust and leader legitimacy.
