# Morning Briefing — August 08, 2026

## This Week's Themes
This week's conversation is dominated by a single, loud signal: **the gap between what leaders intend and what organizations actually experience**. Whether it's founders who think they're driving creativity but are actually suppressing it, managers who say "be more strategic" and think they've given useful feedback, or HR departments that believe they're supporting employees while 86% of workers fear them—the pattern is intention-impact misalignment at every level. A second major theme is **psychological safety as a structural issue, not a sentiment**: multiple stories reveal that safety isn't experienced equally (women pay social costs for creativity, trauma survivors need intentionally designed cultures, anger-based leadership is creeping back in). Third, there's a strong thread around **embedding strategic priorities into systems rather than relying on rhetoric**—DBS Bank measuring innovation as a KPI, the call to decompose vague feedback into observable behaviors, and the argument that trust-building should outweigh AI fluency in succession planning all point to the same conclusion: if it's not in the structure, it's not in the culture.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**DBS Bank's innovation KPI system** is a perfect case study for any presentation on culture change or strategic execution. It concretely illustrates the principle that *what gets measured gets done*—and more importantly, it shows what it looks like when an organization moves innovation from aspirational value to operational reality. Pete can use this as a contrast case: "Most companies put innovation on a poster. DBS put it on every performance review at 20% weight. Which approach do you think changed behavior?" It cleanly demonstrates expectancy theory and goal-setting theory in action, and gives CHROs/COOs something specific to benchmark against.
