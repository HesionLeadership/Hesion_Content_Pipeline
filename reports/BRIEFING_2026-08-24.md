# Morning Briefing — August 24, 2026

## This Week's Themes
The dominant theme this week is **psychological safety under siege** — from leaders who use anger as a management tool, to a Meta CTO publicly humiliating an employee, to cultures that punish failure, the evidence is piling up that organizations are backsliding on the very conditions that enable performance. A second major thread is **systemic misalignment between what organizations say they want and what they actually reward** — innovation is rhetorically celebrated but structurally penalized, relational labor sustains teams but goes unmeasured, and AI productivity gains are captured by companies while employees absorb the accountability risk. **AI's impact on the employee experience** is also becoming unavoidable: algorithmic bias in hiring, murky accountability for AI-assisted decisions, and the framing of AI-driven productivity as the company's property are all creating new fault lines. Finally, **gendered structural inequity** shows up in two distinct stories — biased AI screening tools and invisible relational labor — suggesting this remains a persistent blind spot in talent systems. Taken together, this week's stories paint a picture of organizations that need serious structural and cultural recalibration, not just better intentions.

## Top Story
**When Employees Are Held Accountable for AI-Generated Decisions** (HBR) — This is the most strategically urgent story for CHROs and COOs right now. As organizations rapidly deploy AI tools, they're creating an accountability vacuum where employees must own decisions they didn't fully make, leading to covert workarounds that undermine both AI value and organizational transparency. This is a ticking time bomb in every organization scaling AI, and the leaders who design clear accountability architectures now will have a massive advantage over those who discover the problem after a costly failure.

Pete could unpack the research on non-promotable tasks (Linda Babcock's work), connect it to construct validity failures in performance evaluation systems, and lay out a concrete framework for how CHROs can audit and redistribute invisible relational labor. It's the kind of piece that positions Hesion as translating academic research into operational talent strategy — and it touches gender equity, performance management design, and leadership assessment all at once. Easily 500+ words of original analysis.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**Your Managers Aren't Blocking Innovation. Your Incentives Are** (Inc) — This is a perfect real-world illustration for any presentation on **the say-do gap in organizational culture**. Pete can use it to show how leaders declare they want innovation while their compensation structures, promotion criteria, and performance reviews systematically punish the very behaviors innovation requires. It's a clean, memorable example of reinforcement theory in action: "Your people aren't broken. Your system is working exactly as designed — just toward the wrong outcome." Works beautifully in workshops on culture change, performance management redesign, or strategic alignment.
