# Morning Briefing — November 11, 2026

## This Week's Themes

Three threads dominate this week. First, **key-person risk and governance fragility**—visible in **Can Nvidia’s Jensen Huang Rebuild Trust?** (HBR), **Jensen Huang is the AI boom’s key man. Now, that’s a problem** (Fortune), **Nvidia is investigating Jensen Huang. But its board may have no interest in challenging its leader** (Fast Company), and **The ‘charismatic’ leader trap: Why organizations shouldn’t depend on one individual, no matter how appealing** (Fast Company)—all pointing to the danger of organizations whose identity and oversight structures collapse into a single founder. Second, **AI is changing coordination, not just productivity**, seen in **Why AI-Powered Tiny Teams Need a New Operating Model** (HBR), **Databricks' new AI agent tells CEO Ali Ghodsi the unvarnished truth** (TechCrunch), and **‘Shock absorbers’: Korn Ferry says managers are carrying AI change that executives aren’t ready to lead** (Fortune), each showing that technology shifts the bottleneck to human sensemaking and cross-team trust rather than eliminating it. Third, **culture change requires bold, visible leadership moves**—**Inside Dan Schulman’s war on the old Verizon** (Fortune), **A CEO gave his employees Fridays off. Three years later, revenue is up about 160%** (Fortune), and **Coffee giant Dutch Bros made all of its executives, including the CEO, do a two-week bootcamp where they learned how to pour drinks and run stores** (Fortune) all show leaders using symbolic, structural action to shift mindset at scale.

## Top Story

**Inside Dan Schulman’s war on the old Verizon** (Fortune). This is the clearest, most actionable org-psych case this week: a leader diagnosing a culture of safe goal-setting and learned helplessness, then using layoffs, reversed price hikes, and public confrontation to force a credibility reset. It's a direct playbook for any CHRO or COO trying to break organizational complacency.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate

**Databricks' new AI agent tells CEO Ali Ghodsi the unvarnished truth** (TechCrunch). It's a sharp, relatable hook—a CEO needing an AI tool just to hear the truth from his own 12,000-person company—that invites immediate reflection on psychological safety and information filtering in any audience's org.

Suggested hook: "Your CEO needed an AI agent to tell him what his own employees really think. What does that say about your organization?"

## Best Newsletter Candidate

**Why AI-Powered Tiny Teams Need a New Operating Model** (HBR). This has the deepest structural angle of the batch: it's not about AI replacing people, it's about how shrinking team size relocates coordination costs to inter-team boundaries—shared mental models, decision rights, conflict resolution. That's a meaty, counterintuitive argument (productivity gains look great on paper but stall without new coordination infrastructure) that rewards 500+ words of unpacking, especially since most leaders are currently celebrating AI-driven headcount efficiency without seeing the coordination debt building up behind it.
