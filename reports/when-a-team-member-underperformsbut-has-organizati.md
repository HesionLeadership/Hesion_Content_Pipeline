## Summary
HBR article exploring strategies for managing underperforming employees who possess significant organizational capital—political connections, institutional knowledge, or relationships that make them effectively 'untouchable'—and how leaders can still deliver results despite this dynamic.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This directly addresses the tension between performance management and organizational power dynamics. Employees with high social capital but low performance create a unique challenge where formal authority clashes with informal influence networks, forcing leaders to navigate political landscapes rather than rely solely on merit-based management.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should build systems that distinguish between an employee's social capital and their actual performance contribution, creating transparency around accountability. Leaders must learn to address underperformance diplomatically—leveraging the employee's strengths, redirecting their role, or building coalitions—rather than ignoring the problem or forcing a confrontation that could destabilize team dynamics.
