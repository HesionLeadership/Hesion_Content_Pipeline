## Summary
Insurance executives argue that AI adoption in claims, underwriting, and fraud detection is being undermined by poor data quality and governance, using Shelter Insurance's approach of consolidating customer data before AI deployment as a case study. They outline a five-question test for determining whether an AI agent should be granted decision-making authority, and argue AI governance should be led by executives rather than delegated to IT or compliance.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The piece touches on trust calibration in human-AI collaboration—deciding when to defer to algorithmic judgment versus human judgment—and on how organizations structure authority and accountability when introducing autonomous decision-makers into existing workflows.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should treat AI governance as a core leadership function, not a delegated compliance task, and should design workflows that use AI to surface exceptions while preserving human decision rights in ambiguous or high-stakes cases, building employee trust in AI tools over time.
