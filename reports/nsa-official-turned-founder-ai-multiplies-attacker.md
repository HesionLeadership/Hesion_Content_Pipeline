# NSA official turned founder: AI multiplies attackers’ expertise while defenders are still short on talent

**Published:** Tue, 27 Oct 2026 11:30:00 +0000  
**URL:** [https://fortune.com/2026/10/27/ai-hacking-agents-cybersecurity-talent-gap-synack-jay-kaplan/](https://fortune.com/2026/10/27/ai-hacking-agents-cybersecurity-talent-gap-synack-jay-kaplan/)  
**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
A former NSA operator argues that recent AI-assisted hacking incidents (a South Korean financial data breach and a Chinese state-sponsored espionage campaign using Claude Code) show AI multiplies attacker capability but still requires human judgment to select targets and interpret results; the author uses this to argue executives should measure AI's value by how it extends skilled defenders' reach rather than by headcount reduction.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The piece touches on human-AI augmentation theory: the scarce resource in an AI-saturated environment isn't technical execution but human judgment, creativity, and goal-setting—AI amplifies existing expertise rather than replacing the need for it.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should resist evaluating AI ROI purely through labor-cost reduction; instead, they should redesign roles so AI offloads routine work from their most skilled people, multiplying the reach of scarce judgment/creativity rather than eliminating headcount.
