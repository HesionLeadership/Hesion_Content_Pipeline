## Summary
AI governance frameworks in high-risk industries can make 'humans in the loop' merely reactive to AI alerts rather than proactive threat-spotters, subtly eroding the vigilant judgment that high-reliability organizations depend on. The piece argues that AI-enabled threat-detection systems should instead be designed to preserve and distribute decision authority to front-line humans, as in high-reliability organizing (HRO) practices.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story illustrates how accountability structures and automation can unintentionally shift human cognition from active sense-making to passive alarm-response, undermining the distributed vigilance and psychological ownership that high-reliability organizing research shows is critical to catastrophic-risk prevention.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs implementing AI-driven monitoring systems should design governance structures that preserve front-line employees' proactive judgment and escalation authority, rather than reducing them to passive responders to machine alerts—reinforcing psychological safety and decision ownership even as automation scales.
