## Summary
Flock CEO Garrett Langley defended his company's surveillance technology against mounting public backlash, cancelled contracts, and layoffs, arguing that privacy trade-offs are necessary for societal progress and refusing to take a definitive stance on preventing abuse of the technology.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
Langley exhibits classic diffusion-of-responsibility behavior—framing ethical oversight as the customer's job ('we don't want to tell city leaders how to do their job') while simultaneously claiming moral high ground for the technology's societal benefits, a pattern associated with moral disengagement in leadership.

## Leadership Lesson
When facing stakeholder backlash, CHROs and COOs should recognize that deflecting accountability through narrative framing (comparisons to 'inevitable progress') rather than offering concrete governance or safeguards erodes trust faster than admitting imperfection; leaders must own ethical responsibility for product impact rather than outsourcing it to clients or 'the market.'
