# How E.l.f. Beauty is building AI into its community management

**Source:** Fast Company  
**Published:** 2026-09-02T11:00:00  
**URL:** [https://www.fastcompany.com/91596461/ekta-chopra-elf-beauty-pacesetters-2026](https://www.fastcompany.com/91596461/ekta-chopra-elf-beauty-pacesetters-2026)

## Summary
E.l.f. Beauty's CTO Ekta Chopra has led the company's AI integration by creating tools like E.l.f.luencer (AI-generated social media responses reviewed by humans), internal chatbots, and a secure AI platform that enables nontechnical employees to build their own AI agents. A cross-functional AI steering committee oversees implementation across legal, marketing, and R&D, with 85 agentic AI use cases in pilot.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The strategy of democratizing AI tool-building to frontline employees reflects empowerment and job crafting principles—those closest to the work redesign their own processes, increasing autonomy and engagement while distributing innovation responsibility beyond a central tech team.

## Leadership Lesson
When deploying AI at scale, create governance structures (like cross-functional steering committees) to manage risk, but simultaneously empower non-technical employees with accessible platforms to build their own solutions. This dual approach—centralized oversight with decentralized innovation—maximizes adoption while maintaining quality control through human-in-the-loop review.

## Original Story Summary
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">E.l.f. Beauty’s posts on <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/tiktok" title="TikTok">TikTok</a> and Instagram receive hundreds if not thousands of comments when they go online. Often, the comments include questions about when a product is launching, how the product compares to other E.l.f. offerings, or what type of skin or hair it’s appropriate for. To answer shoppers’ questions in real time, the company created an <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/artificial-intelligence" title="AI">AI</a> tool called E.l.f.luencer—trained on the company&#8217;s stylebook and years of human-written social comments—that autogenerates responses to comments left by consumers on social media platforms.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The comments are approved by human community managers before being posted. “The tool saved a lot of time [for community managers],&#8221; says Ekta Chopra, the company&#8217;s chief technology and AI officer. &#8220;Our response rate was like 30%-40% before launching E.l.f.luencer. Fast forward, it&#8217;s gone above 80%; 90% of the comments are generated by AI in our brand tone, using the language and emojis E.l.f is known for. ” Chopra adds that artificial intelligence at the company is used to augment <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/productivity" title="Productivity">productivity</a>, but all outputs are closely reviewed by human employees.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This is just one initiative Chopra, a 10-year E.l.f. veteran, has overseen. In her role, Chopra helps the company identify and implement its AI ambitions. To oversee AI implementation across the company, Chopra created a cross-functional AI steering committee spanning legal, <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/marketing" title="Marketing">marketing</a>, and R&amp;D with the goal of moving most agentic AI pilots into full production within six months and minimizing risks associated with the technology. Under her leadership, the company is piloting 85 agentic AI use cases. Her deep investment in using AI across departments has helped E.l.f create internal tools and consumer-facing tools like E.l.f.luencer.  </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Internally, Chopra has created AI tools to streamline processes and distribute information at every level of the company. There&#8217;s an internal chatbot called “B.F.e.l.f” that is used by more than 80% of employees. There&#8217;s also an IT help desk assistant called “e.l.f.line” that the company says can accomplish support work equivalent to that of one human help desk employee. Additional internal applications include tools to help products get surfaced by large language models when people search anything on the internet, as SEO continues to deliver fewer returns.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">To accomplish all of this, Chopra created a secure internal AI platform available to every employee that provides approved connectors and APIs, allowing nontechnical staff to build their own agents or apps and deploy them without relying on a central tech team. “We want to democratize that access for elves (E.l.f. employees) because they&#8217;re closest to the work and they can really reimagine that work,” Chopra says. Among the tools employees have built is a dashboard that tracks social sentiment across campaigns and products.&nbsp;</p>
