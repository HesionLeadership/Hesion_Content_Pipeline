# One Month Without AI

## Summary
A software developer recounts how increasing reliance on AI coding agents at work led to loss of understanding of their own codebase, complacency, exhaustion from context-switching across parallel AI agents, and a decline in code quality and personal skill—prompting a deliberate one-month break from AI tools to regain control, competence, and job satisfaction.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The piece illustrates automation complacency and cognitive offloading: as reliance on an intelligent tool increases, professionals can lose situational awareness, critical judgment, and skill mastery, while perceived productivity gains mask actual increases in cognitive load and quality risk.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should treat AI-tool adoption as a skills-risk issue, not just a productivity lever—instituting practices (code review standards, mandatory understanding checks, skill-maintenance time) that prevent automation-induced deskilling and ensure employees retain the judgment needed to catch errors and take ownership of outcomes.
