# Mark Zuckerberg’s Meta bet that AI would shrink its management ranks. Now it’s quietly rebuilding them

**Source:** Fortune  
**Published:** Sat, 12 Sep 2026 09:16:00 +0000  
**URL:** [https://fortune.com/2026/09/12/meta-year-of-efficiency-managers-ai-investment/](https://fortune.com/2026/09/12/meta-year-of-efficiency-managers-ai-investment/)

## Summary
Meta, after a year of pushing managers to return to individual contributor (coding) roles as part of its AI-driven flattening strategy, is now quietly rebuilding its management ranks by asking some engineers in a key AI division if they'd like to lead teams again. The reversal suggests the company underestimated the organizational need for people management.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This illustrates the tension between organizational delayering for efficiency and the fundamental human need for managerial support, coordination, and career development. Removing too many middle managers can create coordination failures, burnout, and loss of institutional knowledge that ultimately undermine the productivity gains the flattening was designed to achieve.

## Leadership Lesson
Before flattening organizational hierarchies, CHROs and COOs should carefully assess the coordination, mentoring, and decision-making functions that middle managers serve—not just their headcount cost. Radical delayering may produce short-term savings but long-term dysfunction; a phased, evidence-based approach with feedback loops is more sustainable than sweeping structural mandates.
