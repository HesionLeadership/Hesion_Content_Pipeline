## Summary
The article argues that hierarchy's core function isn't just routing information but verifying it—deciding what's trustworthy and worth attention. It suggests that if companies capture and use the countless expert corrections and overrides (like a controller catching a flawed revenue figure or an engineer blocking a risky release) to train AI models, they could eventually automate much of this verification work, potentially reducing the need for traditional management hierarchy.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The piece touches on how organizational hierarchy serves a sensemaking and trust-verification function, not merely a coordination one—raising questions about expertise, authority, and where human judgment remains irreplaceable as AI takes over pattern-based verification tasks.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should audit which verification decisions in their organization rely on tacit expert judgment versus rule-based pattern matching, since the latter is increasingly automatable—but this requires rethinking how authority, accountability, and trust are structured, not just how work gets routed.
