# Morning Briefing — October 08, 2026

## This Week's Themes

AI adoption is forcing a leadership identity crisis: stories like **How AI Is Changing Strategy: Rethinking What Your Business Is Designed to Do** (HBR), **CEOs are delegating AI before understanding it** (Fast Company), and **Why the AI age calls for 'founder mode'** (Fortune) all point to executives being asked to redesign structure and decision-making without fully grasping the tool reshaping it. A second theme is the tension between high standards and humane treatment—**Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang says if you want to be successful, prepare to suffer: 'If it's a walk in the park, anybody could do it'** (Fortune), **How to be tough, not a jerk — and why most people get toughness wrong** (Fortune), and **Corgi Says Its 7-Day Workweek Is Voluntary. Experts Say That Answers the Wrong Question** (Inc) all wrestle with where resilience-building ends and burnout culture begins. A third theme is institutional trust under strain, seen in **Amazon responds to data center backlash, says it no longer uses NDAs** (TechCrunch), **Pentagon stops using Anthropic AI tools months after blacklisting company, BBC told** (BBC Business), and **OpenAI's safety firings raise awkward questions** (Fortune), where policy and practice diverge and erode credibility. Finally, shared/co-leadership structures are having a moment, with **David Ellison and Ynon Kreiz announce Skydance leadership team** (CNBC Business) and **David Ellison just brought in a co-CEO to run his new empire: Meet Ynon Kreiz** (CNBC Business) raising classic role-clarity questions.

## Top Story

**How AI Is Changing Strategy: Rethinking What Your Business Is Designed to Do** (HBR) is the most consequential piece today because it moves past "AI as productivity tool" and forces a structural question CHROs and COOs can't dodge: if KPIs and incentives aren't redesigned, AI gains just get absorbed into business as usual. Mollick's "leadership, lab, crowd" framework gives Pete's audience a concrete model for piloting change rather than just discussing it.

Pete could unpack the "centaur/cyborg to conjurer" evolution as a framework for explaining why some AI rollouts feel transformative and others feel like expensive busywork—directly actionable for CHROs rethinking KPIs and COOs rethinking workflow design.
