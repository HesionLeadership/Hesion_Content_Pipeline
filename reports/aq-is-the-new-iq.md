# AQ is the new IQ

**Source:** Fast Company  
**Published:** 2026-09-15T10:52:00  
**URL:** [https://www.fastcompany.com/91550456/aq-is-the-new-iq-adaptability-iq](https://www.fastcompany.com/91550456/aq-is-the-new-iq-adaptability-iq)

## Summary
Alec Litowitz argues that 'Adaptability Quotient' (AQ) is the defining competency for success in uncertain environments—not innate intelligence or boldness, but a learnable discipline of mental simulation, rapid course-correction, ego-free pivoting, and stacking decision-making odds. He frames AQ as essential in an era where AI and algorithmic delegation threaten human cognitive agency.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The concept of AQ maps closely to research on adaptive performance, cognitive flexibility, and learning agility as predictors of leader effectiveness—particularly in volatile, uncertain, complex, and ambiguous (VUCA) environments. The claim that adaptability is learnable rather than innate aligns with growth mindset research and developmental approaches to leadership.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should prioritize assessing and developing adaptability and learning agility over traditional competency models when selecting and developing leaders. Invest in structured decision-making frameworks, psychological safety for rapid experimentation, and deliberate practice in navigating ambiguity rather than relying solely on domain expertise or past performance as predictors of future success.

## Original Story Summary
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">What does it take to operate, let alone succeed, in an environment where the pace is relentless, the stakes are massive, and every decision carries a risk of failure, at least on some scale? How do you orient yourself when you know you can’t rely on the past to inform your sense of what might happen next? How can you hold deep-seated biases at bay, keep your emotions in check, and step outside of your own perspective—“telescope up,” as I like to say—when it’s time to pull the trigger and execute on your decision? And what if all of the above presupposes that you’re not just comfortable, but confident, navigating turbulence and uncertainty as essentially a default state of being?&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Succeeding under these and similar conditions demands not a certain type of person, but a certain way of thinking.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I’ve spent the past thirty-plus years building and leading investment organizations—first as a trading executive, later as a founder and investor—working in environments where decisions are made quickly, under uncertainty, and with real consequences for getting them wrong. Over the course of three decades spent in the daily practice of fast-cycle decision-making—as well as the close study of how others, in and far beyond my industry, make decisions—I started to notice something: The people who consistently outperformed weren’t always the smartest, or the boldest, or even the most experienced. They were the most adaptive: the ones who knew how to mentally “simulate” a potential course of action before they acted, learned fast enough to course-correct mid-flight, and had the humility to pivot and adjust without ego. In fact, I’ve come to believe that the most successful people, especially serial entrepreneurs and those who rack up wins across industries, use a common set of tools and processes, either knowingly or unknowingly. People who employ these techniques may appear to be lucky, but—in reality—they’re not placing random bets; they’re making their own luck. They’re stacking the odds in their favor like a casino. They’re leveraging superior decision-making to increase their chance of success and minimize the risk of total (fatal) catastrophe.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This is high Adaptability Quotient, or AQ. There’s nothing innate about it—just as serially successful people aren’t lucky, by and large (barring the rare exception), they’re not geniuses either. They come equipped with the same mental “hardware” as the rest of us; all they’re doing is engaging that hardware differently and running it more actively, not unlike a learnable operating system for making better decisions under uncertainty. They have a kind of secret, shared playbook—and a hidden code, running quietly in the background of the most consistently successful among us.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This also means that the discipline of cultivating high AQ—the mental architecture behind consistent success, even in chaos—isn’t reserved for the elite. It’s a learnable discipline.&nbsp;</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-intelligence-upgraded">Intelligence, Upgraded</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">More and more, as a species, we’re deferring to algorithms. Outsourcing our judgment to <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/artificial-intelligence" title="AI">artificial intelligence</a> (AI). Submitting (and subsuming) our tastes and preferences and wants and desires to recommendation engines. What we call “progress” often amounts to little more than elegant, comfortable surrender.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The machine uprising won’t come in the form of some real-world analog of Skynet hunting us down with Terminator-like killer robots. It’s already here—in the form of complacency, delegation, default settings. The real threat isn’t that the machines wake up; it’s that we allow ourselves to drift tranquilly off to sleep.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Every week, it seems, the half-life of expertise gets shorter. Industries rise and fall in the span of product cycles. A generation once shaped by change now finds itself outpaced by it. The curve is steepening—not just rapidly but exponentially—and the difference between those who adapt and those who don’t is no longer measured in years, but in updates. We’ve entered an era where the only sustainable advantage is the ability to learn faster than the world around us is changing.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">What we need, and what this pivotal moment demands, is an adaptive uprising. Not to overthrow the machines—but to reclaim what makes us human in the first place.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This isn’t about resisting change. Quite the opposite: It’s about becoming fluent in it—mastering uncertainty and turning chaos into advantage.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Among other things, this means refusing to treat the future as something we passively inherit. At least for now, the future is still something we can build.&nbsp;</p>
