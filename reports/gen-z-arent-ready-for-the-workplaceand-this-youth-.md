# Gen Z aren’t ready for the workplace—and this youth job program CEO is giving them a crash course in emails and workplace etiquette to change that

**Source:** Fortune  
**Published:** Sun, 27 Sep 2026 08:00:00 +0000

**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
Genesys Works, a national nonprofit, runs an eight-week summer program teaching underserved high schoolers workplace etiquette, communication, Excel, and AI skills before placing them in paid corporate internships. CEO Byron Garrett argues employers claim they can't find talent when really they want candidates who are 'ready to go to work,' and cites strong alumni outcomes including higher average salaries than typical college graduates.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story highlights the gap between formal education and organizational socialization—new entrants often lack tacit knowledge of workplace norms (email etiquette, punctuality, reporting structures) that isn't taught in school but is assumed by employers, a classic anticipatory socialization problem.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should recognize that entry-level 'skills gaps' are often socialization gaps, not competence gaps—investing in structured onboarding, mentorship, and explicit norm-setting for early-career hires (especially those without professional networks) can widen talent pipelines and improve retention rather than relying on assumed cultural fluency.
