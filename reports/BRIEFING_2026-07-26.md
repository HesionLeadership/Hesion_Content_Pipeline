# Morning Briefing — July 26, 2026

## Top Story
**Gallup CEO says fixing the 'broken' workplace may be harder than colonizing Mars** (Fortune). Four in five workers globally are disengaged — right back to 2020 levels — which means the collective billions spent on engagement initiatives over the past six years have produced essentially nothing. This is the single most damning data point for any CHRO or COO who believes their current playbook is working: it isn't, and Gallup's numbers suggest the problem is structural (manager quality, job design, autonomy) rather than programmatic (surveys, perks, pizza). If Pete is positioning Hesion as the firm that helps leaders move beyond performative engagement toward evidence-based organizational design, this is the headline to rally around.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate
**AI surveillance is destroying the workplace** (Fast Company). This story hits the sweet spot of timely (AI), counterintuitive (monitoring makes performance *worse*), and actionable — exactly what gets engagement on LinkedIn. It also lets Pete stake a clear position that distinguishes Hesion from compliance-oriented consulting.

**Suggested hook:** "Your employee monitoring software isn't measuring productivity. It's measuring how good people are at pretending to be productive — and the research says it's making everything worse."

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**For women, being creative at work comes with a hidden cost** (Fast Company). This has the deepest org psych layers for a long-form piece: role congruity theory, psychological safety as an unequally distributed resource, the gender-creativity double bind, and the specific irony that organizations demanding more creativity for the AI era are simultaneously penalizing the women who display it. Pete could weave in practical structural interventions (anonymous idea pipelines, structured evaluation criteria for creative contributions, manager training on attribution bias) and connect it to the DBS Bank story as a contrast case — what happens when you formalize innovation as a KPI without auditing who actually gets credit. This is a 700-word newsletter that writes itself and positions Hesion at the intersection of DEI, innovation strategy, and leadership development.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**Stripe's culture of urgency without aggression** (Fast Company). This is a perfect case study for any keynote on the false dichotomy between psychological safety and high performance. The Collisons model that you can pair demanding standards with cooperative feedback norms — writing memos instead of playing politics, leaders staying close to operational reality instead of relying on filtered reports, and "altitude shifting" as a hiring criterion. It's concrete, admirable, and directly challenges the audience: "Are you building a culture where rigor and safety coexist, or have you accidentally chosen one at the expense of the other?" Works especially well for COO audiences who worry that psychological safety means lowering the bar.
