# Our AI agent experience: we scrapped the multiple $60,000 salary marketing jobs and hired one person at a far higher salary

**Source:** Fortune  
**Published:** Sat, 31 Oct 2026 11:30:00 +0000  
**URL:** [https://fortune.com/2026/10/31/dont-ration-company-ai-token-budgets-caps-accenture/](https://fortune.com/2026/10/31/dont-ration-company-ai-token-budgets-caps-accenture/)  
**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
The article argues companies are wrong to cap AI token usage per employee (as Accenture reportedly did), and instead should measure finished-work costs; it cites examples including building a website for a few hundred dollars instead of a $60,000 agency quote, an AI catching errors and suggesting a 40% lower acquisition offer during a board deck review, and replacing several $60,000 entry-level marketing hires with one higher-paid generalist who can now also build analytics software.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The piece illustrates how organizations react to cost uncertainty from new technology by imposing blanket restrictions that penalize their most capable/productive users, rather than redesigning roles around new capability thresholds—a classic case of risk-averse policy-making lagging behind actual workforce capability shifts.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should avoid reactive, uniform caps on new technology adoption; instead they should identify and empower high-leverage users, redesign job architecture around demonstrated capability shifts, and measure cost-per-outcome rather than cost-per-input when evaluating new tools.
