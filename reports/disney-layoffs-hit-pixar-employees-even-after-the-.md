# Disney layoffs hit Pixar employees, even after the success of ‘Toy Story 5’

**Source:** Fast Company  
**Published:** 2026-07-23T20:00:00  
**URL:** [https://www.fastcompany.com/91578810/disney-layoffs-hit-pixar-employees-even-after-the-success-of-toy-story-5](https://www.fastcompany.com/91578810/disney-layoffs-hit-pixar-employees-even-after-the-success-of-toy-story-5)

## Summary
Disney laid off several hundred employees across divisions, with Pixar hit hardest (108 positions), despite Toy Story 5 nearing $1 billion in box office sales. The cuts follow a pattern of layoffs occurring alongside or immediately after major successes, raising questions about how organizations reward contribution during restructuring.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
Pixar's repeated pattern of laying off employees who contributed to blockbuster successes—first with Inside Out 2 in 2024, now during Toy Story 5's run—creates a profound breach of the psychological contract. Workers who 'crunched' to deliver billion-dollar hits and then were terminated without bonuses experience a violation of perceived reciprocity, which can erode trust, engagement, and discretionary effort across the remaining workforce.

## Leadership Lesson
When restructuring during or immediately after a major organizational success, leaders must address the psychological contract explicitly—ensuring that contributors to high-performing products receive fair recognition and transition support. Failing to do so signals to surviving employees that extraordinary effort offers no protection or reward, undermining future discretionary effort and retention of top talent. CHROs should advocate for layoff timing and severance structures that don't visibly punish the people who just delivered wins.

## Strength Score
**8/10** — This story has strong org psych relevance across multiple dimensions: psychological contract violation (laying off contributors to billion-dollar successes without bonuses), survivor syndrome (impact on remaining employees witnessing this pattern), procedural justice concerns (no bumping rights, no union representation), and the tension between strategic workforce restructuring and employee trust. The repeated pattern across 2024 and 2026 makes the organizational dynamics particularly noteworthy.

## Original Story Summary
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Several hundred employees were laid off on Tuesday across multiple <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/disney" id="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/disney" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank" type="link">Disney</a> divisions, including ESPN, National Geographic, Disney Entertainment Television, and Disney Studios. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Surprisingly, Pixar staffers were most heavily impacted—a <a href="https://data.usatoday.com/see-which-companies-announced-mass-layoffs-closings/pixar-ca-layoff-permanent/ca-2627-00039/?query=pixar&amp;page=1" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">July 21 WARN notice </a>outlined that Pixar will cut 108 positions from its Emeryville, California, headquarters. (WARN stands for &#8220;Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification&#8221; Act of 1988.) Editors, technical directors, and software engineers are among those impacted, according to the <a href="https://www.mercurynews.com/2026/07/22/pixar-jobs-emeryville-layoff-disney-toy-story-economy-hollywood-movie/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">notice</a>, reported by <em>The Mercury News</em>.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The majority of layoffs will take place in September, while additional positions will be cut this November, December, and January. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“After careful evaluation of many factors, Pixar has made the decision to permanently reduce the size of the workforce at Pixar’s facility located at 1200 Park Ave.,” Reema Batnagar, Pixar’s vice president of people, wrote in the WARN notice.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“Affected employees do not have bumping rights,” or the ability for senior employees facing layoffs to take on the roles of junior workers, Pixar also stated in the WARN letter.&nbsp;“None of them is represented by a union.”</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Disney<em> </em>did not respond to <em>Fast Company</em>’s request for comment.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Pixar’s recently released <em>Toy Story 5</em> is approaching $1 billion in box office sales—but if these layoffs are any indication, that doesn’t seem to be enough to make up for the performance of some of the studio’s previous releases. <br /><br />A source familiar with the matter told <em><a href="https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/business/disney-pixar-layoffs-2026/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">The Wrap</a> </em>that this round of layoffs occurred in part because of the underperformance of <em>Hoppers</em>, which has earned $389.5 million on a budget of $150 million. A film tends to need to accrue two to 2.5 times its production budget at the global box office to break even, to account for <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/marketing" title="Marketing">marketing</a> costs and extra fees. The studio’s previous animated release, <em>Elio</em>, made $154 million at the worldwide box office, compared with its estimated cost of between $150 and $200 million.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The layoffs mark Pixar’s largest round of cuts since 2024, when 14% of its workforce—around 175 employees—were laid off just as production for <em>Inside Out 2</em> wrapped up. Because the cuts happened just before the film became a major box office hit—eventually grossing $1.69 billion worldwide—the laid-off employees <a href="https://www.ign.com/articles/inside-out-2-was-the-hit-pixar-needed-but-the-laid-off-employees-who-crunched-on-it-are-still-hurting" id="https://www.ign.com/articles/inside-out-2-was-the-hit-pixar-needed-but-the-laid-off-employees-who-crunched-on-it-are-still-hurting" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank" type="link">missed out on any bonuses</a> from its massive success.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Under the leadership of <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91485948/disney-ceo-josh-damaro-will-have-harder-job-being-boss-face-of-it" id="https://www.fastcompany.com/91485948/disney-ceo-josh-damaro-will-have-harder-job-being-boss-face-of-it" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank" type="link">CEO Josh D&#8217;Amaro</a>, who succeeded Bob Iger this March, Disney has already faced company-wide reductions. Earlier this year, it slashed 1,000 jobs, including the Marvel Studios art department, all of the home entertainment team, and several digital marketing roles. <br /><br />While these most recent cuts are smaller in scale, Disney is clearly rethinking its structure amid a rapidly evolving industry driven by new technologies. Back in April, D’Amaro addressed that outlook in a memo to employees.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“Over the past several months, we have looked at ways in which we can streamline our operations in various parts of the company to ensure we deliver the world-class creativity and innovation our fans value and expect from Disney,” he wrote. “Given the fast-moving pace of our industries, this requires us to constantly assess how to foster a more agile and technologically-enabled workforce to meet tomorrow’s needs.”</p>
