## Summary
The article argues that accountability in most organizations is performative—leaders espouse it but consistently deflect blame downward, causing top performers to burn out from carrying consequences that should rest with senior leadership. It outlines how broken accountability cultures form and how leaders can take ownership to fix them.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This directly addresses the organizational justice and attribution dynamics where leaders externalize blame while internalizing credit, eroding trust and psychological safety. Research on perceived organizational support shows that when employees see accountability as asymmetric, engagement and retention suffer disproportionately among high performers.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should audit where accountability actually lands in practice—not just in policy—by examining whether consequences for failures are distributed equitably across hierarchical levels. Leaders who model owning mistakes publicly create cultures where accountability becomes genuine rather than a euphemism for blame-shifting.
