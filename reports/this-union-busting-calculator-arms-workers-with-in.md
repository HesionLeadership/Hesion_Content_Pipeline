# This ‘union-busting’ calculator arms workers with information

**Source:** Fast Company  
**Published:** 2026-09-08T21:30:00  
**URL:** [https://www.fastcompany.com/91603516/this-union-busting-calculator-arms-workers-with-information](https://www.fastcompany.com/91603516/this-union-busting-calculator-arms-workers-with-information)

## Summary
The nonprofit LaborLab launched a 'union-busting calculator' and search tool that give workers visibility into how much employers spend on union avoidance consultants and law firms—part of a $1.7 billion industry. Organizers at workplaces like Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center and the cocktail bar Attaboy report the tools helped build internal cohesion and challenge management claims about resource constraints.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This story highlights a fundamental information asymmetry in employer-employee power dynamics during unionization efforts. When workers gain transparency into organizational resource allocation, it shifts perceived organizational justice and can strengthen collective identity and mobilization—classic drivers of union formation and solidarity.

## Leadership Lesson
Leaders should recognize that spending heavily on union avoidance while claiming insufficient funds for employee compensation creates a credibility gap that erodes trust. CHROs and COOs should proactively invest in employee voice mechanisms, fair compensation, and transparent communication about organizational priorities—addressing root causes of organizing rather than reactive suppression, which increasingly becomes visible and counterproductive.

## Original Story Summary
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">When employees dare to organize their workplace, a <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/u-s-employers-spend-more-than-1-5-billion-annually-on-union-avoidance/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">$1.7 billion industry</a> of consultants and law firms springs into action. According to <a href="https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/general/labortaskforce/docs/508_union-fs-4.pdf" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">the Labor Department</a>, something like 75% of employers enlist union avoidance consultants to quash union efforts. Some of them, including major companies like Amazon and 3M, spend <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/u-s-employers-spend-more-than-1-5-billion-annually-on-union-avoidance/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">many millions of dollars</a> on those consultants, who can also double as legal representation—while the law firms that have made a business out of fighting unions reportedly earn millions of dollars for their work with employers.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But very often, the workers who take on major corporations have little insight into what they’re up against.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">As of 2025, about 16.5 million workers are represented by a union—the highest share in about 16 years, but a much smaller figure than the 50 million Americans who have expressed a desire to join a union. Labor advocates argue that’s partly because companies put so much money behind fighting unions.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The nonprofit workers’ rights watchdog LaborLab launched tools today that could help give workers greater visibility into how their employer may push back on their organizing efforts, and what they’re willing to spend in the process. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A <a href="https://search.laborlab.us/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">search tool</a> captures data from government filings to give workers intel on 800 consultants and thousands of law firms and attorneys—including who they represent and what they charge, as well as their track record with previous union campaigns. The <a href="https://calculator.laborlab.us/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">union-busting cost calculator</a> estimates a number of costs that companies rack up by fighting those efforts instead of voluntarily recognizing the union, from legal expenses to lost <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/productivity" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">productivity</a>. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">While companies are legally required to disclose what they spend on union avoidance, it’s not easy to search federal databases and track down that information. There are also a number of loopholes that allow employers to underreport the total costs of union avoidance, thereby keeping certain expenses off the books.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Consultants do not, for example, have to report when they are simply offering “advice”—a term that is not clearly defined—or not dealing directly with workers. Even when companies do have to provide this information under the law, they don’t necessarily comply. <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/u-s-employers-spend-more-than-1-5-billion-annually-on-union-avoidance/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">According to LaborLab</a>, over half of employers who hired a union avoidance consultant in 2024, and were required to provide a financial disclosure, failed to do so by June 2025, three months after the filing deadline.  </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A number of organizers have already turned to LaborLab’s tools as they navigate their own union campaigns. Sam Doyle, a nurse practitioner who works at the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center in Seattle, has faced significant resistance over the last year and a half while trying to organize nurse practitioners and physician assistants. But she was still shocked to realize how much money may have gone into fighting the union. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“I’m new to this world of organizing, and I don&#8217;t tend to be a numbers and cents person,” Doyle says. “But the calculator, to me, was horrifying. I couldn&#8217;t believe that our employer would spend nearly half a million dollars on fighting us.”&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Zachary Gelnaw-Rubin, a bartender who helped organize the New York cocktail bar Attaboy, found that getting background information on the law firm hired by his employer—including the nature of the firm’s previous work for other companies—was also an effective way to galvanize his colleagues. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“It was very helpful in our ongoing efforts to build and maintain internal cohesion,” Gelnaw-Rubin says. “For the agitation portion of our organizing, it was great because we were able to show it to other workers and be like, ‘Look at this shit.’” (Attaboy workers voted to unionize earlier this year.) </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">LaborLab’s tools also helped organizers at the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center get more information on a union avoidance consultant that had been brought in to speak with employees. “For those of us who want to organize, we don&#8217;t go in with this technical knowledge,” Doyle says. “The calculator and website really helped us understand, ‘Oh wow, we are a cog in this massive wheel of union-busting.’” </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">If they eventually get to the bargaining stage, Doyle believes the calculator will prove useful when management says there isn’t enough money to spend on raises and other changes that would benefit its employees.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“It would make me question the veracity of what they&#8217;re claiming—that ‘we don&#8217;t have the funds to do this,’” she says. “We have just learned that you do have money to do things, and it&#8217;s a matter of where your values are and how you want to treat your employees.”</p>
