# Morning Briefing — August 23, 2026

## This Week's Themes
**Psychological safety is under siege from multiple directions.** Three stories this week — the normalization of anger-based leadership, Meta's CTO publicly shaming an employee's question, and perfection cultures killing innovation — all point to the same problem: leaders are actively destroying the conditions that make organizations functional, and in some cases, they're doing it proudly. **Incentive and system design keeps outpacing culture talk.** The innovation-blocking incentives story, the AI accountability piece, and the AI recruitment bias story all reinforce that structural design choices — what you measure, reward, and automate — matter far more than what you say you value. **Gender equity remains a systems problem, not an individual one.** Two stories highlight how organizational structures systematically disadvantage women: invisible relational labor going unrewarded, and AI tools penalizing career gaps. Both demand structural fixes, not advice for women to advocate harder. **AI governance is becoming an urgent people strategy question**, showing up in hiring bias, accountability design, and the fight over who captures AI productivity gains.

## Top Story
**When Employees Are Held Accountable for AI-Generated Decisions (HBR).** This is the story CHROs and COOs need to sit with. As organizations rush to deploy AI tools, they're dropping AI-generated outputs into existing accountability structures without redesigning who owns what — and employees are responding rationally by masking, reshaping, and performing human judgment over algorithmic outputs. This creates an organizational fiction that undermines both the AI investment and decision transparency. The leadership imperative isn't "adopt AI faster" — it's "redesign accountability before you deploy."

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
