# Inside Dan Schulman’s war on the old Verizon

**Source:** Fortune

**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
Dan Schulman came out of retirement to become Verizon's CEO and is aggressively dismantling the company's bureaucratic culture, cutting 13,000 jobs, reversing planned price increases, and pushing managers to set more ambitious goals after years of losing market share to AT&T and T-Mobile.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story illustrates how organizational inertia and risk-averse goal-setting (managers setting easy targets to guarantee 'success') can calcify into systemic underperformance, and how a new leader can use bold symbolic actions (layoffs, canceling price hikes, public confrontation of complacency) to signal a culture shift and break learned helplessness.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should audit whether internal goal-setting processes reward genuine stretch performance or just safe target-hitting, and recognize that cultural turnarounds often require visible, early high-stakes actions (layoffs, reversing entrenched practices) to establish credibility for change—while being clear-eyed that such demanding cultures will not retain everyone.
