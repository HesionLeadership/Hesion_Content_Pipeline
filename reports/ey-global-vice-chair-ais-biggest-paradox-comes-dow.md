# EY Global Vice Chair: AI’s biggest paradox comes down to what AI can’t do

**Source:** Fortune  
**Published:** Sun, 27 Sep 2026 11:00:00 +0000  
**URL:** [https://fortune.com/2026/09/27/ai-fluency-human-judgment-leadership/](https://fortune.com/2026/09/27/ai-fluency-human-judgment-leadership/)  
**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
An EY Global Vice Chair argues that as AI capabilities grow, the market increasingly rewards distinctly human skills like judgment, context-setting, and knowing when to intervene, since AI cannot define goals or remain accountable for outcomes. The piece cites examples from Lion and Daikin showing efficiency gains when human judgment is kept central to hybrid AI-human workflows.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The article touches on human agency, accountability, and judgment as core psychological constructs that remain valuable even as automation increases, and highlights how workflow design (rigid handoffs vs. integrated collaboration) affects meaningful engagement in work.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should redesign AI-human workflows to preserve integrated human judgment at every stage rather than relegating people to end-of-process approval roles, and should treat 'AI fluency'—including knowing when to override or question AI outputs—as a core leadership competency to train for.

**6/10** — There is a genuine organizational psychology angle around human judgment, accountability, and workflow design affecting engagement and decision quality, but the piece is largely a corporate thought-leadership/promotional article (EY's own case studies) rather than a rigorous psychological analysis, limiting its depth.
