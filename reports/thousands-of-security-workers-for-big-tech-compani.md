# Thousands of security workers for Big Tech companies are on strike in California

**Source:** Fast Company  
**Published:** 2026-09-18T21:00:00  
**URL:** [https://www.fastcompany.com/91609749/thousands-of-security-workers-for-major-tech-companies-are-on-strike](https://www.fastcompany.com/91609749/thousands-of-security-workers-for-major-tech-companies-are-on-strike)

## Summary
14,000 security officers represented by SEIU-USWW have gone on strike across California, protesting stalled contract negotiations with third-party contractors like Securitas and Allied Universal. The workers guard facilities for major tech companies including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, and others, earning a median of ~$20/hour while seeking $30/hour, better healthcare, and retirement benefits.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This story highlights the organizational tensions inherent in fissured employment structures, where the companies that benefit most from workers' labor (Big Tech) are insulated from responsibility by intermediary contractors, creating a diffusion of accountability that undermines worker dignity and perceived organizational justice. Anthropic's preemptive decision to send employees home underscores the paradox of treating workers as indispensable while offering negligible wage increases.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs who rely on contracted workforces should recognize that outsourcing employment doesn't outsource reputational risk or operational dependency. Leaders must ensure that third-party labor arrangements reflect equitable compensation practices, as perceived injustice among contracted workers can disrupt operations, damage employer brand, and signal a values gap between stated mission and actual labor practices.

## Original Story Summary
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Security officers at some of the most prominent tech companies in Silicon Valley have been poised to walk off the job since <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91601758/the-security-workers-who-guard-openai-and-anthropic-could-go-on-strike" rel="noopener" target="_blank">voting to authorize a strike</a> earlier this month. The workers have been in talks with major security contractors like Securitas and Allied Universal for months as they seek to reach a new contract agreement.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Now, as contract negotiations have stalled, 14,000 security officers represented by SEIU-United Service Workers West <a href="https://abc7news.com/post/thousands-bay-area-southern-california-security-guards-major-retailers-tech-giants-begin-strike/19845253/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">are striking</a> across California. The union members took to the streets in San Francisco and Los Angeles at 4:30 a.m. local time on Friday to push for meaningful wage increases and better benefits. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The SEIU-USWW campaign has highlighted the gulf between hourly pay for security workers and the deep coffers of the tech giants who use their services. While the workers are technically employed by third-party contractors, the union has said they provide building security for companies like OpenAI, Anthropic, Salesforce, Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, Nvidia, Cisco, and Netflix; they also serve a variety of other employers, from hospitals to retailers. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">According to the union, some of the security contractors have proposed a mere $0.65 raise in hourly pay over the next two years; Allied Universal, the largest security firm in the world, reportedly offered a wage increase of just $0.25 per hour in 2027, with no raises for the following three years. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">SEIU-USWW president David Huerta previously described this proposal as “shameful, wrong, and deeply, deeply disrespectful.” </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In a statement, an Allied Universal spokesperson said: &#8220;We have made substantial progress in negotiations as part of our commitment to finding common ground. We value the ongoing dialogue, have approached discussions with flexibility and a willingness to work collaboratively toward solutions, and remain focused on reaching a fair and mutually beneficial agreement.&#8221; (Securitas was not immediately available for comment.)</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Security personnel have become even more critical as companies like OpenAI and Anthropic have <a href="https://www.wsj.com/us-news/the-ai-backlash-has-tech-executives-fearing-for-their-lives-30c43972" rel="noopener" target="_blank">faced threats</a> amid mounting public distrust of <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/artificial-intelligence" title="AI">AI</a>. A few weeks ago, <a href="https://www.businessinsider.com/anthropic-san-francisco-staff-work-remote-office-security-strike-2026-8" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><em>Business Insider</em> reported</a> that Anthropic had instructed its employees to work from home, in case their security officers decided to go on strike. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“Clearly, Anthropic understands how indispensable security workers are, even if the companies bargaining with them don’t,” Huerta told Bay Area public media outlet <a href="https://www.kqed.org/news/12097028/bay-area-security-workers-threaten-action-after-anthropic-office-closure" rel="noopener" target="_blank">KQED</a> recently. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In the meantime, median pay for security workers in California remains around $20 per hour, <a href="https://laborcenter.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Demographic-and-Job-Characteristics-of-the-Security-Guard-Workforce-in-California.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">according to an analysis</a> by the UC Berkeley Labor Center. While median pay for security officers is higher in the Bay Area—closer to $24 per hour—that’s still significantly lower than the median wage for all workers in the region, which crosses $42 per hour. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In Los Angeles, median pay for security workers is less than $20 per hour, a figure noticeably lower than the median wage for all workers in the greater L.A. area, which is higher than $27 per hour. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">According to the UC Berkeley analysis, more than 44% of all security officers in California qualify as low-wage workers; in the Bay Area, more than two-thirds of security workers are classified as low-wage. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The SEIU-USWW members who went on strike Friday are aiming for hourly pay of $30, along with more affordable healthcare coverage, expanded retirement benefits, and robust training. Contract workers who provide ancillary services to tech companies have previously walked off the job, often ramping up public pressure on those employers to negotiate more favorable terms with third-party contractors. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In its recent demonstrations, the union has pointed out that as the cost of living continues to rise precipitously in cities like San Francisco, security workers may eventually have no choice but to leave if their pay doesn’t improve—to the detriment of the many companies that have come to rely on their labor.&nbsp;</p>
