# Graduates are managing AI - but are they learning much else?

**Source:** BBC Business  
**Published:** Wed, 04 Nov 2026 00:04:35 GMT  
**URL:** [https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c820vz6wyp65o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c820vz6wyp65o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)  
**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
Companies are creating new AI-focused graduate roles (AI associate, AI automation analyst) where entry-level employees manage AI agents rather than learning traditional trade skills. PwC research found AI-influenced entry-level roles are seven times more likely to require senior-level skills like strategic decision-making, raising questions about whether graduates are skipping foundational professional development.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This touches on skill acquisition sequencing and the traditional 'pyramid' model of professional development, where junior employees build domain expertise before taking on strategic responsibilities—AI may be compressing or bypassing this developmental scaffolding.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should recognize that AI-accelerated responsibility isn't the same as AI-accelerated competence; organizations need to deliberately pair AI-tool fluency with structured mentorship and domain-knowledge transfer, or risk producing employees who can orchestrate AI outputs without understanding what makes those outputs good.
