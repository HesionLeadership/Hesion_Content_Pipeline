# Morning Briefing — July 27, 2026

## Top Story
**Gallup CEO says fixing the workplace may be harder than colonizing Mars** (Fortune). Four in five workers globally are disengaged — back to 2020 lows — and Clifton is essentially saying the entire industry's playbook isn't working. This is a direct challenge to every CHRO's strategy: if engagement is flatlined despite billions in investment, the diagnosis is wrong, not just the dosage. For Hesion's audience, this is the burning platform story — it validates the need for fundamentally different leadership approaches rather than incremental program tweaks.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate
**AI FOMO is a distraction from being a good leader** (Fast Company). This cuts against the dominant narrative every C-suite leader is hearing right now and will stop the scroll. CHROs and COOs are drowning in AI pressure from their boards, and telling them the real succession differentiator is trust — not technical fluency — is both counterintuitive and deeply evidence-based.

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**For women, being creative at work comes with a hidden cost** (Fast Company). This story has layers worth unpacking: the collision between what organizations say they want (creativity, risk-taking, innovation) and the social penalties they actually impose on women who deliver it. You can tie it to psychological safety research, role congruity theory, the current AI-era premium on creative skills, and concrete structural interventions. It also pairs beautifully with the DBS Bank story (innovation KPIs) and the likability labor piece — you could build a thesis that most organizations' innovation strategies are structurally gendered without anyone noticing.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**Stripe's culture of urgency without aggression** (Fast Company). This is a perfect case study for illustrating the paradox Pete teaches: high standards and psychological safety aren't opposites — they're multipliers. The specific details are gold for a keynote — writing culture replacing politics, leaders staying in operational pain rather than relying on filtered reports, 268 alumni founders as proof the model works. Use it to illustrate what "cooperative rigor" looks like in practice versus the more common pattern of either nice-but-mediocre or demanding-but-toxic cultures.

**Concept it illustrates:** You can have both accountability and safety — and the organizations that figure this out become talent factories.
