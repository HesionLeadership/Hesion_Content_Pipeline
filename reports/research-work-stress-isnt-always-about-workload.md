# Research: Work Stress Isn’t Always About Workload

**Source:** HBR  
**Published:** 2026-09-17T12:05:17Z  
**URL:** [https://hbr.org/2026/09/research-work-stress-isnt-always-about-workload](https://hbr.org/2026/09/research-work-stress-isnt-always-about-workload)

## Summary
Research identifies three distinct types of work stress—role ambiguity, role conflict, and role overload—and argues that each requires a different leadership response rather than a one-size-fits-all approach to reducing workload.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This directly maps to classic role stress theory (Kahn et al., 1964), which distinguishes between ambiguity, conflict, and overload as separate stressors with distinct antecedents and outcomes. Effective leadership intervention requires accurate diagnosis of which stressor is dominant rather than defaulting to workload reduction.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should build diagnostic frameworks that distinguish between employees who lack clarity (need better role definition and communication), face competing demands from multiple stakeholders (need prioritization support and boundary setting), and are genuinely overburdened (need workload redistribution). Treating all stress as overload misallocates resources and leaves root causes unaddressed.
