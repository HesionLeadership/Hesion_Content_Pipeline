# The rise of the ‘two-job job’: 61% of workers say they’re now doing more than one role

**Source:** Fortune  
**Published:** Thu, 01 Oct 2026 14:26:13 +0000  
**URL:** [https://fortune.com/2026/10/01/rise-of-the-two-job-61-of-workers-say-theyre-now-doing-more-than-one-role/](https://fortune.com/2026/10/01/rise-of-the-two-job-61-of-workers-say-theyre-now-doing-more-than-one-role/)  
**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
A Korn Ferry survey of 16,000+ professionals finds 61% of workers now perform the duties of more than one role, with workloads up sharply amid corporate restructuring and management cuts (e.g., Uber's 10% workforce and 20% manager reduction). Despite AI adoption, many employees report heavier workloads and falling motivation, while experts like Peter Cappelli argue that cutting managers shifts work onto remaining employees rather than eliminating it.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story illustrates role overload and the erosion of psychological contracts when organizations flatten structures without reducing actual work volume, leading to manager burnout, employee disengagement, and a widening perception gap between leadership (who see AI efficiency gains) and frontline workers (who experience AI as added burden).

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should audit actual work volume before cutting management layers or headcount—eliminating a role does not eliminate the work, and failing to redistribute or genuinely reduce tasks leads to burnout, lost productivity, and demotivation that erase projected savings. Leaders should also validate efficiency claims about AI with frontline data, not just C-suite perception, and build structural support for AI-related 'invisible work' into job design and performance systems rather than treating it as an unpaid stretch assignment.
