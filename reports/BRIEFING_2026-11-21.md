Finally, the gap between stated hiring policy and actual practice surfaces in **Goodwill CEO says hiring managers are going behind their bosses' backs and looking for workers with college degrees, not skills** (Fortune).

## Top Story
**'The bottleneck used to be dumpling-folding talent': Din Tai Fung CEO on America's top-grossing restaurant chain turning craft into corporate training** (Fortune) matters because it's a clean, concrete case study of an organizational learning challenge every CHRO scaling specialized operations will face: how do you convert tacit, apprenticeship-based craft into teachable systems without losing quality? Din Tai Fung's 12-week training program and standardized systems offer a replicable model, not just an inspiring anecdote.

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**Goodwill CEO says hiring managers are going behind their bosses' backs and looking for workers with college degrees, not skills** (Fortune) has the deepest org psych angle and the most room to unpack. It's a textbook espoused-vs-enacted policy gap: companies announce skills-based hiring, but hiring managers quietly revert to degree screens—likely driven by similarity-attraction bias/homophily. A 500+ word piece could walk through why policy announcements fail without behavioral audits, how homophily operates unconsciously in interview panels, what structured, skills-based assessment actually looks like in practice, and why Gen Z is bearing the brunt of this implementation gap. This gives Pete room to offer a concrete framework (audit hiring manager decisions, bias-mitigation training, structured rubrics) that CHROs can act on immediately—exactly the kind of practical, theory-backed content his newsletter audience wants.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**Tech investor Bill Gurley says workers who went through the 'college conveyor belt' and chased safe jobs are at high risk of AI automation** (Fortune) works well as a live discussion prompt. It illustrates the 'calling vs. job' orientation distinction (Wrzesniewski et al.) and lets Pete pose a direct question to the room: are your people doing a job, or pursuing a calling—and which group is more exposed to AI disruption? It's debatable enough to generate real audience engagement rather than passive listening.
