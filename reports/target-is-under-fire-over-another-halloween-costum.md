# Target is under fire over another Halloween costume just weeks after apologizing for the last one

**Source:** Fast Company  
**Published:** 2026-09-04T20:30:00  
**URL:** [https://www.fastcompany.com/91602666/target-is-under-fire-over-another-halloween-costume-just-weeks-after-apologizing-for-the-last-one](https://www.fastcompany.com/91602666/target-is-under-fire-over-another-halloween-costume-just-weeks-after-apologizing-for-the-last-one)

## Summary
Target, Walmart, and Amazon pulled a 'German Army Soldier Adult Costume' resembling Nazi uniforms from their websites, marking Target's second Halloween costume controversy in weeks after previously removing a children's clown costume criticized for resembling blackface imagery.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This represents a repeated failure in organizational quality control and product review processes, suggesting systemic issues in how large retailers vet third-party merchandise for cultural sensitivity rather than isolated incidents.

## Leadership Lesson
After a high-profile product failure, leaders must implement immediate systemic reviews of similar inventory rather than treating each incident as isolated—a promised corrective action that clearly wasn't executed between Target's first and second costume controversy.

## Strength Score
**6/10** — The story reveals a meaningful organizational learning failure: Target publicly committed to examining 'how this happened and what needs to change' after the first incident, yet a nearly identical issue arose weeks later. This speaks directly to organizational learning, quality assurance processes, and the gap between crisis communication promises and actual systemic change. The involvement of multiple retailers also points to broader supply chain oversight issues.

## Original Story Summary
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Just a few weeks after coming under fire for a children&#8217;s Halloween costume that <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91595260/target-halloween-costume-clown-blackface-jim-crow" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">drew comparisons to blackface caricatures</a>, Target&#8217;s Halloween collection is under fire again, although this time around the retailer isn&#8217;t alone in the controversy.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Target, Walmart, and Amazon have pulled a “German Army Soldier Adult Costume” from their websites. Originally sold by Orion Costumes, the outfit features a vintage-looking jacket and pants set that resembles the uniforms of Nazi soldiers during World War II.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">While the costume is no longer listed on any of the three retailers’ websites, <a href="https://www.amazon.com/ORION-COSTUMES-German-Soldier-Costume/dp/B0FYQ7FBGV" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">links to</a> <a href="https://www.target.com/p/orion-costumes-german-army-soldier-adult-costume-x-large/-/A-1006527241" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">archived</a> <a href="https://www.walmart.com/ip/Orion-Costumes-German-Army-Soldier-Adult-Costume-Standard-Male/18032119604" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">pages</a> reveal the product’s name and ties to Orion Costumes.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">According to <a href="https://www.threads.com/@authorkstarling/post/DcsK5maFC3X?xmt=AQG0DvC9oAUM7JBfurAt0nhxHuW2p6IbvFGMgoTS1gosLQ" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">screenshots</a> of the product listings shared on social media, the costume was originally available for purchase for $49.99 at Walmart and Target. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The product description indicates the costume includes a jacket, trousers, boot covers, and a hat. While the description does not mention Nazis, it does reference the 1940s; the Nazi party ruled Germany into the spring of 1945.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“If you’re looking for a vintage style look for your next fancy dress night, why not try a 1940s theme?” a screenshot of the product description says.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Fast Company</em> was not able to independently verify the screenshots.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The product removal comes just weeks after Target had to pull another Halloween costume for insensitive historical allusions. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Back in August, a children’s costume listed on Target’s website as “Kids’ Glows Under Blacklight Circus Clown Halloween Costume Bodysuit” was called out for resembling imagery from Jim Crow-era blackface minstrelsy. On August 23, after screenshots of the costume went viral on social media, Target pulled the costume from its website.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“As a company, we know we got this wrong, and we are deeply sorry. The costume is offensive and should never have been part of our assortment. It is no longer available for sale,” Target <a href="https://corporate.target.com/press/statement/2026/08/target-statement-on-offensive-halloween-costume" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">said in a statement</a> at the time. “We know this is especially hurtful for our Black guests, team members and partners. Removing the costume is an important first step, and the company is looking closely at how this happened and what needs to change to ensure this won’t happen again.”</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Yet less than a month later, the national retailer is once again pulling a costume from its inventory, now joined by two other giants in the industry.</p>
