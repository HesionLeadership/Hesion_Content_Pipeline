## Summary
Wharton professor Ethan Mollick argues that AI's rapid advancement—especially autonomous AI agents capable of multi-hour tasks—requires companies to fundamentally redesign strategy, organizational structure, and management roles rather than just use AI as a productivity tool. He outlines a 'leadership, lab, crowd' model for organizational AI adoption and describes evolving human-AI collaboration from 'centaur/cyborg' work to 'conjurer' work, where humans assign and oversee AI-driven team-like output.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The piece touches on role ambiguity and job redesign (blurring boundaries between coders, designers, managers), the psychology of leader risk tolerance in uncertain environments, and how incentive/KPI structures shape whether technological productivity gains translate into real organizational value.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should proactively redesign KPIs, incentives, and job boundaries to prevent AI gains from being absorbed into 'more of the same' output, invest in cross-functional experimentation labs to pilot new AI-enabled workflows, and model personal risk-tolerant AI use to set cultural norms for broader adoption.
