# Morning Briefing — October 27, 2026

## This Week's Themes
The dominant theme this week is the gap between AI adoption and AI payoff: stories like **NSA official turned founder: AI multiplies attackers' expertise while defenders are still short on talent** (Fortune), **AI delivers 'zero financial payoff' to most companies, Apollo economist says** (TechCrunch), **AI is everywhere except in the GDP numbers — here is what triggers the break** (Fortune), and **Twilio CMO: We shrank 2 weeks of work down to one day with AI. Then the unsexy work began** (Fortune) all point to the same conclusion: technology without organizational redesign and human judgment doesn't convert to performance. A second theme is culture as lived experience versus stated values—**What Interruptions Say About Your Organizational Culture** (MIT Sloan Review), **Staying Principled in a Time of Uncertainty** (HBR), and **How the CEO of Rolls-Royce Turned an Underperforming Company Around** (HBR) all show culture gets tested (and revealed) in moments of friction, pressure, or blind spots, not in culture decks. A third theme is leader affect and psychological safety, carried by **Careful, managers. Your mood affects your whole team's performance** (Fast Company) and **Why leaders shouldn't have to apologize for private remarks** (Fast Company). Finally, there's a structural workforce theme—**American Express is cutting 5% of its workforce to 'prepare for AI'** (CNBC Business) and **'There's nothing temporary about a temporary store': How Spirit Halloween's CEO took it from 130 stores to 1,550** (Fortune) both show organizations deliberately redesigning hierarchy and span of control, not just headcount.

## Top Story
**What Interruptions Say About Your Organizational Culture** (MIT Sloan Review). This is the strongest piece today because it translates an abstract, hard-to-measure concept—inclusion—into something CHROs can actually observe and coach in real time: who gets cut off in meetings and who doesn't. It matters because it reframes "culture work" away from values statements and toward teachable, in-the-room behaviors leaders can change this week.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate
**How the CEO of Rolls-Royce Turned an Underperforming Company Around** (HBR) — the "burning platform" framing and a CEO who started building his team before his official start date is vivid, quotable, and instantly shareable for an exec audience.
Suggested hook: "Rolls-Royce's new CEO didn't wait for his start date to tell employees the truth they already knew."

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**Staying Principled in a Time of Uncertainty** (HBR). This one has the deepest org psych layering for a 500+ word treatment: it's not just a leadership-under-pressure story, it's a case study in sensemaking (defining reality and giving hope during crisis), the gap between espoused and enacted values when diversity commitments face political backlash, and status quo bias as a barrier to innovation inside large, established organizations. Chenault's track record across 9/11 and 2008 gives Pete concrete historical anchors to build a framework around—how leaders pick a small number of simple priorities (liquidity, profitability, selective growth) instead of trying to solve everything, and how they build internal coalitions to defend values with business rationale rather than abandoning them. That's rich enough to unpack across multiple sections: crisis sensemaking, values resilience, and innovation inertia.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**NSA official turned founder: AI multiplies attackers' expertise while defenders are still short on talent** (Fortune). Use this as the live illustration of human-AI augmentation theory: AI doesn't replace judgment, it multiplies whoever already has it. Great prompt for a workshop exercise on redesigning roles so AI offloads routine work from your most skilled people instead of just cutting headcount.
