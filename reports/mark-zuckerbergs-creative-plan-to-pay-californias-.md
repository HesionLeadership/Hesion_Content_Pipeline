# Mark Zuckerberg’s creative plan to pay California’s proposed billionaire tax without losing control of Meta

## Summary
Meta's board approved a new nonvoting Class C stock class that will let CEO Mark Zuckerberg sell shares to cover a potential California billionaire tax (Prop 40) without diluting his ~60% voting control; SEC filings reveal Zuckerberg pressured the board on this issue since July, reportedly threatening to sell his Palo Alto home and trigger a media firestorm if a solution wasn't found.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This illustrates board capture and asymmetric power dynamics, where a founder-CEO's implicit threats (property sale, departure) shape governance decisions, raising questions about board independence versus accommodation of a dominant leader's personal financial interests.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should scrutinize whether governance structures are genuinely independent or primarily reactive to founder/CEO pressure; boards should establish clear protocols for separating personal financial accommodations from organizational strategy to maintain perceived fairness and avoid setting precedents of leader-driven policy capture.
