# Fired OpenAI researchers say they were let go for 'prioritising safety'

**Source:** BBC Business  
**Published:** Fri, 09 Oct 2026 09:30:49 GMT  
**URL:** [https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cvlydn8d3lkjo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cvlydn8d3lkjo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)  
**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
Three former OpenAI researchers (Mikita Balesni, Tomek Korbak, Jasmine Wang) claim they were fired for raising AI safety concerns, while OpenAI maintains they were dismissed for a 'significant breach of trust' involving mishandling sensitive information. The researchers published an open letter alleging the firings have created a chilling effect, making current employees afraid to speak up about safety issues.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This is a textbook case of whistleblower dynamics and psychological safety erosion—when employees perceive that raising concerns leads to punishment (regardless of the official reason given), it creates a chilling effect that suppresses future dissent, even if the company's stated rationale is legitimate.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs must recognize that the *perceived* reason for a termination matters as much as the actual reason, especially in cases touching on ethics or safety; failing to transparently and credibly communicate disciplinary rationale can destroy psychological safety org-wide, chilling the exact escalation behaviors leaders claim to want.
