# Morning Briefing — October 20, 2026

## This Week's Themes
The dominant theme this week is **AI's erosion of human judgment and trust structures** — seen in **What happens when people have to jump in to clean up AI's mess?** (Fast Company), **Why companies may be liable for AI errors** (Fast Company), **Team Dynamics Weaken When Employees Turn to AI instead of Colleagues** (HBR), and **The Defining Challenge of Responsible AI Is Designing Systems That Preserve Human Judgment When and Where It Counts** (MIT Sloan Review), all pointing to skill atrophy and automation bias as organizations lean harder on AI. A second theme is **institutional norms under pressure** — **Even Benchmark thinks AI is eroding the venture pact against backing rivals** (TechCrunch) and **Anthropic is letting METR researchers look under the hood with employee-level access** (TechCrunch) both show trust-based systems giving way to either erosion or deliberate transparency mechanisms. A third theme is **reframing "soft" organizational dynamics as strategic data**, visible in **When You Feel Envy, Turn It Into Something Productive** (HBR), **Recognition is a talent strategy** (Fast Company), and **This is how great managers respond to disengaged employees** (Fast Company). Finally, several stories — **Two decades ago, he asked what's the matter with Kansas? Now he's back to look at the 70-year con of 'creativity' and 'innovation'** (Fortune) and **Move slow and make things: What Japanese craftsmanship can teach business about quality, patience, and expertise when AI makes speed cheap** (Fortune) — question whether organizational rhetoric around speed and innovation actually holds up against deeper, evidence-based practice.

## Top Story
**When You Feel Envy, Turn It Into Something Productive** (HBR). This matters because it hands CHROs and COOs a practical reframe for an emotion leaders typically try to suppress or ignore — treating envy as diagnostic data about unmet career aspirations rather than a morale problem. The SIRN framework gives leaders a concrete tool to convert rivalry into mentorship and retention conversations, which is rare, actionable territory in talent management.

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**When You Feel Envy, Turn It Into Something Productive** (HBR). This story has the deepest org psych foundation of the two available today — it's grounded in social comparison theory and emotion regulation research, and distinguishes malicious from benign envy in a way that directly informs leadership practice. There's enough here to build out 500+ words: the distinction between envy types, the SIRN framework (even with one element still unexplored, which creates a natural hook to dig deeper), and the leadership application of building psychological safety around admiration and rivalry. Pete could connect this to his own work on psychological safety and turn it into a piece on why suppressing envy backfires — it festers into malicious comparison — while surfacing it (safely) turns it into mentorship pipelines and clearer career conversations. This is exactly the kind of "hidden emotion as leadership signal" content that differentiates Hesion's thinking from generic HR advice.
