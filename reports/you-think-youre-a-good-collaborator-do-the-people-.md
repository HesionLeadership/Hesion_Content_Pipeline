# You Think You’re a Good Collaborator. Do the People You Manage Agree?

**Published:** 2026-09-15T12:05:37Z

## Summary
Leaders often overestimate their collaborative abilities while engaging in behaviors like dominating conversations and micromanaging that undermine teamwork. The article explores the gap between leaders' self-perception as collaborators and how their direct reports actually experience their leadership.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This directly addresses the well-documented self-other rating discrepancy in leadership assessment, where leaders consistently rate themselves more favorably than their subordinates do—a core finding in 360-degree feedback research. The specific behaviors identified (conversation dominance, micromanagement) reflect failures in shared leadership and psychological safety.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should implement regular 360-degree feedback mechanisms specifically measuring collaborative behaviors, not just outcomes. Leaders need structured self-awareness interventions to close the gap between their perceived and actual collaborative impact, with particular attention to conversational turn-taking and delegation practices.
