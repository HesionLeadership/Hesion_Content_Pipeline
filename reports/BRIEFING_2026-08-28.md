# Morning Briefing — August 28, 2026

## This Week's Themes
**Psychological safety is under siege from multiple directions.** Three stories this week — anger-based leadership at scale, Meta's CTO publicly shaming an employee question, and perfection cultures killing innovation — all point to the same thing: leaders are actively destroying the conditions that make organizations work, sometimes deliberately and sometimes by neglect. **Incentive misalignment is the structural root beneath the cultural symptoms.** Two stories (incentives blocking innovation, plus the frontline innovation piece from MIT Sloan) make the case that organizations are getting exactly the behavior they're paying for — and it's not the behavior they say they want. **Gender equity has a systems design problem, not just a bias problem.** The AI recruitment tools story and the invisible relational labor story both show how organizational systems — whether algorithmic or evaluative — structurally disadvantage women in ways that individual awareness can't fix. **AI accountability is an emerging leadership design challenge.** The HBR piece on employees masking AI-generated decisions and the BBC piece on biased AI screening tools signal that organizations are deploying AI faster than they're redesigning the governance, accountability, and fairness structures around it.

## Top Story
**"When Employees Are Held Accountable for AI-Generated Decisions" (HBR)** — This is the story CHROs aren't ready for. As organizations push AI tools into operational decision-making, employees are quietly reshaping, overriding, or disguising AI outputs to protect their own credibility — creating an invisible layer of impression management that undermines both the AI investment and organizational transparency. This is a structural design problem, not a training problem, and it sits squarely in the CHRO and COO's lane: who owns the decision when the human and the algorithm disagree, and have you made it safe enough for people to say so out loud?

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**"The hidden cost for women of being 'easy' to work with" (Fast Company)** — This has the deepest org psych substrate. Pete can connect it to the "non-promotable tasks" research (Linda Babcock et al.), construct validity failures in performance appraisal, role theory, and the systematic under-valuation of relational leadership competencies. There's a rich argument to make: organizations say they value collaboration and culture, then promote exclusively on individual output metrics — and the people absorbing the cost of that hypocrisy are disproportionately women. This is a 500+ word piece that can offer CHROs a concrete audit framework: map who's doing the invisible work, formalize it, rotate it, and tie it to advancement.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**"Your Managers Aren't Blocking Innovation. Your Incentives Are" (Inc)** — This is a perfect keynote illustration because it reframes blame. Most executives in the room will nod when Pete says "your middle managers resist change," and then he can flip it: "They're not resisting. They're responding rationally to the system you designed." It cleanly illustrates reinforcement theory, principal-agent dynamics, and the gap between espoused values and enacted reward systems. Great for any talk on culture change, innovation, or performance management redesign. The takeaway writes itself: "Don't blame the players. Fix the game."
