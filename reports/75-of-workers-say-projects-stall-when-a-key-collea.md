# 75% of Workers Say Projects Stall When a Key Colleague Takes PTO. That’s a Leadership Problem

**Source:** Inc  
**Published:** Fri, 04 Sep 2026 08:00:00 -0400  
**URL:** [https://www.inc.com/scott-hutcheson/75-percent-pto-work-stall-leadership-problem/91400929](https://www.inc.com/scott-hutcheson/75-percent-pto-work-stall-leadership-problem/91400929)

## Summary
A survey finds that 75% of workers report projects stalling when a key colleague takes PTO, indicating that many teams lack resilience and rely too heavily on single individuals. The article frames this as a leadership failure in cross-training, documentation, and building redundancy into team structures.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This reflects the well-documented 'key person dependency' problem, rooted in poor knowledge management and inadequate role redundancy. Research on team resilience and transactive memory systems shows that teams perform better when knowledge is distributed rather than siloed in one individual.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should audit teams for single points of failure by investing in cross-training programs, standardized documentation, and shared accountability structures—ensuring no one person's absence can halt progress. Building team resilience is a leadership responsibility, not an individual contributor problem.
