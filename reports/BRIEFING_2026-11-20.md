# Morning Briefing — November 20, 2026

## This Week's Themes

Three themes dominate this week. First, **key-person dependency and succession governance** runs through nearly every Nvidia story — **California AG subpoenas Nvidia over worries about Huang's ties with China** (TechCrunch), **California targets Nvidia in China AI chip probe** (BBC Business), **Nvidia can run without Jensen Huang. Can the AI boom?** (Fortune), and **Jensen Huang Says He Would Return to Nvidia if Asked** (NYT Business RSS) — and extends to **Ex-GM CEO on Mary Barra succession planning: 'It's going to be tough'** (CNBC Business) and **Home Depot names Richard McPhail as new CEO, beats earnings expectations** (CNBC Business). Second, **AI is forcing organizations to redesign decision rights and workflows faster than their governance structures can absorb**, seen in **Behind the recent wave of IT outages: AI is driving an unprecedented number of software patches** (Fortune) and **'You either take the wheel, or we're going to run into the wall': The founder who sold Wrike for $2.25 billion forced his engineers to build around AI** (Fortune). Third, **leadership credibility and authenticity under pressure** shows up in **I helped grow a steel company from $750 million to $12 billion. CEOs should use AI before they grade employees on it** (Fortune) and **Sesame Workshop CEO says you'll never see an AI-powered Elmo toy: 'We could have made a lot of money … but we're just not going to do that'** (Fortune). The Nvidia saga in particular is becoming a live case study in whether boards can hold the line against charismatic-founder pressure, a thread Pete can mine for weeks.

## Top Story

**'You either take the wheel, or we're going to run into the wall': The founder who sold Wrike for $2.25 billion forced his engineers to build around AI** (Fortune). This is the clearest, most concrete example this week of deliberate disequilibrium as a change management tactic — a founder intentionally created unsustainable conditions to force adoption, and even his best people resisted for months. It gives CHROs and COOs a real playbook (and real warning) for AI-driven restructuring: productivity gains alone don't guarantee change; you need imposed urgency, redefined success metrics, and sustained reinforcement.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate

**California AG subpoenas Nvidia over worries about Huang's ties with China** (TechCrunch). It has everything LinkedIn rewards: a boardroom power-abuse scandal, national security stakes, and a governance failure CHROs will recognize instantly from their own founder-dependency conversations.

Suggested hook: "A board that won't touch a founder's conduct until a subpoena forces its hand isn't practicing governance — it's practicing hope."

## Best Newsletter Candidate

**Behind the recent wave of IT outages: AI is driving an unprecedented number of software patches** (Fortune). This is the deepest org psych story of the day because it's not really about AI failing — it's about organizations bolting high-velocity AI diagnostics onto legacy human approval pipelines never built to keep pace. That creates exhausted staff rubber-stamping recommendations they can't fully vet, diffused accountability between "AI recommended it" and "a human approved it," and now congressional scrutiny. A 500+ word piece could walk through this as a sociotechnical mismatch: the technology procurement decision and the organizational change management decision got separated, and separating them is exactly what's causing bank, NSA, and Microsoft outages. Pete can extend this directly to his CHRO/COO audience — ask them point blank whether their own AI rollouts gave reviewers more bandwidth and authority, or just more things to approve faster. It also ties neatly to the Wrike story's theme of forced change without addressing underlying bottlenecks, giving the newsletter a natural "two sides of the same coin" structure: one company intentionally broke its old process to force new behavior, another broke under the weight of new tools without redesigning the old process at all.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example

**Ex-GM CEO on Mary Barra succession planning: 'It's going to be tough'** (CNBC Business). This is a textbook, teachable case of succession planning done as a structured, multi-year process — internal candidates versus external search, done proactively rather than in crisis — and it contrasts well live against the Nvidia stories as a "what good succession planning looks like" versus "what happens without it."
