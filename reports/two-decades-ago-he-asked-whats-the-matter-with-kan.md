# Two decades ago, he asked what’s the matter with Kansas? Now he’s back to look at the 70-year con of ‘creativity’ and ‘innovation’

**Published:** Tue, 20 Oct 2026 12:00:00 +0000

**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
Author Thomas Frank discusses his new book 'The Creativity Con,' which argues that the corporate cult of 'innovation' and 'creativity'—traced back to ad executive Alex Osborn's invention of 'brainstorming' in the 1950s—has functioned as a decades-long ideological racket that devalued blue-collar work and is now threatening the white-collar 'creative class' through AI.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The piece raises questions about how organizational rhetoric and buzzwords (innovation, creativity) can function as ideology rather than evidence-based practice, shaping who is seen as valuable (creative class vs. blue-collar workers) and justifying economic outcomes like outsourcing and automation.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should critically interrogate whether their organization's 'innovation' and 'creativity' initiatives are substantive practices with real evidence behind them, or largely branding/ideology that obscures class-based disparities in how different types of work and workers are valued.
