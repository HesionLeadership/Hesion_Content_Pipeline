# Morning Briefing — November 12, 2026

## This Week's Themes

The dominant theme this week is founder-dependency and key-person risk, driven by the Nvidia crisis: **Jensen Huang is the AI boom's key man. Now, that's a problem** (Fortune), **The 'charismatic' leader trap: Why organizations shouldn't depend on one individual, no matter how appealing** (Fast Company), and **Nvidia is investigating Jensen Huang. But its board may have no interest in challenging its leader** (Fast Company) all point to the same structural failure: boards and companies that let a single charismatic leader become inseparable from governance itself. A closely related thread is succession readiness as risk management, seen in **Why Nvidia turned to 'strategic' CFO Colette Kress to clean up Jensen Huang's mess** (Fortune) and **Who could replace Jensen Huang? A highly unscientific list of possible successors** (TechCrunch), both showing what happens when a 33-year-tenured founder-CEO has no credible bench. A third theme is managers and middle layers absorbing change executives aren't ready to lead, visible in **'Shock absorbers': Korn Ferry says managers are carrying AI change that executives aren't ready to lead** (Fortune) and **Databricks' new AI agent tells CEO Ali Ghodsi the unvarnished truth** (TechCrunch), both pointing to information filtering and role strain up and down the hierarchy. Finally, a quieter but persistent theme is work design catching up to AI, shown in **The Math Driving Agentic AI Adoption in NetOps** (HBR) and **Why AI-Powered Tiny Teams Need a New Operating Model** (HBR) — both argue that autonomy and coordination structures, not just tools, determine whether AI adoption actually works.

## Top Story

**Jensen Huang is out at Nvidia — who is Colette Kress, his interim replacement?** (TechCrunch) is the top story because it's the fullest, highest-scored account of a governance failure every CHRO needs to internalize right now: a 33-year founder-CEO with ~60 direct reports, no succession plan, and an undisclosed relationship with a direct report, forced out overnight. It matters because it shows boards can have the authority to act decisively in a crisis — but only after the damage, stock drop, and reputational exposure have already happened.

## Best Newsletter Candidate

**Why Nvidia turned to 'strategic' CFO Colette Kress to clean up Jensen Huang's mess** (Fortune) has the deepest org psych angle and deserves the long-form treatment. It's not just a succession story — it's a case study in how extreme centralization (Huang reportedly had ~60 direct reports) creates organizational fragility that doesn't show up until a crisis forces the issue. Pete could unpack why boards default to "trusted insider" successors during reputational emergencies, what Kress's deep institutional knowledge actually buys the company versus what it can't replace (Huang's personal relationships with partners), and the broader lesson for CHROs: key-person dependency is an enterprise risk that needs active measurement — span of control, bench depth, deputy structures — long before a scandal forces the board's hand. There's enough material here (centralization, investor trust, internal promotion dynamics, crisis continuity) to easily carry 500+ words without padding.
