# Uber Is Cutting Managers. Who Will Do the Work They Leave Behind?

**Source:** Inc  
**Published:** Fri, 11 Sep 2026 07:00:00 -0400  
**URL:** [https://www.inc.com/scott-hutcheson/uber-is-cutting-managers-who-will-do-the-work-they-leave-behind/91403147](https://www.inc.com/scott-hutcheson/uber-is-cutting-managers-who-will-do-the-work-they-leave-behind/91403147)

## Summary
Uber is reducing its management layers as part of a broader organizational restructuring, but the work previously handled by those managers doesn't disappear—it gets redistributed to remaining employees, raising questions about workload, burnout, and organizational effectiveness.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
Delayering initiatives often assume managerial work is redundant overhead, but research shows that middle managers play critical roles in coordination, employee development, and sense-making. Removing them without redesigning workflows risks role ambiguity, increased strain, and erosion of organizational support for remaining employees.

## Leadership Lesson
Before cutting management layers, CHROs and COOs should conduct a thorough work-redistribution analysis—mapping where managerial tasks (coaching, coordination, escalation handling) will actually land. Flattening without redesigning creates invisible workload that degrades performance and retention.
