# Morning Briefing — October 13, 2026

## This Week's Themes
The dominant theme this week is AI transformation as a workforce and trust problem, not a tooling problem—seen in **Fortune's AIQ Summit: The conversation has moved from experimentation to execution** (Fortune), **With AI productivity, your team is burning out** (Fast Company), **The economist behind the 'China shock' says AI is a paradoxical 'skill-disequalizer' for Gen Z workers** (Fortune), and **CEOs are delegating AI before understanding it** (Fast Company). A second theme is psychological safety under strain, especially around whistleblowing and dissent, shown in **Fired OpenAI safety researchers dispute misconduct claims, warn of chilling effect** (TechCrunch) and **Fired OpenAI researchers say they were let go for 'prioritising safety'** (BBC Business), with echoes in **'Dungeons & Dragons' creative teams join union as concerns mount over pay discrepancies and 'forced AI use'** (Fast Company). A third theme is leader proximity and trust-building as a retention lever, running through **Ancestry's CEO says a billionaire boss taught him to eat lunch with employees in the break room** (Fortune), **Why a good boss matters more than working from home** (Fast Company), and **Nvidia's secret to retaining staff? No org charts, a flat structure, and two extra days off every quarter** (Fortune). Finally, organizational structure as a moving target—not a fixed advantage—connects **Restructuring Mobile Game Giant Supercell for a Turnaround** (HBR) with **Why the AI age calls for 'founder mode'** (Fortune).

## Top Story
**Why Great Teams Don't Need to Like Each Other** (HBR). This reframes a persistent leadership myth—that culture-building means forcing social bonding—with research showing task cohesion (shared purpose) beats social cohesion for performance. It's directly actionable for CHROs rethinking onboarding and team design, and it's the highest-scored story of the day.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate
**Why Great Teams Don't Need to Like Each Other** (HBR) — the counterintuitive headline alone invites debate and shares among HR leaders who've been sold "culture fit" dogma for years.
Suggested hook: "Your team doesn't need a group chat full of inside jokes. It needs to agree on what winning looks like."

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**Fortune's AIQ Summit: The conversation has moved from experimentation to execution** (Fortune). This is the richest story for a longer piece because it spans multiple org psych threads in one place: workforce psychological readiness, governance as a trust mechanism, burnout risk from AI-enabled overproduction, and the gap between high- and low-performing firms on talent investment. It gives Pete enough material to build a 500+ word piece connecting AI strategy to culture and retention, rather than treating AI as an IT initiative.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**Restructuring Mobile Game Giant Supercell for a Turnaround** (HBR). A concrete, vivid case of a company splitting into two structures—flat/autonomous for new creative work, hierarchical for scaling mature products—after five years without a hit. Perfect illustration for a workshop on how structures that once drove success can become liabilities, and why one-size-fits-all culture doesn't survive growth.
