# Morning Briefing — October 28, 2026

## This Week's Themes
The dominant theme this week is that AI transformation succeeds or fails based on organizational readiness, not the technology itself — see **AI Is Thriving at Capital One** (MIT Sloan Review), **American Express is cutting 5% of its workforce to 'prepare for AI'** (CNBC Business), and **AI is everywhere except in the GDP numbers — here is what triggers the break** (Fortune). A closely related thread is that human judgment remains the scarce resource in an AI-saturated world: **NSA official turned founder: AI multiplies attackers' expertise while defenders are still short on talent** (Fortune) and **AI delivers 'zero financial payoff' to most companies, Apollo economist says** (TechCrunch) both argue technology amplifies expertise rather than replacing it. A third theme centers on employee psychology under disruption — anxiety, ambivalence, and trust — captured in **Leaders Need to Grapple with Employee Ambivalence on AI** (HBR), **AI is killing 10,000-plus jobs per month and 9% of jobs are at risk, analysts say** (Fortune), and **The Hidden Upside of Tech Challenges** (MIT Sloan Review). Finally, several stories explore how leader behavior and organizational identity shape culture from the inside out, including **Careful, managers. Your mood affects your whole team's performance** (Fast Company), **What Interruptions Say About Your Organizational Culture** (MIT Sloan Review), and **How the CEO of Rolls-Royce Turned an Underperforming Company Around** (HBR).

## Top Story
**AI Is Thriving at Capital One** (MIT Sloan Review) is the top story because it gives CHROs and COOs a concrete, enterprise-scale model for what responsible AI adoption actually requires: heavy investment in training and data infrastructure, not just tool rollout. It's a direct counterpoint to the "AI disappointment" narratives circulating elsewhere this week, grounded in a real, long-term transformation at a major financial institution.

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**AI Is Thriving at Capital One** (MIT Sloan Review) has the deepest org psych angle and the most material to sustain 500+ words. The story isn't really about AI — it's about sociotechnical systems theory in action: technology adoption only works when paired with role clarity, process redesign, and sustained training investment ("tradecraft"). Pete could use this to walk CHROs through why so many AI rollouts stall (lack of infrastructure and capability-building) while Capital One's didn't, and tie it back to change management fundamentals — leadership communicating long-term vision over short-term ROI, which is itself a sensemaking and trust-building exercise. There's enough substance here — multiple functions (software dev, fraud, compliance, customer service), a named philosophy, and a clear leadership stance on patience over quick wins — to build a full case study newsletter.
