# These are the skills you need to navigate an increasingly uncertain future

**Source:** Fast Company  
**Published:** 2026-09-01T10:06:00  
**URL:** [https://www.fastcompany.com/91585811/durable-skills-for-an-uncertain-future-skills-career-advice-uncertainty](https://www.fastcompany.com/91585811/durable-skills-for-an-uncertain-future-skills-career-advice-uncertainty)

## Summary
Based on three years of teaching at the Stanford d.school, the authors identify three clusters of durable skills leaders need to navigate uncertainty: abilities to anticipate (radical curiosity, scanning for weak signals, holding multiple possibilities), abilities to imagine (reperception, developing a personal point of view, making futures feel tangible), and abilities to design (rapid prototyping of ideas, bridging diverse perspectives, strategic clarity about values and learning goals).

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The article highlights that leaders who thrive amid disruption rely on meta-cognitive orientations—ambiguity tolerance, cognitive flexibility, and divergent thinking—rather than static technical expertise, aligning with research on adaptive leadership and learning agility as predictors of performance in volatile environments.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should invest in developing 'learning agility' and ambiguity tolerance across leadership pipelines rather than over-indexing on domain expertise; build organizational capacity for environmental scanning, speculative prototyping, and cross-functional sensemaking to prepare for discontinuous change rather than merely optimizing for incremental improvement.

## Strength Score
**6/10** — The article addresses genuine leadership development constructs—ambiguity tolerance, learning agility, cognitive flexibility, and cross-boundary collaboration—that are well-studied in organizational psychology. However, it is primarily a thought-leadership piece from an academic design course rather than drawing on empirical organizational research, and the examples are individual practitioners rather than organizational cases. The org psych connection is real but somewhat conceptual rather than grounded in specific organizational dynamics.

## Original Story Summary
<p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="h-most-design-and-business-education-teaches-students-to-solve-problems-that-already-exist-but-in-a-world-shifting-at-supersonic-speed-the-more-urgent-skill-isn-t-problem-solving-it-s-learning-how-to-notice-navigate-and-shape-the-problems-coming-next">Most design and business education teaches students to solve problems that already exist. But in a world shifting at supersonic speed, the more urgent skill isn’t problem-solving—it’s learning how to notice, navigate, and shape the problems coming next.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">After three years of teaching our course &#8220;View from the Future&#8221; at the Stanford d.school, and learning alongside practitioners building at the absolute edges of their fields, a striking pattern has emerged. The leaders who thrive through disruptive times don&#8217;t rely on static technical expertise. Instead, they rely on a set of durable skills—foundational orientations to anticipate, imagine, and design new possibilities. These capacities compound over a career, and they are learnable.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-abilities-to-anticipate">Abilities to Anticipate</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="h-exploring-the-future-is-about-cultivating-a-stance-of-active-anticipation-and-learning-to-discern-between-what-is-probable-and-what-is-possible">Exploring the future is about cultivating a stance of active anticipation, and learning to discern between what is <em>probable</em> and what is <em>possible</em>.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="h-most-organizations-view-tomorrow-through-the-lens-of-probable-futures-they-take-the-realities-of-today-and-extrapolate-them-out-a-bit-trends-extend-technologies-mature-behaviors-shift-in-predictable-arcs-while-useful-for-quarterly-planning-it-keeps-you-trapped-inside-the-cone-of-what-is-already-thinkable-moving-beyond-that-cone-requires-a-combination-of-unbridled-curiosity-and-the-stamina-to-navigate-ambiguity">Most organizations view tomorrow through the lens of &#8220;probable futures.&#8221; They take the realities of today and extrapolate them out a bit—trends extend, technologies mature, behaviors shift in predictable arcs. While useful for quarterly planning, it keeps you trapped inside the cone of what is already thinkable. Moving beyond that cone requires a combination of unbridled curiosity and the stamina to navigate ambiguity.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="h-the-leaders-we-ve-studied-consistently-move-past-narrow-domain-expertise-to-become-interested-and-interesting-across-a-wide-range-of-topics-this-radical-curiosity-helps-them-spot-the-quiet-disconnected-signals-of-nascent-trends-before-those-trends-hit-mainstream-consciousness-consider-the-modern-luddite-phenomenon-coined-by-nelly-mensah-and-cynthia-selin-a-burgeoning-movement-among-younger-generations-to-reclaim-agency-from-technology-signaled-years-in-advance-by-ai-fatigue-big-tech-backlash-and-the-elevated-social-status-of-being-offline-those-who-saw-it-coming-were-scanning-widely-not-staring-straight-ahead-months-later-we-saw-it-manifest-globally-from-gen-z-graduates-booing-ai-references-at-commencements-to-a-broader-cultural-pushback-against-automated-lives">The leaders we&#8217;ve studied consistently move past narrow domain expertise to become interested—and interesting—across a wide range of topics. This radical curiosity helps them spot the quiet, disconnected signals of nascent trends before those trends hit mainstream consciousness. Consider the &#8220;Modern Luddite&#8221; phenomenon (coined by Nelly Mensah and Cynthia Selin)—a burgeoning movement among younger generations to reclaim agency from technology, signaled years in advance by <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/artificial-intelligence" title="AI">AI</a> fatigue, Big Tech backlash, and the elevated social status of being offline. Those who saw it coming were scanning widely, not staring straight ahead. Months later, we saw it manifest globally, from Gen Z graduates booing AI references at commencements to a broader cultural pushback against automated lives.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="h-anticipating-shifts-like-these-requires-staying-open-long-enough-for-the-right-signals-to-emerge-without-collapsing-prematurely-into-a-single-comfortable-answer-the-most-sophisticated-practitioners-we-ve-encountered-aren-t-predicting-exactly-where-things-are-going-they-are-tracking-gaps-holding-multiple-possibilities-at-once-and-building-toward-them-leonardo-giusti-chief-design-officer-of-archetype-ai-exemplifies-this-while-the-tech-world-debates-whether-ai-is-primarily-software-or-hardware-he-is-focused-entirely-on-the-ambiguous-middle-ground-he-calls-physical-ai-systems-that-continuously-sense-and-interpret-the-world-rather-than-waiting-to-be-prompted-focusing-on-the-in-between-like-this-matters-because-it-s-where-new-possibilities-take-shape">Anticipating shifts like these requires staying open long enough for the right signals to emerge without collapsing prematurely into a single, comfortable answer. The most sophisticated practitioners we&#8217;ve encountered aren&#8217;t predicting exactly where things are going; they are tracking gaps, holding multiple possibilities at once, and building toward them. Leonardo Giusti, Chief Design Officer of Archetype AI, exemplifies this: while the tech world debates whether AI is primarily software or hardware, he is focused entirely on the ambiguous middle ground he calls &#8220;Physical AI&#8221;—systems that continuously sense and interpret the world rather than waiting to be prompted. Focusing on the &#8220;in between&#8221; like this matters because it&#8217;s where new possibilities take shape.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-abilities-to-imagine">Abilities to Imagine</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="h-science-fiction-writer-bruce-sterling-once-called-futurism-the-art-of-reperception-the-ability-to-look-at-something-that-already-exists-and-see-it-in-a-fundamentally-different-way-interrogating-the-familiar-to-unlock-a-totally-new-possibility-what-we-ve-learned-is-that-this-isn-t-a-mystical-gift-it-s-a-practiced-orientation-nbsp">Science fiction writer Bruce Sterling once called futurism the &#8220;art of reperception&#8221;—the ability to look at something that already exists and see it in a fundamentally different way, interrogating the familiar to unlock a totally new possibility. What we&#8217;ve learned is that this isn&#8217;t a mystical gift; it&#8217;s a practiced orientation.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="h-if-anticipation-illuminates-the-edge-of-what-s-possible-imagination-is-what-brings-those-ideas-to-life-with-tangibility-and-form-the-future-belongs-to-those-who-can-create-the-picture-or-story-first">If anticipation illuminates the edge of what&#8217;s possible, imagination is what brings those ideas to life with tangibility and form. The future belongs to those who can create the picture or story first.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Speculative artist and &#8220;body architect&#8221; Lucy McRae embodies this. She literally defined her own job title to reflect her interdisciplinary talents when existing roles didn&#8217;t capture her capabilities. Working at the intersection of bioengineering and the human body, she created a visual short called Swallowable Parfum—depicting a digestible capsule that releases fragrance through your skin. The technology doesn&#8217;t yet exist, but the film was so vivid it generated real purchasing inquiries from major cosmetics companies. She made a future not yet feasible feel real enough to move people to act.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Crucially, this kind of imagination requires a developed personal point of view (POV)—a lens that reflects individual taste, depth of experience, and hard-earned judgment. In a world increasingly flooded with AI-generated output, your particular way of seeing is the one thing that cannot be replicated. Technical skill might get someone a first interview, but a distinct POV is what makes contributions truly unique.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-abilities-to-design">Abilities to Design</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="h-the-practitioners-navigating-disruption-most-gracefully-share-a-comfort-with-giving-form-to-ideas-fast-making-thinking-tangible-across-different-mediums-a-quick-sketch-a-rough-material-sample-a-role-played-scenario-before-a-solution-is-fully-formed-in-an-era-of-digital-abundance-hands-on-making-becomes-more-valuable-not-less-it-surfaces-blind-spots-advances-experimentation-and-moves-teams-forward-faster-than-endless-conversation">The practitioners navigating disruption most gracefully share a comfort with giving form to ideas fast: making thinking tangible across different mediums—a quick sketch, a rough material sample, a role-played scenario—before a solution is fully formed. In an era of digital abundance, hands-on making becomes more valuable, not less. It surfaces blind spots, advances experimentation, and moves teams forward faster than endless conversation.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="h-but-giving-form-to-an-idea-is-only-half-the-battle-you-also-have-to-design-the-conditions-where-genuinely-different-perspectives-can-collide-productively-civic-innovator-jennifer-brandel-has-built-her-career-around-this-kind-of-sensemaking-across-difference-from-reinventing-public-engagement-in-journalism-to-exploring-what-she-calls-the-interstitium-the-connective-tissue-that-keeps-communities-resilient-when-asked-what-wisdom-would-most-improve-our-collective-future-her-answer-was-disarmingly-simple-go-out-and-figure-out-how-to-get-along-as-automation-absorbs-transactional-work-the-human-capacity-to-bridge-and-convene-across-difference-becomes-our-highest-value-asset">But giving form to an idea is only half the battle. You also have to design the conditions where genuinely different perspectives can collide productively. Civic innovator Jennifer Brandel has built her career around this kind of sensemaking across difference, from reinventing public engagement in journalism to exploring what she calls the &#8220;Interstitium&#8221;—the connective tissue that keeps communities resilient. When asked what wisdom would most improve our collective future, her answer was disarmingly simple: <em>&#8220;Go out and figure out how to get along.&#8221; </em>As automation absorbs transactional work, the human capacity to bridge and convene across difference becomes our highest-value asset.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="h-holding-it-all-together-is-a-strategic-design-practice-knowing-what-you-re-trying-to-learn-not-just-what-you-re-trying-to-build-the-practitioners-who-navigate-disruption-most-gracefully-aren-t-the-ones-with-the-clearest-view-of-what-s-coming-they-re-the-ones-with-the-clearest-sense-of-what-they-value-and-what-they-re-building-toward">Holding it all together is a strategic design practice: knowing what you&#8217;re trying to learn, not just what you&#8217;re trying to build. The practitioners who navigate disruption most gracefully aren&#8217;t the ones with the clearest view of what&#8217;s coming. They&#8217;re the ones with the clearest sense of what they value, and what they&#8217;re building toward.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-moving-beyond-the-map">Moving Beyond the Map</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="h-what-three-years-teaching-with-people-working-at-the-edge-of-future-professions-has-taught-us-is-that-there-is-no-single-blueprint-for-what-comes-next-and-that-s-exactly-the-orientation-we-need-to-cultivate-in-the-next-generation-of-leaders-not-a-static-map-but-a-way-of-moving">What three years teaching with people working at the edge of future professions has taught us is that there is no single blueprint for what comes next. And that&#8217;s exactly the orientation we need to cultivate in the next generation of leaders: not a static map, but a way of moving.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="h-as-one-student-beautifully-reflected-at-the-end-of-the-quarter-before-i-saw-the-future-as-a-deterministic-march-toward-perfect-efficiency-now-i-see-that-we-actually-get-to-choose-what-values-guide-us-into-the-future">As one student beautifully reflected at the end of the quarter: <em>&#8220;Before, I saw the future as a deterministic march toward perfect efficiency. Now I see that we actually get to choose what values guide us into the future.&#8221;</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="h-the-future-isn-t-waiting-to-be-predicted-it-s-waiting-to-be-designed-and-leaders-educators-and-builders-have-far-more-agency-in-that-process-than-they-think-that-agency-comes-from-intentionally-building-the-durable-skills-that-turn-future-uncertainty-into-opportunity-skills-around-sensing-what-s-possible-seeing-what-s-desirable-and-creating-what-s-newly-doable-they-are-practices-of-awareness-synthesis-and-experimentation-design-practices-that-help-us-build-a-desirable-future-rather-than-simply-accept-the-one-we-re-handed">The future isn&#8217;t waiting to be predicted. It&#8217;s waiting to be designed. And leaders, educators, and builders have far more agency in that process than they think. That agency comes from intentionally building the durable skills that turn future uncertainty into opportunity. Skills around sensing what&#8217;s possible, seeing what&#8217;s desirable, and creating what&#8217;s newly doable. They are practices of awareness, synthesis, and experimentation. Design practices that help us build a desirable future, rather than simply accept the one we&#8217;re handed.</p>
