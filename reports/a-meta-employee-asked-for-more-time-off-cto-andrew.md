# A Meta Employee Asked for More Time Off. CTO Andrew Bosworth Called the Question ‘Very Dumb’

**Published:** Sat, 08 Aug 2026 19:15:53 -0400

## Summary
Meta CTO Andrew Bosworth publicly called an employee's question about restoring paid 'Meta Days' off 'very dumb,' arguing that productivity gains from AI tools should be channeled into more output rather than more leisure time for employees.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
A senior leader publicly shaming an employee's question about benefits signals low psychological safety and reflects a transactional view of the employer-employee relationship, where productivity gains are captured solely by the organization rather than shared with workers.

## Leadership Lesson
How leaders respond to employee questions—especially in public forums—sets the tone for organizational culture. CHROs should coach executives that dismissive or humiliating responses to legitimate benefit inquiries suppress voice behavior, erode trust, and can damage employer brand, even when the answer is 'no.' The framing of AI productivity gains as belonging exclusively to the company rather than being shared also has significant implications for employee engagement and retention.

## Strength Score
**9/10** — This story sits squarely at the intersection of psychological safety, leader communication style, organizational justice, and the emerging tension around how AI productivity dividends are distributed. Bosworth's public rebuke is a textbook case of voice suppression by leadership, and the underlying policy question about who benefits from productivity gains is a core org psych and labor economics issue.
