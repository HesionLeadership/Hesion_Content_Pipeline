## This Week's Themes
AI disruption and its psychological toll on workers is the dominant throughline this week, visible in **Will AI kill the Asian entertainment industry, or reinvent it?** (Fortune), **'Dungeons & Dragons' creative teams join union as concerns mount over pay discrepancies and 'forced AI use'** (Fast Company), and **The rise of the 'two-job job': 61% of workers say they're now doing more than one role** (Fortune). A parallel thread examines whether ethics and oversight functions have real teeth inside fast-moving organizations, seen in **OpenAI's human rights lead: What the military could do with AI 'keeps me up at night'** (Fortune), **OpenAI's safety firings raise awkward questions** (Fortune), and **OpenAI safety employee resigns, claiming the company's 'culture is broken'** (TechCrunch). Leadership structure itself is under scrutiny too—**Why the AI age calls for 'founder mode'** (Fortune) champions high-agency, centralized decision-making, while the co-CEO arrangements at Skydance/Paramount (**David Ellison and Ynon Kreiz announce Skydance leadership team**, **David Ellison just brought in a co-CEO to run his new empire**, CNBC) raise the opposite question of role clarity under shared power. Finally, trust-based management keeps surfacing as a differentiator, from **'We need to trust our employees': This billionaire CEO went from counting cars in the parking lot past 6pm to embracing hybrid work** (Fortune) to the organizational-identity balancing act in **How Christina Zhu turned Sam's Club into Walmart's unlikely growth engine in China** (Fortune).

## Top Story
**Why the AI age calls for 'founder mode'** (Fortune) matters because it names the leadership style CHROs and COOs are already fighting with internally: high-agency, conviction-driven, close-to-the-work decision-making that speeds up AI-era execution but can just as easily concentrate power and crowd out dissent. With Altman, Barra, and Kalanick as live examples, it gives Pete's audience a concrete frame for the tension between speed and governance that's showing up across their own org charts.

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**OpenAI's human rights lead: What the military could do with AI 'keeps me up at night'** (Fortune) has the deepest org psych angle of the batch. It raises a question every CHRO building a responsible-AI or ethics function should be losing sleep over: does this role have actual authority to stop or shape decisions, or is it a branding exercise that lets leadership point to a title while real power stays with engineers and sales? Sarah Yager's candor about military AI applications, autonomous weapons, and the narrow demographic makeup of the people setting AI policy gives Pete material to unpack groupthink, symbolic compliance structures, and the gap between stated values and operational power—exactly the kind of structural hypocrisy that a 500+ word piece can dig into with nuance a LinkedIn post can't.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**Will AI kill the Asian entertainment industry, or reinvent it?** (Fortune) is a strong live illustration of how workforce displacement anxiety gets managed differently depending on regulatory and cultural context—contrasting China's rapid AI adoption and studio layoffs with Western unionized resistance (SAG-AFTRA). It's a clean case study for a workshop segment on change management: the same disruptive technology, two very different organizational responses to employee voice and job security.
