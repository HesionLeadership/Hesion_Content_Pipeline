# ‘The bottleneck used to be dumpling-folding talent’: Din Tai Fung CEO on America’s top-grossing restaurant chain turning craft into corporate training

## Summary
Din Tai Fung, now America's top-grossing restaurant chain by per-store sales, overcame its historic growth bottleneck—scarce dumpling-folding talent—by building a 12-week corporate training program and standardized systems, mirroring how other Chinese-American food businesses (like Panda Express) scaled by replacing family/apprenticeship labor with codified processes.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story illustrates how organizations scale craft-based, tacit knowledge (traditionally transferred via apprenticeship/family labor) by converting it into explicit, teachable systems—a core challenge in organizational learning and knowledge management theory.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs scaling specialized or craft-dependent operations should invest early in codifying tacit expertise into structured training pipelines (with clear role titles, time-to-competency benchmarks, and dedicated training managers) rather than relying on informal mentorship or family/network-based labor pools, which don't scale and create succession risk.
