**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
Kiley Pulley, a 23-year-old who started as a part-time Walmart cashier at 16, is now the company's youngest U.S. store manager, earning six figures by consistently volunteering for extra responsibilities and seeking mentorship. The article also notes that Walmart CEOs John Furner and Doug McMillon similarly rose from entry-level roles by proactively taking on visible, high-exposure tasks.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story illustrates internal labor market mobility and self-initiated job crafting, where employees who proactively seek stretch assignments and visibility with senior leaders accelerate promotion rates and build perceived promotability through low-risk 'trial runs' of higher roles.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should formalize low-risk promotion pathways (e.g., covering for managers, cross-functional projects, succession-planning exposure) so high-potential employees can demonstrate readiness before formal promotion, reducing hiring risk and increasing retention of ambitious talent.
