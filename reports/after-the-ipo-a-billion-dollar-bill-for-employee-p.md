# After the I.P.O., a Billion-Dollar Bill for Employee Paydays

**Source:** NYT Business RSS  
**Published:** Sat, 12 Sep 2026 12:00:02 +0000  
**URL:** [https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/12/business/dealbook/double-trigger-rsus.html](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/12/business/dealbook/double-trigger-rsus.html)

## Summary
Startups are using compensation strategies like stock-based pay and deferred expenses that keep reported costs low during private years, but upon going public face massive catch-up bills—sometimes billions of dollars—when employee stock options vest and equity compensation must be recognized. This creates a sudden financial reckoning that can shock investors and reshape company finances post-IPO.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This highlights the tension between short-term compensation cost management and long-term organizational financial health. Deferred equity compensation creates psychological contracts with employees who expect significant payouts at IPO, while simultaneously obscuring the true cost of human capital from future stakeholders.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should ensure compensation strategies are transparent and sustainable—equity-heavy pay structures that defer costs can attract talent but create hidden liabilities. Leaders must model the full lifecycle cost of compensation packages and communicate honestly with both employees and investors about the true economics of their workforce investment.
