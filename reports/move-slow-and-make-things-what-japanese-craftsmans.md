# Move slow and make things: What Japanese craftsmanship can teach business about quality, patience, and expertise when AI makes speed cheap

**Source:** Fortune  
**Published:** Sun, 18 Oct 2026 09:00:00 +0000  
**URL:** [https://fortune.com/2026/10/18/ai-human-craft-competitive-advantage/](https://fortune.com/2026/10/18/ai-human-craft-competitive-advantage/)  
**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
The article argues that as AI makes speed and routine output cheap, businesses should adopt Japanese 'shokunin' (craftsmanship) principles—depth over breadth, cultivated taste, patience/vertical integration, and ownership of one's work—to build enduring competitive advantage. Key list items: Principle 1: Go deep, not wide (Shimano's century of bicycle component focus); Principle 2: Taste is a skill (Japanese denim makers' pursuit of authentic selvedge craftsmanship); Principle 3: Patience as strategy (YKK's vertical integration and long-term trust-building); Principle 4: Sign your work (introduced via the Avedis cymbal-making origin story, cut off before full explanation).

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The piece touches on how deep domain mastery, patience, and intrinsic pride in craft (rather than short-term metrics) shape organizational identity, employee expertise development, and long-term trust-based relationships with stakeholders.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should consider that as AI absorbs routine work, the strategic use of freed-up time should go toward deepening expertise and quality control rather than just increasing output volume—and that building long-tenured, specialized talent (via depth-focused career paths and vertical ownership of key processes) can become a durable source of competitive advantage that's hard to replicate.
