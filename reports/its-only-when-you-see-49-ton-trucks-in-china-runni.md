## Summary
Octopus Energy CEO Greg Jackson argues that 'incumbency cost'—the tendency of established industries to keep doing what they've always done—is the biggest brake on European competitiveness, particularly in electric vehicles, where Chinese manufacturers without legacy auto industries are outpacing European incumbents. He illustrates this with his own career pivoting from reframing a struggling mirror business to building Octopus Energy as a tech-first outsider that overtook UK energy incumbents in nine years.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story illustrates organizational inertia and the 'competency trap'—where established firms' sunk investments, routines, and stakeholder relationships (what Jackson calls 'incumbency') actively prevent them from perceiving or acting on disruptive opportunities that outsiders see clearly.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should recognize that organizational success breeds structural blind spots—incumbents often need deliberate mechanisms (separate innovation units, outside hires, customer-outcome framing) to escape entrenched assumptions, since internal politics and legacy business models often make incumbents policy-captured and slow to adapt rather than genuinely outcompeted on merit.
