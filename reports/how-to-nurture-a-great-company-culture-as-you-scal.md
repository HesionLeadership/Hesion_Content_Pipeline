# How to nurture a great company culture as you scale up

**Source:** Fast Company  
**Published:** 2026-08-24T05:00:00  
**URL:** [https://www.fastcompany.com/91591352/how-to-nurture-a-great-company-culture-as-you-scale-up](https://www.fastcompany.com/91591352/how-to-nurture-a-great-company-culture-as-you-scale-up)

## Summary
As companies scale, maintaining an engaged culture becomes harder due to increased complexity and distance between leadership and frontlines. The article recommends three 'brand anchors' to preserve culture during growth: 1) An inspiring purpose that articulates why the company exists beyond profit, 2) A crystal-clear vision that specifies where the company is headed with no ambiguity, and 3) Compelling core values that define expected behaviors and how the organization will achieve its goals.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The article aligns with research on purpose-driven organizations and value congruence, where shared organizational identity buffers against the cultural dilution that accompanies structural complexity during scaling. The Gallup finding that employees with strong purpose are 5.6x more engaged underscores the motivational power of meaning-making in organizational contexts.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs scaling their organizations should codify and actively reinforce purpose, vision, and values as structural anchors before growth dilutes founder-era culture—treating these not as aspirational wall posters but as operational tools embedded in hiring, onboarding, performance management, and leadership communication practices.

## Strength Score
**6/10** — The article directly addresses a core organizational psychology topic—culture maintenance during organizational growth—and references relevant research (Gallup engagement data, organizational values literature). However, the treatment is practitioner-level and somewhat surface-level, relying heavily on well-worn examples (Nike, Southwest, JFK) without deeper engagement with the complexity of culture change, implementation challenges, or potential downsides of strong cultures. The three anchors are sound but not novel in the OB literature.

## Original Story Summary
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The larger your business grows, the more difficult it is to maintain a <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91512743/how-best-cultures-operate-like-open-kitchens" id="https://www.fastcompany.com/91512743/how-best-cultures-operate-like-open-kitchens" type="link">highly engaged culture</a> where employees are excited about the work they do and are committed to helping the organization meet its business and <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/branding" title="Branding">branding</a> goals. Why? Because growth automatically introduces complexity in the workplace and causes the distance between the boardroom and the front line to grow as workplace head count increases.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">As the business scales, instead of experiencing the passion of the founders firsthand, new hires receive guidance from other employees who may not have that same passion for the business. Instead of senior leadership being able to pull together the entire team at a moment’s notice into all-hands huddles to communicate important information and updates, senior leadership now has to send (often impersonal) emails and memos rather than having face-to-face encounters—losing out on opportunities to strengthen workplace bonds between leadership and employees.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Out of necessity, the workforce becomes structured into new departments (and sometimes additional locations), and new layers of management are brought on to oversee those departments, with each new layer increasing the chance that the senior leadership’s vision for the company becomes lost in translation.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For founders and senior leadership teams, navigating these adverse symptoms of growth can feel like making your way through a minefield blindfolded. Fortunately, keeping your culture intact as you scale is a solvable problem. Just take a look at companies like Southwest Airlines, Patagonia, Ritz-Carlton, and Zappos—all recognized for their highly engaged culture—and you’ll quickly realize that culture does not have to become a casualty of growth.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">After spending nearly two decades in the branding industry helping companies build brands that are loved by employees and customers (in that order), I’ve identified a handful of assets that have immense potential to help companies anchor their brands in place as they scale. Perhaps unsurprisingly, I refer to them as “brand anchors.” Below are the top three brand anchors that I recommend to my senior leadership clients who are experiencing high growth in their organizations and who want to preserve their culture as they scale.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-1-inspiring-purpose">1. Inspiring purpose</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The first brand anchor that a company should have is an inspiring purpose that articulates why the company exists beyond making money. When a company has an inspiring purpose that resonates with employees, employees are more likely to feel emotionally connected to the organization, find meaning in their roles, and focus their energies on a singular goal.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For instance, Nike exists to bring inspiration and innovation to every athlete in the world. Because they understand the company’s “why,” employees constantly challenge themselves to create products, technologies, and experiences that inspire athletes and help them perform better. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In effect, Nike’s “why” anchored a culture of innovation in place, even as it scaled from being a scrappy startup to become the business leviathan it is now. If Nike’s purpose was just to sell more shoes, its employees would never have been inspired to pursue the innovations that have made Nike the business it is today—with an <a href="https://insights.greyb.com/nike-patents/">estimated 30,000 patents</a> for innovations that have helped equip athletes to perform at their peak.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">If you’re wondering why having an inspiring purpose&nbsp;can help anchor an organization in place, the answer lies in the power of engagement. Research by <a href="https://news.gallup.com/poll/697403/purposeful-work-boosts-engagement-few-experience.aspx">Gallup</a> has shown that employees with a strong sense of purpose at work are 5.6 times as likely to be engaged in their jobs as those with a low sense of purpose.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-2-crystal-clear-vision">2. Crystal-Clear Vision</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The second brand anchor I recommend companies invest in is what I refer to as a crystal-clear vision. If a company’s purpose clarifies why it exists, a crystal-clear vision explains where the company is going. <a href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/01492063251390851">Research</a> suggests that clarity of vision is linked to positive outcomes, such as job satisfaction, organizational commitment, and employee engagement.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">One of the most powerful examples of a crystal-clear vision comes from President John F. Kennedy&#8217;s challenge to NASA in 1961, when he declared: &#8220;This nation should commit itself to achieving the goal, before this decade is out, of landing a man on the moon and returning him safely to the Earth.&#8221;&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">JFK’s vision was clear, specific, and easy for anyone to understand, with absolutely no room for ambiguity. Every scientist, engineer, technician, and support employee involved in the space program knew exactly what the vision was and what they were working toward. More importantly, they embraced the vision and worked together to make it a reality.&nbsp;</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-3-compelling-core-values">3. Compelling Core Values</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The third brand anchor I recommend is having compelling core values. While an inspiring purpose clarifies why the organization exists and a crystal-clear vision explains where the business is going, compelling core values clarify <em>how</em> the organization will get where it’s going and how employees should behave along the journey. <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010974/">Research</a> suggests that shared organizational values provide meaning and purpose to work, which ultimately results in better workplace performance.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A good example of how compelling core values, once embraced by employees, support an organization’s purpose and vision is <a href="https://www.southwest.com/about-southwest/">Southwest Airlines</a>, a company renowned for its legendary customer service. Southwest has been <a href="https://www.swamedia.com/news-and-stories/news-release/southwest-airlines-ranked-first-by-jd-power-in-customer-satisfaction-for-fifth-c-MCKCYDY7NATRBZ5NPQDVDSS4NYPM">ranked first</a> by JD Power in customer satisfaction among economy travelers for five consecutive years. It has also been on the receiving end of <a href="https://www.swamedia.com/about-southwest/awards-and-recognition">dozens more awards</a> for its customer service over the years. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Southwest’s purpose, “to connect people to what&#8217;s important in their lives through friendly, reliable, and low-cost air travel,” explains why the company exists, while its vision, “to be the world&#8217;s most loved, most efficient, and most profitable airline,” clarifies where the organization is going.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Southwest has three core values, which the company has chosen to express as its Employee Promise: “I will demonstrate my Warrior Spirit by striving to be my best and never giving up. I will show my Servant’s Heart by delivering Legendary Customer Service and treating others with respect. I will express my Fun-LUVing Attitude by not taking myself too seriously and embracing my Southwest Family.” </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">These core values are also directly responsible for Southwest’s well-deserved reputation for its off-the-charts customer service, for its highly engaged culture, and for employees who are more than willing to go the extra mile for passengers.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Maintaining a highly engaged culture as you scale can be challenging, but it’s not impossible. If you want to anchor your culture in place, try implementing these three brand anchors across your organization. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Start with “why” by articulating an inspiring purpose. Let your people know where you’re taking the organization by sharing a crystal clear-vision. And be clear about what behaviors are expected of employees by documenting and reinforcing your compelling core values. Chances are that if you do a good job of implementing these brand anchors into your workplace, you’ll be in a much better position to maintain your culture as you grow.</p>
