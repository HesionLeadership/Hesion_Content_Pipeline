# Research: Why Some Junior Employees Work Well with AI—and Others Don’t

**Source:** HBR  
**Published:** 2026-07-23T12:05:05Z  
**URL:** [http://feeds.hbr.org/2026/07/research-why-some-junior-employees-work-well-with-ai-and-others-dont](http://feeds.hbr.org/2026/07/research-why-some-junior-employees-work-well-with-ai-and-others-dont)

## Summary
A new study reveals that foundational skills like critical thinking, AI literacy, and domain knowledge don't reliably predict which junior employees will work effectively with AI tools, suggesting other factors are at play.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The finding challenges conventional assumptions about AI readiness, pointing to dispositional and metacognitive factors—such as adaptability, willingness to iterate, and comfort with ambiguity—as potentially more important than technical competence in human-AI collaboration.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should rethink AI training programs that focus solely on technical upskilling; instead, invest in identifying and developing employees' adaptive mindsets, metacognitive skills, and tolerance for uncertainty, and redesign selection and development criteria for AI-augmented roles accordingly.
