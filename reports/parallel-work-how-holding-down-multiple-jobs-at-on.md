## Original Story Summary
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Employees used to go to work and then come home. Now, they commonly go to one job and then to another. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It&#8217;s called parallel work, and it essentially means taking on multiple jobs as opposed to having a single full-time position. That’s different from just having a <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/side-hustle" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">side gig</a> in addition to a full-time one; it’s also different from <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91516745/how-to-make-fractional-leadership-work" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">fractional work</a>, wherein senior-level employees work a fraction of full-time hours for a variety of clients. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Parallel work means having literally two jobs at once—they could even be two full-time roles at the same company, or maybe even working in an entirely different career on top of your existing one.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">While it sounds offbeat, it provides extra income streams and professional opportunities in parallel to one another. And it’s becoming more widespread.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">According to a <a href="https://careers.intch.org/research" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">new study from Intch</a>, a platform that connects top professionals with businesses for part-time roles, employees are taking on new roles while keeping their old ones in droves.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The study analyzed more than 142,000 employees&#8217; career histories, which included 1.05 million roles, and found that parallel work has doubled across generations: While around 29% of boomers hold multiple roles at the same time, Gen Xers do so 44% of the time. And for the youngest generations of workers, the numbers are even higher: 59% of Gen Zers are working in multiple roles, while 64% of millennial workers do. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Essentially, that means employment is now less of a straight line than ever. These days, workers are gaining multiple specialized skills at once by juggling concurrent positions, often in the same area, but for different employers.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Yakov Filippenko, CEO and founder of Intch, tells <em>Fast Company</em> that employees &#8220;holding positions at different companies at the same time&#8221; is another way that parallel roles are playing out.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;We have a real-life example on our own team at Intch: Our head of HR also has a full-time job at another tech company, and our head of payments has a similar arrangement,&#8221; Filippenko says.<br /><br />The CEO adds: &#8220;Both are fully transparent about it. It&#8217;s a way for professionals to pursue opportunities at more than one company without leaving their existing jobs.”</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">While working side gigs isn&#8217;t exactly new, more people seem to be holding multiple roles that are either full-time or part-time as a way to maintain financial security, but also to diversify their skills. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">According to a <a href="https://www.fiverr.com/news/fiverr-next-gen-of-work-report-2025" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">2025 survey</a> from Fiverr, an online marketplace for freelance services, 67% of Gen Zers said they need more than one stream of income for financial reasons. And more than half (55%) said they believe traditional employment will become obsolete, as individuals take on more than one role more often. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Still, holding multiple roles might be savvy in terms of your bank account and broadening your skill set, but it seems likely to lead to burnout.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">However, some Gen Zers say that using technology—like leaning on <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/artificial-intelligence" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">AI</a> or even being able to run businesses online with a flexible schedule—actually creates a sense of freedom.<br /><br />Carissa Ferguson, an actor who does voice-overs, creates content online, and is a copywriter, said in the Fiverr survey that balancing several roles allows for financial freedom that makes leaning deeper into creative work possible. “Having multiple income streams means I never have to turn down an acting or creative opportunity because I’m worried about paying rent,” she says.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Ferguson continues: “I can pack up my laptop and keep my revenue streams running from anywhere. That’s freedom most traditional employees will never experience.” </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Given that younger generations tend to work several jobs simultaneously, it makes sense that they&#8217;re experiencing higher levels of burnout as well. According to a 2025 <a href="https://talkerresearch.com/poll-a-quarter-of-americans-are-burnt-out-before-theyre-30/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">poll of 2,000 adults</a>, a quarter of workers are citing burnout before reaching the age of 30.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Interestingly, while the trend took off during the pandemic—which made sense because many workers had spare time—the data has barely budged since. In 2019, 68% of professionals started a new job while already employed. By 2022, that number had grown to 75%, where it has largely remained. In 2025, 73% were still doing so.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Employees used to <a href="https://www.inc.com/joe-procopio/why-employees-get-away-with-secretly-working-multiple-jobs/91253934" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">attempt to hide the fact</a> that they were working <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91372762/employees-should-be-able-to-work-multiple-jobs" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">multiple jobs at once</a>. But as the trend has stuck around, and a modern economy perhaps demands multiple streams of income, that&#8217;s no longer true. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;In a separate snapshot of 700,000 profiles, 97% remained publicly discoverable, and professionals were roughly six times less likely to hide their profiles than they had been two years earlier,&#8221; the Intch study explained.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The trend may mean that leaders have to look at <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/hiring" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">hiring</a> differently, moving toward specialized part-time roles for individual tasks instead of filling one complete position with one person.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Likewise, they may also need to examine résumés from a different lens. While a long list of positions was once seen as a lack of focus, this mindset appears to be changing. This shift is particularly true given the median professional has held seven positions over their career.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">However, the research asserts that this is not necessarily a bad thing. In fact, in modern times, as taking on multiple jobs is commonplace, it could even suggest more &#8220;depth&#8221; rather than &#8220;drift.&#8221;</p>
