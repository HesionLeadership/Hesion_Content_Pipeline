# Palantir CEO Alex Karp says neurodivergent workers will thrive in the AI era. An autistic psychologist says he has a point

**Source:** Fortune  
**Published:** Sun, 25 Oct 2026 08:09:00 +0000  
**URL:** [https://fortune.com/2026/10/25/palantir-ceo-alex-karp-neurodivergent-people-tech-ai-think-differently/](https://fortune.com/2026/10/25/palantir-ceo-alex-karp-neurodivergent-people-tech-ai-think-differently/)  
**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
Palantir CEO Alex Karp, who is dyslexic, claims neurodivergent workers will thrive in the AI era due to unique cognitive traits like pattern recognition and nonlinear thinking. Autistic psychologist Daniel Wendler agrees there's merit to this but cautions against stereotyping all neurodivergent people as hidden geniuses, arguing the deeper opportunity is redesigning workplaces that have historically been built around neurotypical norms.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story touches on cognitive diversity as an asset during organizational disruption, and highlights how workplace systems are often designed around neurotypical defaults, creating structural disadvantages for neurodivergent employees that AI-driven change could help correct.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should avoid framing neurodivergent inclusion as hiring 'hidden geniuses' and instead focus on redesigning workflows, communication norms, and accommodation structures that allow diverse cognitive styles to contribute—this is both an equity issue and a potential innovation lever during periods of technological disruption.
