# Morning Briefing — September 28, 2026

## This Week's Themes
Two dominant threads emerge today: the psychology of leaders under pressure, and the hidden risks organizations build into themselves without noticing. Stories 1, 2, 3, and 9 all circle back to how leaders and employees process uncertainty and stress — whether that's misdiagnosing stress types, letting anxiety sabotage transformation, or watching burnout quietly unravel a marquee AI lab. Meanwhile, stories 6, 7, and 8 point to structural fragility hiding in plain sight: knowledge concentration, unresolved founder tension, and the illusion of team alignment. AI's role in organizational life is also maturing as a topic — it's no longer just "will AI replace jobs" but questions of accountability (Story 10) and skill atrophy in the next generation of experts (Story 4). Taken together, the throughline is diagnosis over assumption: leaders keep applying generic fixes (reduce workload, trust that "we're aligned," assume buy-in) to problems that require much more specific, structural intervention.

## Top Story
Story 1 (HBR: Work Stress Isn't Always About Workload) is the most immediately useful piece for CHROs and COOs. It reframes a problem every executive thinks they already understand — stress — by resurrecting decades-old role theory and turning it into an actionable diagnostic. This is exactly the kind of research-backed reframe that gives Pete's audience a new lens for old problems, and it's easy to translate into a client tool or assessment.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate
Story 6 (Your biggest risk? The employees you rely on the most) has the strongest hook potential — it's counterintuitive, universally relatable, and taps into real anxiety leaders have about turnover. 
Hook: "Your best employee isn't your biggest asset. They might be your biggest risk."

## Best Newsletter Candidate
Story 2 (MIT Sloan: How Leadership Anxiety Derails Transformation) has the richest research base — nearly 760 hours of observation and 300+ interviews — and the psychological paradox at its core (leaders sabotaging the very change they championed) is layered enough to sustain 500+ words of analysis. It's a natural fit for unpacking the gap between cognitive commitment and emotional threat response, with direct implications for how Hesion designs leadership development around change initiatives.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
Story 8 ("We're Aligned") is a perfect live-audience exercise. It's concrete, easy to demonstrate in the room (have three people restate a decision in their own words and watch the mismatches appear), and illustrates shared mental model theory in a way that lands instantly with technical and non-technical audiences alike.
