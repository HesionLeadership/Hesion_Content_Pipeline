# Why Lateral Hires Rarely Become CEO

**Source:** HBR  
**Published:** 2026-08-05T12:15:07Z  
**URL:** [https://hbr.org/2026/08/why-lateral-hires-rarely-become-ceo](https://hbr.org/2026/08/why-lateral-hires-rarely-become-ceo)

## Summary
Lateral hires rarely become CEO because organizations form collective judgments about institutional trust and representational legitimacy long before boards formally select a leader. Insiders accumulate 'institutional capital'—deep relational networks, cultural fluency, and organizational trust—that external hires struggle to replicate regardless of their competence.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This reflects research on in-group favoritism, organizational socialization, and the role of relational trust in leadership emergence. Leaders who rise internally benefit from repeated social exchanges that build legitimacy, making CEO selection as much about accumulated social capital as about demonstrated capability.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs should recognize that CEO succession pipelines are shaped by invisible institutional trust-building that begins years before formal selection. If the organization wants lateral hires to be viable CEO candidates, it must deliberately create structured opportunities for them to build relational capital, cultural credibility, and organizational trust—not just deliver results.
