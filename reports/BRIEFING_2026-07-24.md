# Morning Briefing — July 24, 2026

## Top Story
**Gallup CEO says disengagement is back to 2020 lows — four in five workers are checked out** (Fortune). This is the number that should be on every CHRO's desk this morning. Despite billions spent on engagement programs, wellness perks, and culture initiatives, the needle hasn't moved — which means the entire industry's approach is fundamentally broken. For Hesion's audience, this is both a wake-up call and a business development opportunity: if your engagement strategy isn't anchored in manager quality, job design, and systemic accountability, you're decorating a burning building.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate
**"The 'Job-Hugging' Trend Is Cracking: Why Half of Employees Are Now Looking to Leave"** (Inc). This is LinkedIn gold because it's counterintuitive and urgent — most leadership teams are interpreting low turnover as a sign their culture is working, when it's actually a pressure cooker about to blow. **Hook:** *"Your best people didn't stay because they were engaged. They stayed because they were scared. And now they're leaving."*

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**"For women, being creative at work comes with a hidden cost"** (Fast Company). This deserves deep analysis because it sits at the intersection of three themes Pete writes about — psychological safety, innovation culture, and equity — and reveals a structural contradiction most organizations haven't confronted. You can unpack the double bind (organizations demanding creativity while punishing women who display it), connect it to role congruity theory, challenge the assumption that "encouraging innovation" is a neutral act, and offer concrete structural interventions (anonymous ideation, blind evaluation of proposals, auditing whose ideas get funded). This is a 700-word piece that positions Hesion as thinking beyond surface-level DEI and into the mechanics of how bias actually operates in innovation systems.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**"Your Company Doesn't Need You as Much as You Think. That's the Problem"** (Inc). This is a perfect opening exercise for a leadership offsite or keynote on organizational resilience and delegation. The concept it illustrates: **founder/leader dependency as a structural failure, not a badge of honor.** Pete could ask the room, "Could your organization run for two weeks without you? If the answer is no, that's not proof of your value — it's proof of your failure to build capacity." It's provocative, personal, and immediately diagnostic. Pairs well with content on distributed leadership, succession depth, and the psychology of indispensability.
