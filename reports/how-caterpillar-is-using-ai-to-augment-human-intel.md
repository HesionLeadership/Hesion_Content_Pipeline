# How Caterpillar is using AI to augment human intelligence—and optimize its supply chain

**Source:** Fast Company  
**Published:** 2026-09-02T11:00:00  
**URL:** [https://www.fastcompany.com/91596460/jamie-engstrom-caterpillar-pacesetters-2026](https://www.fastcompany.com/91596460/jamie-engstrom-caterpillar-pacesetters-2026)

## Summary
Caterpillar CIO Jamie Engstrom leads the company's AI transformation by creating an AI Center of Excellence, building internal data tools, and optimizing supply chain operations. She champions an 'additive' approach where AI augments rather than replaces human intelligence, emphasizing safe experimentation, trust in governance, and cultural buy-in as critical to technology adoption.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
Engstrom's approach highlights that technology adoption is fundamentally a trust and culture problem, not a technical one. Her emphasis on psychological safety through 'safe experimentation,' transparent governance, and framing AI as augmentation rather than replacement directly addresses employee resistance rooted in job insecurity and change anxiety.

## Leadership Lesson
When implementing transformative technology like AI, CHROs and COOs should invest as heavily in building employee trust—through clear guardrails, data governance transparency, and safe experimentation cultures—as they do in the technology itself. Framing AI as a tool that elevates workers to higher-value tasks rather than displacing them is essential for adoption and engagement.

## Original Story Summary
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Jamie Engstrom has a winning personal motto: &#8220;We reserve the right to work smarter tomorrow&#8221;—shorthand for her IT organization&#8217;s commitment to keep learning as <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/artificial-intelligence" title="AI">AI</a> advances and Caterpillar&#8217;s business evolves. As CIO and SVP, Engstrom <a href="https://www.forbes.com/sites/peterhigh/2025/04/21/jamie-engstrom-on-technology-talent-and-transformation-at-caterpillar/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">leads a global IT team</a> of more than 2,200 professionals across 28 countries. Though <a href="https://www.cionews.com/post/caterpillar-cio-ai" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Caterpillar has used AI</a> in some capacity for three decades, Engstrom now drives the company’s use of AI for things like advanced analytics, automation, and supply chain optimization. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">She approaches these key functions in a holistic way. “We think about a digital thread from our demand planning at the enterprise level, and supply planning through to manufacturing and operations, and being able to apply AI to that,” she says. “We&#8217;re seeing some really awesome early benefits,” she adds, citing improved quality on the factory floor, better labor planning leading to a reduction in variable labor costs, better management of material shortages, and more. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Engstrom created Caterpillar&#8217;s IT AI Center of Excellence and <a href="https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2025/accenture-and-snowflake-drive-enterprise-reinvention-with-ai-and-data" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">partnered with Accenture and Snowflake</a> to extend that work into manufacturing quality, financial reporting, and supply chain planning. “That is a major part of our strategic initiative and, frankly, where I spend a significant amount of my time,” she says. Under her direction, Caterpillar built an internal data assistant on a <a href="https://www.caterpillar.com/en/news/caterpillarNews/2026/snowflake-ai-award.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Snowflake-based</a> enterprise data foundation, letting employees query trusted company data and get answers in seconds. In June 2026, that work earned Caterpillar <a href="https://www.caterpillar.com/en/news/caterpillarNews/2026/snowflake-ai-award.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Snowflake&#8217;s AI Innovator Award</a> for the second consecutive year. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Engstrom has championed an additive, rather than a reductive, approach to leveraging artificial intelligence. AI, she says, is best used for automating mundane tasks and augmenting human intelligence so that workers can do more valuable work. “I think creating the culture [of] safe experimentation, and the education of what the ‘art of the possible’ is, is where employees are going to embrace it more, versus feeling like AI is going to be a competition to their job,” she notes.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Getting workers to buy into the idea of reimagining processes using AI is a question of trust and, Engstrom explains, it’s not just trust in the accuracy and relevance of AI models’ outputs. “Technology adoption is going to accelerate when our employees trust both the processes, the guardrails, the access, and the governance that we have in place—when they have confidence in the underpinning data to be able to know that the output that they&#8217;re getting is <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/productivity" title="Productivity">productive</a> and meaningful for them.”</p>
