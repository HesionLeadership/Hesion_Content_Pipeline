# What Interruptions Say About Your Organizational Culture

**Source:** MIT Sloan Review

## Summary
The article argues that meeting interruptions are powerful, underexamined signals of organizational culture, revealing whose voices are valued and whose are not. Drawing on research published in the Journal of Business Ethics, including vignette experiments and meeting observation studies, the authors found that uneven patterns of interruption contribute to psychological exclusion and can prompt employees to self-censor.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The piece connects micro-level interactional dynamics (turn-taking, interruption patterns) to macro-level constructs like psychological ownership, voice, and inclusion, showing how everyday communication behaviors operationalize abstract cultural values.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should train leaders to actively monitor and manage meeting dynamics—who gets interrupted and who doesn't—since these micro-moments shape employees' real-time perception of psychological safety and belonging far more than stated values or culture decks.
