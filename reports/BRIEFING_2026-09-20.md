# Morning Briefing — September 20, 2026

## This Week's Themes
The dominant theme this week is **diagnostic leadership**—the idea that leaders fail not because they lack good intentions but because they misdiagnose what's actually happening (treating all stress as overload, treating all resistance as disloyalty, treating all performance gaps as effort problems). This shows up across at least four stories. A strong secondary theme is **culture as infrastructure**, with multiple pieces (cultural rituals, organizational storytelling, sabbaticals) arguing that culture isn't built through declarations but through repeated, designed micro-behaviors and systems. **Leader self-awareness** runs through several stories too—leaders' own anxiety derailing transformation, leaders needing to anticipate emotional reactions to feedback, leaders confusing loyalty demands with earned commitment. Finally, **trust as the operating system** connects the AI surveillance piece, the AI agent adoption piece, and the sabbatical research: organizations that design for trust outperform those that design for control, whether the context is technology adoption, monitoring, or time off.

## Top Story
**How Leadership Anxiety Derails Transformation (MIT Sloan Review).** A four-year ethnographic study with 760 hours of observation is rare depth, and its core finding—that leaders sabotage the very changes they champion because of unmanaged anxiety—is the most strategically consequential insight this week. Every CHRO and COO Pete works with is running some form of transformation right now. This research names the invisible failure mode: not resistance from below, but emotional reversal from above. It makes a direct case for the kind of leader-level psychological support Hesion provides.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate
**Research: Work Stress Isn't Always About Workload (HBR).** This is tailor-made for LinkedIn because it challenges a widespread assumption with a clean, actionable framework. Most leaders default to "reduce workload" when employees report stress, and this piece says that's often the wrong intervention. **Suggested hook:** *"Your stressed-out employees don't all need less work. Some need clarity. Some need you to stop giving them contradictory priorities. Here's why the distinction matters more than you think."*

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**Dethroning Loyalty (MIT Sloan Review).** This has the deepest org psych vein to mine for a longer piece. Pete could unpack the distinction between earned commitment and demanded loyalty, connect it to psychological safety research (Edmondson), authoritarianism under economic pressure, and the real-world damage when leaders confuse compliance with engagement. There's rich territory here around how "loyalty" becomes a tool of control precisely when organizations most need honest dissent—during downturns and uncertainty. It also connects naturally to the leadership anxiety piece (anxious leaders demand loyalty as a stabilizing mechanism) and the surveillance story (monitoring as loyalty enforcement). A 500-800 word newsletter could position Pete as someone who names uncomfortable dynamics other consultants dance around.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**AI Surveillance Is Destroying the Workplace (Fast Company).** This is a vivid, concrete illustration of what happens when organizations confuse accountability with control. Pete could use this in any talk on trust, autonomy, or self-determination theory—the finding that monitored employees actually break *more* rules is counterintuitive and memorable. It lands as a cautionary tale: "You installed the software to improve performance. Here's what it actually did." It also works as a bridge into conversations about what *does* drive performance (clear expectations, output-based evaluation, psychological ownership), which is where Hesion's work lives.

- **To Adopt AI at Scale, Employees Need to Trust Agents (HBR):** Solid applied trust research. Pairs well with the surveillance story as a "trust done right vs. trust destroyed" contrast.
