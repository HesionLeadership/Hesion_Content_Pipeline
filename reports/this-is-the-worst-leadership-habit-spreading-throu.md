## Summary
The article argues that anger and intimidation are becoming a widespread management shortcut in business, with leaders using fear and aggression as tools to drive performance. It warns that this trend undermines organizational health and that smart leaders should resist adopting anger-based management styles.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
Research consistently shows that abusive supervision and fear-based leadership erode psychological safety, reduce employee engagement, and increase turnover. The normalization of anger as a leadership tool reflects a regression from evidence-based management toward destructive leadership patterns.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should actively monitor for the emergence of fear-based management norms in their organizations, invest in leadership development that builds emotional regulation skills, and establish clear behavioral standards that distinguish accountability from intimidation.
