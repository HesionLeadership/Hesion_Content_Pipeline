## Summary
Wharton professor Adam Grant argues that effective teams don't require members to like each other socially, but rather need 'task cohesion'—alignment on shared purpose and goals. He discusses practical applications including accelerated onboarding, structured informal interactions, and inviting new hires to showcase their distinctive strengths rather than simply conform to culture.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The core insight distinguishes social cohesion (liking each other) from task cohesion (shared purpose/goals), with research suggesting task cohesion is the stronger predictor of team performance in both work and sports settings.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should prioritize building alignment around mission and goals over forcing social bonding or 'family' culture; invest in structured practices like pre-boarding outreach, paired informal interactions (e.g., cross-functional lunches), and onboarding that highlights new hires' unique strengths rather than pure cultural assimilation.
