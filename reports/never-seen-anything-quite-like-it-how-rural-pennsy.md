## Summary
Rural Pennsylvania communities have formed bipartisan opposition to a proposed $165 million Amazon-linked data center (Project Hazelnut), uniting Democrats and Republicans against eminent domain power-line threats and secretive development practices. The backlash has forced Gov. Shapiro to reverse fast-track permitting policies, mirroring similar bipartisan data center resistance emerging nationally.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story illustrates a classic organizational communication failure: companies and government officials pursued rapid deployment without transparent stakeholder engagement, triggering a trust breakdown that transcended traditional political tribalism—a case study in how procedural injustice (secrecy, lack of voice, eminent domain threats) unifies otherwise divided stakeholders against an institution.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs leading large infrastructure or change initiatives should prioritize early, transparent stakeholder communication and participatory decision-making processes—failing to do so risks uniting diverse constituencies in opposition regardless of the merits of the underlying initiative, as perceived procedural unfairness often matters more than the substantive benefits being offered.
