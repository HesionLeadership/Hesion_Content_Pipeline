# Morning Briefing — August 13, 2026

## This Week's Themes
The dominant theme this week is **the architecture of distributed leadership**—multiple stories converge on how organizations must shift from centralized, heroic leadership models to systems that embed capability, creativity, and accountability across every level. Stories on founder bottlenecks (Story 1), DBS Bank's innovation KPIs (Story 10), and leadership drift (Story 9) all point to the same underlying tension: organizations say they want distributed ownership but structurally reinforce dependence on the top. A strong secondary theme is **feedback and communication quality**—the "be more strategic" piece (Story 2) and the AI FOMO piece (Story 7) both highlight how vague language and misplaced priorities distort the signal leaders send and receive. **Psychological safety as a structural design problem** rather than a feel-good aspiration appears across at least three stories (Stories 5, 6, and 7), with the gender and creativity piece adding a critical equity dimension. Finally, **change readiness and execution** threads through the HBR strategy readiness piece (Story 8) and the leadership drift article (Story 9)—both arguing that the bottleneck to strategic progress is rarely the strategy itself but the human system that must carry it.

## Top Story
**"Leadership Drift" Is Stalling Your AI Strategy (HBR)** — This one hits hardest because it names a phenomenon every CHRO and COO has felt but few can articulate: the collective, nobody's-fault paralysis that stalls leadership teams on consequential decisions. It's not about AI specifically—it's about how diffusion of responsibility, pluralistic ignorance, and status quo bias conspire to make entire executive teams inert. Pete, this is a concept your clients will immediately recognize in themselves, and it has direct implications for how you coach leadership team effectiveness.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate

Every mid-career professional has received this feedback and felt confused by it. **Suggested hook:** *"I once watched a VP tell a high-performer to 'be more strategic'—and neither of them could explain what that actually meant. ## Best Newsletter Candidate
**"For women, being creative at work comes with a hidden cost" (Fast Company)** — This deserves the full 500+ word treatment because it challenges a deeply held assumption in the innovation conversation: that psychological safety, once established, benefits everyone equally. The org psych angle is rich—you can pull in role congruity theory, the concept of idiosyncrasy credits (which connects to Story 6), and the research on backlash effects against women who display agentic behavior. The practical implications for CHROs are substantial: most organizations' innovation programs are structurally gender-blind, which means they may be systematically amplifying certain voices while penalizing others. This is a piece where Pete can combine empirical depth with a genuine call to action—audit whose ideas get heard, funded, and celebrated—without veering into platitudes.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**"How a brilliant founder can become their company's creative bottleneck" (Fast Company)** — This is a textbook illustration of how **early-stage adaptive behaviors calcify into cultural dysfunction**. The concept of "follower dependency" and learned helplessness in ideation is visceral and immediately recognizable to any executive who's watched a team wait for the founder to speak before offering ideas. The practical intervention—leader speaks last—is simple enough to demonstrate live in a workshop. Pete could even run a real-time exercise: have the most senior person in the room practice withholding their view while others generate ideas, then debrief the group's experience. It perfectly illustrates the shift from directive to participative leadership.
