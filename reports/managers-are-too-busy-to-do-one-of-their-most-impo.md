# Managers Are Too Busy to Do One of Their Most Important Jobs. 5 Ways CEOs Can Fix It

## Summary
The article argues that companies overload managers with competing priorities, administrative tasks, and excessive meetings, preventing them from doing their most important job: leading and developing their people. The 5 CEO fixes include: 1) Ruthlessly prioritize and cut conflicting mandates, 2) Reduce meeting and administrative burden, 3) Invest in manager development and coaching skills, 4) Redesign spans of control so managers have manageable team sizes, and 5) Hold senior leaders accountable for enabling managers rather than just demanding results from them.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This directly addresses role overload and role conflict in middle management—well-documented phenomena where competing demands degrade both manager well-being and subordinate outcomes. The tension between administrative burden and people leadership reflects the broader organizational challenge of aligning structural design with stated leadership expectations.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should audit the actual time managers spend on people leadership versus administrative tasks and competing priorities, then structurally redesign workloads—including spans of control, meeting cadences, and priority hierarchies—to ensure managers can fulfill the coaching and development role that drives retention and performance.
