## Summary
Psychologist Michelle Gielan discusses 'resilient optimism'—a mindset that acknowledges real challenges while maintaining belief that one's actions can still make a difference. Drawing on an 11-year dataset of 25,000+ business professionals, she links optimism to higher productivity, sales, promotion rates, and lower burnout, and explains how leaders can cultivate it in themselves and teams using techniques like 'power leads.'

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story directly engages organizational psychology constructs like dispositional optimism, locus of control/self-efficacy ('belief that behavior matters'), and burnout, and ties them to measurable performance and engagement outcomes using a validated scale (LOT-R).

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should treat optimism as a trainable competency rather than a fixed trait—leaders can be coached to acknowledge difficult realities while explicitly reinforcing employees' sense of agency, which the data suggests reduces burnout and increases engagement and productivity.
