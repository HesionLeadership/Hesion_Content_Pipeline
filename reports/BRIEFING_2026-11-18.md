# Morning Briefing — November 18, 2026

## This Week's Themes
The dominant theme this week is founder-dependency and succession governance, playing out almost entirely through the Nvidia saga—**Nvidia says it won't reinstate Huang as it reports soaring sales** (BBC Business), **Jensen Huang says he would be 'honored' if allowed to return as Nvidia CEO** (TechCrunch), **Trump backs return of ex-Nvidia boss after 'Bring Jensen Back' campaign launched** (BBC Business), and **NVIDIA Announces CEO Transition** (Hacker News) all test whether a board can hold the line on governance when a charismatic founder has outsized public and peer support. A closely related thread is succession execution done well versus poorly: **Home Depot names Richard McPhail as new CEO, beats earnings expectations** (CNBC Business) and **Meet Nvidia's billionaire interim CEO, Colette Kress** (Fortune) both show internal-promotion models designed to preserve stability, while **Where was Nvidia's security team?** (TechCrunch) and **Nvidia's new chairman Harvey Jones is a 'charming elder statesman'** (Fortune) expose the oversight blind spots that let founder-centric power concentrate unchecked in the first place. A third theme is the halo effect and misplaced loyalty to charismatic leaders, seen in **OpenAI CEO Sam Altman says it's 'hard to imagine the AI industry without Jensen'** (TechCrunch) and **Jordan Belfort's new AI voice startup, Tone, wants to sell like the 'Wolf of Wall Street'** (TechCrunch). Finally, a smaller but distinct theme on participatory decision-making and trust-building shows up in **$970 million Pacsun CEO says Gen Alpha and Gen Z are 'greatly misunderstood'—so she made a board of 12- to 22-year-olds to give her advice** (Fortune) and in workforce-trust framing around AI adoption in **Honeywell CEO says AI isn't about replacing workers as C.H. Robinson touts 60% productivity gains** (Fortune).

## Top Story
**Nvidia says it won't reinstate Huang as it reports soaring sales** (BBC Business) is the story of the day because it's a live, high-stakes test of a core org psych question: do organizations actually need their "irreplaceable" leader? Nvidia's board held firm on governance while posting record sales under an interim CEO, directly challenging the founder-dependency myth that Pete's CHRO and COO audience grapples with constantly when planning (or avoiding) succession.

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**OpenAI CEO Sam Altman says it's 'hard to imagine the AI industry without Jensen'** (TechCrunch) has the deepest org psych angle for a longer piece. It's not just a succession story—it's a live case study in the halo effect colliding with governance accountability: Huang stepped down amid affairs with employees and alleged national security concerns, yet peers like Altman and Brockman publicly expressed personal loyalty and support, and a 200,000-signature petition pushed for his return despite the board declining. This is rich territory for 500+ words: Pete can unpack how organizations build governance structures resilient to sunk-cost attachment and public pressure campaigns, why high-status leaders get given ethical passes their organizations can't afford, and how CHROs can pressure-test their own board's independence before a similar crisis hits. The tension between "we love and respect this person" and "the board must still act" is exactly the kind of nuance that makes for a meaty, non-obvious newsletter.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example

This is a concrete, teachable moment for a workshop segment on succession communication—Pete can use Colette Kress's handling of the $105B OpenAI deal framing as a real script example.
