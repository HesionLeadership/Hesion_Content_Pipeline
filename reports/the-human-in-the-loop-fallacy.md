# The “Human-in-the-Loop” Fallacy

## Summary
The article critiques the 'human-in-the-loop' model of AI adoption, arguing it merely assigns shrinking leftover tasks to humans rather than genuinely reorganizing work around new capabilities. It offers examples (IKEA, Morgan Stanley, Garfield Law, BBC Verify) of companies that rebundled roles and governance around new opportunities AI creates, rather than just using humans as a safety-check layer on automation.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The piece illustrates the 'irony of automation'—as systems become more reliable, human overseers grow complacent and deskilled, undermining the very oversight function they're meant to provide; this connects to vigilance decrement and skill atrophy research in human factors psychology.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should treat AI transformation as an opportunity to redesign roles and decision rights around newly possible work (continuous skills assessment, embedded governance, new career paths), rather than defaulting to using top talent as a fatigue-prone 'error-catching' layer on automated systems.
