# Workers Are 17 Times More Likely to Ask a Chatbot Than Their Boss. That’s a Trust Problem

**Source:** Inc  
**Published:** Fri, 11 Sep 2026 06:00:00 -0400  
**URL:** [https://www.inc.com/dave-kerpen/workers-ask-chatbot-boss-trust-problem-ai-adobe-survey/91403556](https://www.inc.com/dave-kerpen/workers-ask-chatbot-boss-trust-problem-ai-adobe-survey/91403556)

## Summary
Workers are 17 times more likely to ask an AI chatbot for help than to approach their boss, revealing a deep trust deficit in manager-employee relationships. AI is exposing that many managers were providing far less value in guidance, feedback, and support than they believed.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This highlights a fundamental breakdown in psychological safety and trust within manager-employee dyads. When employees prefer a non-judgmental AI over their supervisor for information and guidance, it signals that managers have failed to create environments where asking questions or admitting uncertainty feels safe.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should treat employees' preference for AI over managers as a diagnostic signal, not a technology trend. Invest in rebuilding managerial trust through training in psychological safety, active listening, and non-judgmental coaching—because if a chatbot outperforms your managers on approachability, you have a leadership development crisis, not a technology adoption story.
