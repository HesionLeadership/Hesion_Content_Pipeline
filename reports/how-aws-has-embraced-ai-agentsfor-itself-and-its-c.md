# How AWS has embraced AI agents—for itself and its clients

**Source:** Fast Company  
**Published:** 2026-09-02T11:00:00  
**URL:** [https://www.fastcompany.com/91599506/ruba-borno-pacesetters-2026](https://www.fastcompany.com/91599506/ruba-borno-pacesetters-2026)

## Summary
AWS VP Ruba Borno transformed her own engineering organization into an AI-first unit where 76% of production code is AI-assisted, increasing shipping throughput by 88%. She advocates starting with specific use cases and proper data infrastructure rather than wholesale transformation, and argues AI ROI should be measured by new capabilities enabled rather than cost savings on existing processes.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
Borno's approach addresses a core change management challenge: building employee trust in AI tools by ensuring they work well from the start (proper data infrastructure) and reframing success metrics so workers see AI as capability-expanding rather than cost-cutting, which reduces threat perception and resistance.

## Leadership Lesson
When driving AI adoption, leaders should focus on two trust-building levers: (1) ensure tools have proper data foundations before rollout so employees experience competent AI rather than frustrating failures, and (2) reframe measurement from 'doing the same work cheaper' to 'enabling previously impossible work'—this shifts the psychological framing from replacement threat to capability enhancement.

## Original Story Summary
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Ruba Borno leads AWS&#8217;s <a href="https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/how-to-lead-through-the-ai-disruption" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Global Specialists and Partners</a> organization, working with companies that use Amazon Web Services to turn <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/artificial-intelligence" title="AI">AI</a> advances into business impact.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">To help make the case, she&#8217;s also turned her own AWS Marketplace engineering organization into a unit where humans define the requirements and artificial intelligence generates the code, backed by autonomous agents that triage incidents and deliver fixes without waiting for a prompt. As a result, the group increased its shipping throughput by 88%, improved time to production by 21%, and maintained steady rollback rates. More than 76% of the code that goes into production is now AI-assisted. Borno has set an unambiguous internal tone: <a href="https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/how-to-lead-through-the-ai-disruption" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">&#8220;We must use AI</a> every day in nearly everything we do,&#8221; as she put it in a talk at McKinsey. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Under her leadership, AWS Partner Central introduced <a href="https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/03/aws-partner-central-agents-accelerate-co-sell/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">AI agents</a> that qualify sales opportunities on arrival, deliver tailored guidance, and route each deal to the right people. “For partners and customers, that means deals move faster and the right resources get matched sooner,&#8221; she says, adding that more than 1,000 partners have adopted the agents since launch.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">She also oversaw a reinvention of AWS Marketplace, which now features an <a href="https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2025/11/aws-marketplace-agent-mode-ai-enhanced-search/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">agent mode</a> that conversationally guides customers to offerings, as well as &#8220;<a href="https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2025/11/aws-marketplace-express-private-offers" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">express private offers</a>&#8221; that let customers get personalized pricing in minutes rather than through lengthy sales discussions.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The first key to getting worker buy-in and finding success with AI pilot programs, Borno says, is making sure that the AI tools have access to the right contextual data. This means building the plumbing (infrastructure) to connect the right data, label and organize it so it&#8217;s easy to find, and set controls to ensure only the right people or agents have access, she explains. “You don&#8217;t have to unify your entire data warehouse on day one. Work backwards from a specific use case, get that data right, and scale from there.” Without the right data, she adds, the AI models won’t have the ground truth data needed to generate useful outputs, and workers will soon lose trust.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Borno says businesses also need to think differently about measuring the success of AI programs. Most companies, she notes, try to measure AI ROI against existing processes and existing budgets, and that&#8217;s why so many pilots stall. “The greatest value of AI doesn&#8217;t come from doing the same work cheaper,” she says. “It comes from doing work that wasn&#8217;t possible before.”</p>
