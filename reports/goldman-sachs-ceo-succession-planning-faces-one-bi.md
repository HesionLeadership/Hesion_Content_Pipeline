# Goldman Sachs CEO succession planning faces one big problem

**Source:** CNBC Business  
**Published:** Tue, 29 Sep 2026 20:50:10 GMT  
**URL:** [https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/29/goldman-sachs-ceo-succession-planning.html](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/29/goldman-sachs-ceo-succession-planning.html)  
**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
Goldman Sachs' board has reportedly discussed a succession plan that would replace CEO David Solomon with President John Waldron as early as next year, elevating Solomon to executive chairman. The plan faces risk because Solomon may be reluctant to step aside amid strong bank performance, while Waldron—who has fielded outside offers and received an $80 million retention package—may not wait indefinitely.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This illustrates the classic succession paradox: incumbent leaders performing well have psychological and structural disincentives to relinquish power (loss of status, influence, identity), while designated successors experience escalating impatience and flight risk the longer transitions are delayed.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and boards should decouple succession timing from short-term performance narratives and establish binding, time-bound transition triggers (not just discussions) to prevent both CEO entrenchment and successor attrition—retention packages alone don't solve the incentive misalignment.
