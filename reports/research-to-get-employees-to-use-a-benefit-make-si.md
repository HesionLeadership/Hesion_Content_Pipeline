# Research: To Get Employees to Use a Benefit, Make Signing Up a Little Harder

**Source:** HBR  
**Published:** 2026-08-26T12:15:18Z  
**URL:** [https://hbr.org/2026/08/research-to-get-employees-to-use-a-benefit-make-signing-up-a-little-harder](https://hbr.org/2026/08/research-to-get-employees-to-use-a-benefit-make-signing-up-a-little-harder)

## Summary
Research suggests that adding a small amount of meaningful friction to the sign-up process for employee benefits—such as requiring a brief commitment exercise or questionnaire—can significantly improve long-term follow-through on benefits that require ongoing effort, like wellness programs or training initiatives.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This taps into behavioral science principles around commitment devices and effort justification: when employees invest even minimal effort to enroll, they experience greater psychological ownership and are more likely to sustain engagement, reducing the common problem of benefit underutilization.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs should reconsider the assumption that removing all friction from benefit enrollment maximizes value. Strategically designing small commitment steps at sign-up—such as goal-setting exercises or brief reflection prompts—can boost sustained participation in programs requiring ongoing effort, ultimately improving ROI on employee benefits investments.
