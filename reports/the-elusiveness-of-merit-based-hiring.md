# The Elusiveness of Merit-Based Hiring

**Source:** MIT Sloan Review  
**Published:** Tue, 04 Aug 2026 11:00:00 +0000  
**URL:** [https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/the-elusiveness-of-merit-based-hiring/](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/the-elusiveness-of-merit-based-hiring/)

## Summary
Organizations abandoning DEI commitments in favor of proclaimed 'meritocratic' hiring are not actually achieving merit-based hiring. The article argues that framing DEI and merit as antithetical dichotomies is flawed, and that simply dropping DEI programs does not automatically produce meritocratic outcomes due to persistent cognitive biases and structural barriers in hiring processes.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This directly addresses foundational org psych research on implicit bias, structured vs. unstructured hiring, and the illusion of meritocracy—where believing a system is meritocratic can actually increase biased decision-making because evaluators feel their judgments need less scrutiny.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should recognize that removing DEI programs without replacing them with evidence-based, structured hiring practices (e.g., standardized interviews, blind resume reviews, validated assessments) will likely increase bias rather than improve merit-based selection. Leaders must invest in procedurally fair talent systems regardless of political framing.
