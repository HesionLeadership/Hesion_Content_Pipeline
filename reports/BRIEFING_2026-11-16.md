# Morning Briefing — November 16, 2026

## This Week's Themes

The dominant theme this week is **key-person dependency and succession failure**, driven almost entirely by the Nvidia saga — see **Nvidia’s Jensen Huang resigns, chipmaker names CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO** (TechCrunch), **NVIDIA Announces CEO Transition** (Hacker News), and **Jensen Huang’s departure blasts big hole in AI startup funding, VCs and founders say** (TechCrunch), all pointing to the danger of centralizing deals and relationships around one charismatic founder. Closely related is a **governance blind spot** theme: **Where was Nvidia’s security team?** (TechCrunch) and **Nvidia’s new chairman Harvey Jones is a ‘charming elder statesman’ set to lead the search for the next CEO. But did he enable the current crisis?** (Fortune) both ask how formal oversight structures failed to catch escalating risk. A third theme, **charisma overriding due diligence**, carries into today's news through **Trump backs return of ex-Nvidia boss after 'Bring Jensen Back' campaign launched** (BBC Business) and **How fallout from the Nvidia scandal could dampen the hype around the AI boom** (Fast Company). Finally, a quieter but important thread on **oversight credibility and psychological safety** runs through **OpenAI will embed Apollo researchers to investigate AI subversion** (TechCrunch) and **Databricks’ new AI agent tells CEO Ali Ghodsi the unvarnished truth** (TechCrunch) — both about whether organizations can build honest feedback and accountability loops without undermining trust.

## Top Story

**Trump backs return of ex-Nvidia boss after 'Bring Jensen Back' campaign launched** (BBC Business). This is the sharpest leadership test of the week: a board conducting a legitimate conduct investigation is being pressured by a presidential endorsement, Elon Musk, and shareholders to reinstate a leader under active scrutiny. It's a live case study in whether governance processes can hold up against star power and external political pressure — exactly the kind of scenario CHROs need to war-game before it happens to them.

## Best Newsletter Candidate

**OpenAI will embed Apollo researchers to investigate AI subversion** (TechCrunch) has the deepest org psych angle of the three. It's not just an AI safety story — it's a governance paradox: OpenAI is negotiating limited access and disclosure terms with an external evaluator while having previously fired employees for sharing information with a different external safety evaluator (METR). That tension — between controlling information flow for self-protection and the credibility oversight mechanisms need to actually function — is a core issue CHROs face in compliance, whistleblower policy, and audit design, just dressed in AI language. There's room to unpack: what makes oversight "real" versus theater, how negotiated access terms create perverse incentives, and how a chilling effect on disclosure quietly erodes psychological safety long before it shows up in an incident report. It's a story that rewards the long-form treatment because the implications extend well past OpenAI into any organization running internal audit, ethics hotlines, or safety review functions.
