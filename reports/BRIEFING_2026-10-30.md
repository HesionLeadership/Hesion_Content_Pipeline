# Morning Briefing — October 30, 2026

## This Week's Themes
Three threads dominate this week. First, AI readiness gaps: organizations are racing to deploy AI faster than their data, governance, or workforce structures can support, as seen in **Insurers' AI Ambitions Are Outpacing Their Data** (HBR), **How readiness debt is affecting organizations** (Fast Company), **AI Is Thriving at Capital One** (MIT Sloan Review), and **American Express is cutting 5% of its workforce to 'prepare for AI'** (CNBC Business). Second, AI mandates are colliding with employee values and trust, shown in **You don't need to believe in a god to get a religious exemption from using AI at work** (TechCrunch) and **Lose your job to AI? Here's what to do if a company wants you back** (Fast Company). Third, leader affect and sensemaking keep surfacing as a performance lever, from **Build the Mindset That Keeps Your Team Moving Forward** (HBR) to **Careful, managers. Your mood affects your whole team's performance** (Fast Company) and **Staying Principled in a Time of Uncertainty** (HBR). A fourth, related theme is organizational inertia—**‘It's only when you see 49-ton trucks in China running on batteries that you realize how serious electrification is’: Octopus Energy CEO Greg Jackson on Europe's incumbency problem** (Fortune) and **How the CEO of Rolls-Royce Turned an Underperforming Company Around** (HBR) both show how past success breeds blind spots that outsiders or data must break through.

## Top Story
**Build the Mindset That Keeps Your Team Moving Forward** (HBR) is the strongest pick today—full article text, highest score, and a directly actionable framework for leaders. It matters because it gives CHROs and COOs a concrete alternative to the false binary of forced positivity versus doom-and-gloom crisis talk, grounding "resilient optimism" in specific tactics like reframing stress and restructuring meetings around progress and agency.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate
**You don't need to believe in a god to get a religious exemption from using AI at work** (TechCrunch) is made for LinkedIn—it's surprising, legally provocative, and taps directly into the anxiety every CHRO is quietly feeling about AI mandates. Suggested hook: "A university administrator just got a legal exemption from using AI at work—without believing in a god. Here's why every CHRO needs to see this coming."

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**Insurers' AI Ambitions Are Outpacing Their Data** (HBR) has the deepest org psych angle for a longer piece. It's not just a data-quality story—it's about trust calibration between humans and algorithms, and who holds decision rights when AI agents start making judgment calls in ambiguous, high-stakes situations. The five-question test for granting AI decision authority, plus the argument that governance must sit with executives rather than IT or compliance, gives Pete a meaty framework to unpack: how do organizations structure accountability when the "decision-maker" is no longer fully human? That's a rich 500+ word exploration of authority, trust, and organizational design under AI integration.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**‘It's only when you see 49-ton trucks in China running on batteries that you realize how serious electrification is’: Octopus Energy CEO Greg Jackson on Europe's incumbency problem** (Fortune) is a vivid, visual illustration of the competency trap—incumbents trapped by sunk investments and legacy routines, outpaced by outsiders with no legacy to protect. Jackson's own career pivot makes it a compelling personal case study for a keynote on organizational inertia and structural blind spots.
