# Morning Briefing — August 25, 2026

## This Week's Themes
**Psychological safety is under siege from multiple directions this week.** Three stories converge on leaders actively destroying it—through anger-based management (Inc), public shaming of employee questions (Meta/Bosworth), and perfectionism cultures that punish failure (Inc on innovation). That's not a coincidence; it's a pattern worth naming loudly. **Incentive and system design is the second dominant theme**—stories on innovation-blocking incentives, AI accountability gaps, and unrecognized relational labor all point to the same structural problem: organizations say they want one thing but reward another. **AI's organizational footprint keeps expanding**, showing up in three distinct stories—biased recruitment tools filtering out women, accountability gaps when employees own AI-generated decisions, and the Bosworth incident framing AI productivity gains as belonging exclusively to the company. **Gender equity** threads through multiple pieces too, with AI hiring bias and invisible relational labor both highlighting how systems structurally disadvantage women in ways that feel neutral on the surface. Finally, **the design of collaboration and cognitive bandwidth** emerges from HBR's work on intermittent collaboration and MIT Sloan's piece on frontline innovation—both arguing that how we structure work time matters more than how much effort we demand.

## Top Story
**"When Employees Are Held Accountable for AI-Generated Decisions" (HBR)** — This is the story Pete's audience needs to sit with. As organizations rush to deploy AI tools, almost none have rethought accountability structures, and this research shows the predictable result: employees are quietly reshaping, reinterpreting, and masking AI outputs to protect their credibility. CHROs and COOs are building a layer of organizational theater on top of their AI investments, and they don't even know it. This is a governance and culture design problem that will define the next two years of AI adoption.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate

It's the kind of post that gets shared because people recognize themselves or their organizations in it. **Suggested hook:** "The highest-performing person on your team is probably doing work you've never measured, will never reward, and couldn't function without. And she knows it."

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**"Your Managers Aren't Blocking Innovation. Your Incentives Are" (Inc)** — This deserves the deep treatment because it reframes one of the most common complaints Pete hears from C-suite clients ("our middle managers resist change") as a system design failure, not a people failure. Pete can layer in reinforcement theory, principal-agent dynamics, and Kerr's classic "On the Folly of Rewarding A, While Hoping for B"—then offer a concrete audit framework CHROs can use to stress-test whether their incentive architecture actually supports stated strategic priorities. There's a natural bridge to the perfectionism/innovation culture piece and the frontline innovation story that would make this a rich, multi-sourced analysis.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**Meta CTO calling an employee's question "very dumb" (Inc)** — This is a gift for any presentation on psychological safety or leader communication. It's a single, vivid, public moment that illustrates exactly how senior leaders destroy voice behavior—and it comes with a second layer about who captures AI productivity gains. Pete can use it to demonstrate that psychological safety isn't an abstract concept; it's built or destroyed in specific moments, in specific words, in front of specific audiences. Perfect for a "what would you do differently?" exercise with a leadership team.
