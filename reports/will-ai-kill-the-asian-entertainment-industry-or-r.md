**Published:** Wed, 07 Oct 2026 07:00:00 +0000

## Summary
Generative AI is rapidly disrupting Asian film and entertainment industries, with AI-generated short dramas, films, and virtual actors proliferating in China, Hong Kong, and Singapore—sometimes at a fraction of traditional production costs. The piece contrasts enthusiastic adoption in Asia (due to lighter IP regulation and cost pressures) with more resistant, unionized pushback in the West (e.g., SAG-AFTRA strikes), while noting audiences still show mixed reception to fully AI-made content.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story illustrates how organizations and industries respond differently to disruptive technology based on regulatory environment, labor organization, and cultural norms—iQiyi's full business-model pivot to AI content exemplifies organizational identity transformation under technological pressure, while the contrast between Chinese studio layoffs and Western union resistance shows divergent models of managing workforce displacement anxiety.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs managing AI-driven disruption should recognize that technology adoption speed is shaped less by capability and more by regulatory/cultural context and workforce voice mechanisms (unions, protest norms); leaders should proactively address job displacement fears and retraining rather than assuming market pressure alone will determine adoption pace.
