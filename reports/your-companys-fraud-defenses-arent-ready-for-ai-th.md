**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
AI is making it easier to fake voices, faces, and documents, allowing fraudsters to exploit gaps between business functions even when individual systems are secure. The article illustrates this with a 1960s case where Tino De Angelis used forged warehouse receipts and seawater-filled tanks to fake $175 million in vegetable oil collateral, exploiting lenders' trust in the American Express name rather than verifying the actual goods.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The case illustrates how organizations substitute trust in institutional brands or symbols (a trusted name, a signature, a familiar voice) for direct verification, creating structural blind spots between departments or functions that bad actors can exploit.

## Leadership Lesson
COOs and CHROs should audit the handoff points between business functions (finance, procurement, HR, operations) where trust in a name, title, or credential substitutes for independent verification—because AI will make these trust shortcuts far easier to exploit than in the De Angelis era.
