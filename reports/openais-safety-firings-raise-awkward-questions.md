# OpenAI’s safety firings raise awkward questions

**Source:** Fortune  
**Published:** Mon, 05 Oct 2026 11:09:59 +0000  
**URL:** [https://fortune.com/2026/10/05/openai-safety-firings-raise-awkward-questions/](https://fortune.com/2026/10/05/openai-safety-firings-raise-awkward-questions/)  
**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
OpenAI fired three safety researchers (Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak, Mikita Balesni) for allegedly sharing confidential company information with an outside AI-safety organization, sparking debate over whether this constitutes whistleblower retaliation. The firings coincide with another safety-focused departure (David Robinson, who resigned and criticized OpenAI's culture as 'broken') and come amid increasing regulatory scrutiny and industry pressure for AI labs to be more transparent about safety incidents.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This story illustrates the tension between organizational control/confidentiality and psychological safety for employees raising safety concerns, particularly when internal channels are perceived as inadequate or when the organization's culture discourages external escalation of concerns.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should proactively establish clear, trusted internal channels for employees to raise safety/ethical concerns, because when employees feel internal reporting is ineffective or risky, they may turn to external parties—creating legal, reputational, and cultural fallout. Ambiguous policies around information-sharing with external watchdogs/auditors (especially in evolving regulatory environments) need to be clarified before incidents force reactive, defensive terminations that can appear retaliatory even if legally defensible.
