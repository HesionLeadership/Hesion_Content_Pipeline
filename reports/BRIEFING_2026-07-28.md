# Morning Briefing — July 28, 2026

## Top Story
**Gallup CEO says fixing the workplace may be harder than colonizing Mars** (Fortune). Four in five workers globally are disengaged — back to 2020 levels — despite billions poured into engagement programs over the past decade. This is the single most damning indictment of how most organizations approach engagement: they're surveying without acting, offering perks instead of redesigning work, and failing to hold managers accountable for the human experience on their teams. For CHROs and COOs, this isn't a data point — it's an existential question about whether their current playbook is fundamentally wrong.

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**For women, being creative at work comes with a hidden cost** (Fast Company). This story has layers worth unpacking in 500+ words: the overlap between creativity traits and masculine-coded behavior, the double bind this creates as organizations demand more innovation in the AI era, and the structural interventions that actually work versus the well-meaning but useless "just speak up" encouragement. Pete can connect this to psychological safety research (Edmondson), role congruity theory (Eagly & Karau), and practical audit frameworks CHROs can deploy immediately. It also ties neatly to Story 6 on likability labor, creating a thematic package about who actually bears the cost of organizational culture.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**Stripe's culture of urgency without aggression** (Fast Company). This is a masterclass case study for any presentation on high-performance culture. It concretely illustrates the concept that psychological safety and high standards are not trade-offs — they're multipliers. Specific, stealable practices (rigorous writing culture, leaders doing operational work themselves, mission framing that expands talent pools, 268 alumni founders as proof of entrepreneurial identity formation) make this tangible rather than aspirational. Use it to replace the tired Google/Pixar examples. The concept it illustrates: **cooperative rigor as a performance architecture**, not just a vibe.
