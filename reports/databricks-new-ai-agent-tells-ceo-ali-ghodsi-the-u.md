## Summary
Databricks unveiled an updated AI agent, Genie, capable of completing multi-step tasks autonomously using an ontology built from customer data. CEO Ali Ghodsi demonstrated using it internally to analyze employee satisfaction survey results, evaluate executive-suggested fixes, summarize a failed sales pitch, and prep for interviews—framing it as a way to access unfiltered information he wouldn't otherwise hear at his company's scale (12,000 employees).

## Organizational Psychology Angle
As organizations grow beyond Dunbar's number (~150), information filtering and self-censorship increase up the hierarchy, insulating leaders from negative feedback; Ghodsi frames AI agents as a technological workaround to this structural communication breakdown.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should recognize that organizational scale inherently degrades the flow of candid feedback to leadership, and should actively build structures (AI-assisted or otherwise, such as skip-level meetings, anonymous surveys, or ombudspersons) that counteract this natural filtering rather than assuming technology alone solves the underlying trust and psychological safety gaps.

## Strength Score
**6/10** — The story raises a legitimate and well-documented org psych phenomenon (information distortion and MUM effect in hierarchies, Dunbar's number) but the piece is ultimately a product promotion for Databricks' Genie, and the 'unvarnished truth' framing is anecdotal and unverified—AI summarizing data isn't equivalent to solving trust or psychological safety issues, which are more about interpersonal dynamics than data access.
