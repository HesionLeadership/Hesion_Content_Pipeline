# This CEO lived on canned soup and took just two days off for his daughter’s birth. Now, he admits he lost sight of proper work-life balance

**Published:** Sat, 07 Nov 2026 14:23:20 +0000  
**URL:** [https://fortune.com/article/acely-ceo-ron-schneidermann-work-life-balance-ate-canned-soup-startup-hustle-gen-z-advice-former-alltrails/](https://fortune.com/article/acely-ceo-ron-schneidermann-work-life-balance-ate-canned-soup-startup-hustle-gen-z-advice-former-alltrails/)  
**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
Serial startup CEO Ron Schneidermann describes sacrificing work-life balance while building Liftopia (living on canned soup, taking only two days off for his daughter's birth), and how that experience led him to institute company-wide balance rituals as CEO of AllTrails (monthly outdoor days) and now Acely (monthly no-deliverable hackathons). He also advises young workers to prioritize genuine networking and curiosity over credentials.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story illustrates how leaders' lived experiences of burnout can shape organizational policy design, and how founder-modeled overwork norms get institutionalized unless deliberately counteracted with structural rituals (e.g., mandated breaks).

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should recognize that leader behavior and personal narratives of sacrifice often get mythologized into cultural norms; proactively building structural rituals (protected time, no-deliverable days) is more effective than relying on individual willpower to shift burnout culture.
