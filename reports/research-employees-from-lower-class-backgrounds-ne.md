# Research: Employees from Lower-Class Backgrounds Negotiate Less—and Face More Backlash

**Source:** HBR  
**Published:** 2026-08-03T12:05:27Z  
**URL:** [https://hbr.org/2026/08/research-employees-from-lower-class-backgrounds-negotiate-less-and-face-more-backlash](https://hbr.org/2026/08/research-employees-from-lower-class-backgrounds-negotiate-less-and-face-more-backlash)

## Summary
Research shows employees from lower socioeconomic backgrounds negotiate less frequently for pay and benefits, and when they do negotiate, they face greater backlash and penalties compared to peers from higher-class backgrounds. The article discusses how leaders can redesign negotiation systems and address systemic bias to create more equitable outcomes.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This highlights the intersection of social class, workplace equity, and structural bias in compensation processes—demonstrating how organizational systems can perpetuate inequality through informal negotiation norms that disproportionately disadvantage employees from lower-class origins.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs should move toward structured, transparent compensation frameworks (e.g., standardized pay bands, proactive salary adjustments) rather than relying on individual negotiation, and train managers to recognize class-based bias in how they respond to negotiation attempts from different employees.
