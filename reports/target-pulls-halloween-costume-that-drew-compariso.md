# Target pulls Halloween costume that drew comparisons to blackface caricatures, apologizes

**Source:** Fast Company  
**Published:** 2026-08-24T23:00:00  
**URL:** [https://www.fastcompany.com/91595260/target-halloween-costume-clown-blackface-jim-crow](https://www.fastcompany.com/91595260/target-halloween-costume-clown-blackface-jim-crow)

## Summary
Target pulled a children's Halloween costume from its website after social media users pointed out its resemblance to blackface minstrelsy caricatures. The incident follows Target's 2025 decision to roll back its DEI initiatives, with critics arguing the lack of diverse perspectives in decision-making rooms led to the oversight.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This case illustrates how reducing workforce diversity in decision-making pipelines—particularly after scaling back DEI programs—can create blind spots in product development and marketing review processes, leading to culturally harmful outcomes that damage organizational reputation and stakeholder trust.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should ensure that product review and approval processes include diverse perspectives at every stage—from design to marketing photography. Cutting DEI infrastructure doesn't just signal values; it removes the organizational safeguards that prevent costly and reputation-damaging errors born from homogeneous decision-making.

## Original Story Summary
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Target has removed a children&#8217;s Halloween costume from its website after social media users pointed out that it closely resembled imagery from Jim Crow-era blackface minstrelsy.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The $25 costume—listed as &#8220;Kids&#8217; Glows Under Blacklight Circus Clown Halloween Costume Bodysuit&#8221;—had a black-and-orange harlequin pattern alongside a mask featuring an oversize, exaggerated smile. The product&#8217;s <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/marketing" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">marketing</a> photograph showcased a Black child wearing the costume while holding a pose that people said evokes segregation-era <a href="https://atlantablackstar.com/2026/08/24/target-pulls-halloween-costume-over-racist-image/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">caricatures of Black people,</a> which were performed by white people in blackface.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">After posts on Threads, <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/tiktok" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">TikTok</a>, and X quickly gained visibility on August 23, Target removed the costume—part of its &#8220;Hyde and Eek! Boutique&#8221; collection—from its website. According to <em><a href="https://www.newsweek.com/target-removes-kids-clown-halloween-costume-after-backlash-12359344" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Newsweek</a></em>, metadata from the original listing indicates that the product had been available since earlier this summer.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;As a company, we know we got this wrong, and we are deeply sorry. The costume is offensive and should never have been part of our assortment. It is no longer available for sale,&#8221; the company said in statement on Monday. As of Monday evening, Target had not responded to requests for comment from <em>Fast Company. </em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;This is what happens when you don&#8217;t have people in the room that look like us. You end up doing some shit like this,&#8221; a user noted <a href="https://www.tiktok.com/@therealdoubledeemuva/video/7677267142387043597?is_from_webapp=1&amp;sender_device=pc" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">on TikTok</a>.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Target&#8217;s statement continued: &#8220;We know this is especially hurtful for our Black guests, team members, and partners. Removing the costume is an important first step, and the company is looking closely at how this happened and what needs to change to ensure this won’t happen again.&#8221;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The controversy also follows Target&#8217;s decision to roll back its diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) initiatives in 2025. The decision was met with backlash and boycott efforts from the public, some of which are ongoing as community organizers <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91508957/the-target-boycott-over-dei-isnt-over-yet" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">seek more concrete DEI commitments</a> from the retailer.</p>
