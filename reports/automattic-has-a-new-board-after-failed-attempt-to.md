# Automattic has a new board after failed attempt to put CEO on leave

**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
Automattic CEO Matt Mullenweg rebuilt the company's board weeks after the previous board tried and failed to place him on leave; the new board includes a sci-fi author, a wellness author, and two co-founders of the failed, bot-plagued social app IRL. Mullenweg used his 84% voting control to reverse the ouster attempt within 33 hours and replaced departing directors, the CFO, and chief legal counsel.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This case illustrates the risks of concentrated founder control and weak board independence: when a CEO holds supermajority voting power, governance mechanisms designed to provide checks and balances become largely symbolic, and board 'independence' can be replaced with loyalist appointees after a failed challenge.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should recognize that dual-class share structures and founder-controlled voting can neutralize board oversight; when evaluating governance risk, organizations should assess not just board composition but the actual power dynamics behind removal and appointment authority, and build safeguards (e.g., independent counsel access, whistleblower protections) that don't depend solely on board goodwill.
