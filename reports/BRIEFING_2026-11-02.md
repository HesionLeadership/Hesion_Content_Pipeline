# Morning Briefing — November 02, 2026

## This Week's Themes
The dominant theme this week is AI oversight anxiety—the gap between how organizations say they're deploying AI and how it actually reshapes human judgment, surfacing in **The AI Governance Paradox** (MIT Sloan Review), **The "Human-in-the-Loop" Fallacy** (HBR), **EqualAI CEO: Your AI assistant is acting in your name. Who signed off?** (Fortune), and **'The best thing that you can do for your career is to make yourself redundant': Inside Stellantis' AI push after the 'starting gun went off'** (Fortune). A second theme is suppressed dissent and overconfidence in leadership structures, running through **Boards Need Conflict to Make Better Decisions. Here's What Holds Them Back.** (HBR) and **Silicon Valley has a crisis of (over) confidence** (Fast Company). A third theme is leadership instability eroding organizational identity, visible in **Why Wendy's is losing the burger wars—and is now a takeover target for Nelson Peltz** (Fortune) and **How the CEO of Rolls-Royce Turned an Underperforming Company Around** (HBR). Finally, several stories point to how organizational environments shape behavior beyond formal policy, including **Want to increase voter turnout? Start with the workplace** (Fortune) and **Your Employees' Unofficial Shortcuts Aren't the Problem. Your Official Process Is** (Inc).

## Top Story
**The AI Governance Paradox** (MIT Sloan Review). This matters because it reframes a governance problem as a human factors problem: well-intentioned "human in the loop" oversight can quietly convert vigilant, proactive employees into passive alarm-responders. For CHROs and COOs rolling out AI monitoring in high-stakes settings, this is a direct warning that governance design choices can erode the very judgment they're meant to protect.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate
**'The best thing that you can do for your career is to make yourself redundant': Inside Stellantis' AI push after the 'starting gun went off'** (Fortune) — the quote itself is a built-in hook, and the tension between genuine excitement and skill-loss fear makes it instantly relatable to any audience managing AI rollouts.
Suggested hook: "An engineer told Fortune the best thing for his career is to make himself redundant. Here's why that's either brilliant leadership or a psychological time bomb."

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**The "Human-in-the-Loop" Fallacy** (HBR). This has the deepest org psych angle of the batch because it names a precise mechanism—the "irony of automation"—where increasing system reliability breeds human complacency and deskilling, undermining the oversight function humans are supposedly there to provide. It's backed by concrete company examples (IKEA, Morgan Stanley, Garfield Law, BBC Verify) showing what it looks like when organizations get this right by rebundling roles around new capabilities instead of relegating people to shrinking leftover tasks. This gives Pete real case material to build a 500+ word piece around: he can walk through why "human in the loop" has become a reassuring but hollow phrase, connect it to vigilance decrement and skill atrophy research from human factors psychology, and then pivot to a practical framework—continuous skills assessment, embedded governance, new career paths—that CHROs can actually implement. It also pairs naturally with the Stellantis and AI Governance Paradox stories, giving him room to synthesize a multi-source argument about redesigning decision rights rather than just supervising machines.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**Why Wendy's is losing the burger wars—and is now a takeover target for Nelson Peltz** (Fortune).
