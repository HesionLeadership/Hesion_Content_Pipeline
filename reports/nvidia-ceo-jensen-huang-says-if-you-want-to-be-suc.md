# Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang says if you want to be successful, prepare to suffer: ‘If it’s a walk in the park, anybody could do it’

## Summary
Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang argues that enduring suffering, resilience, and low expectations are essential to achieving extraordinary success, a philosophy echoed by Ray Dalio's 'Pain + Reflection = Progress' and Harvey CEO Winston Weinberg's views on failure.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story touches on grit, resilience, and reflective learning from failure as drivers of achievement, but also risks glorifying chronic overwork and fear-driven motivation (Huang's 7-day workweeks and persistent sense of vulnerability) as virtues rather than burnout risks.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should distinguish between building organizational resilience through structured reflection on setbacks (Dalio's model) versus normalizing a culture of suffering or fear-based overwork, which can drive burnout and attrition even among high performers.

## Strength Score
**6/10** — There's a genuine connection to resilience, grit, and failure-based learning research, but the piece is largely anecdotal, survivorship-biased (billionaire CEOs rationalizing extreme behavior), and lacks critical examination of the downsides of 'suffering' as a leadership philosophy.
