## This Week's Themes

The dominant theme this week is **trust calibration in human-AI collaboration** — how much autonomy to grant AI systems and how to sequence it without triggering resistance or complacency, seen in **Exclusive: Honeywell Technologies launches a 'brain behind the building' with Nvidia and Dell. Its CTO says humans will run it for now** (Fortune), **How long will humans be needed for translation?** (BBC Business), and **Fortune's AIQ Summit: The conversation has moved from experimentation to execution** (Fortune). A second theme is **procedural justice and institutional trust as enablers (or blockers) of change**, running through **No forms, no fuss: Abu Dhabi wants AI to eliminate government paperwork** (Fortune), **'Never seen anything quite like it': How rural Pennsylvania's $165 million data center fight became representative of the bipartisan battle against AI** (Fortune), and the recruitment fairness breakdown in **'I applied for 200 jobs' - How 'spray and pray' AI job applications can backfire** (BBC Business). A third theme is **manager behavior and psychological safety as the real engagement lever**, visible in **This is how great managers respond to disengaged employees** (Fast Company, article not read), **Why a good boss matters more than working from home** (Fast Company), and the whistleblower chilling-effect stories on OpenAI. Finally, **insider trust and security** surfaces sharply in **FBI arrests OpenAI employee accused of planting hidden recording device in conference room** (TechCrunch).

## Top Story

**'I applied for 200 jobs' - How 'spray and pray' AI job applications can backfire** (BBC Business) is the top story. It exposes an escalating arms race where AI is degrading the signal value of hiring's most basic tools — CVs, cover letters, interviews — on both sides of the table simultaneously. For CHROs, this isn't a tech problem; it's a validity and fairness crisis in selection systems that demands structural redesign, not patchwork fixes.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate

Hook: "Your hiring process isn't broken because of AI. It was already broken — AI just made the cracks visible."

## Best Newsletter Candidate

**'I applied for 200 jobs' - How 'spray and pray' AI job applications can backfire** (BBC Business) has the deepest org psych angle and the most room to unpack in 500+ words. The story isn't really about AI — it's about what happens when the signaling mechanisms both sides rely on (a well-crafted cover letter, a thoughtful interview answer) stop meaning anything. This is a procedural justice problem: candidates report feeling the process is "unfair" and "exhausting," which has direct implications for employer brand and offer-acceptance rates, not just screening efficiency. A newsletter could walk through: (1) why traditional selection tools were already weak predictors of job performance before AI accelerated their collapse, (2) the psychological toll of "spray and pray" on candidates and the parallel toll of "AI misconduct" detection on recruiters, (3) concrete alternatives — structured interviews, work samples, situational judgment tests — that are validated, harder to game, and arguably more humane, and (4) the transparency imperative: organizations that disclose their own AI use in screening will likely retain more trust than those caught hiding it. This gives Pete a chance to position Hesion as the firm that redesigns selection architecture, not just reacts to the latest AI headline.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example

**Exclusive: Honeywell Technologies launches a 'brain behind the building' with Nvidia and Dell. Its CTO says humans will run it for now** (Fortune) works well as a live illustration of **deliberate trust calibration and change sequencing in automation rollouts**. The concrete detail — fully autonomous capability deliberately held back for 2-3 years to let humans verify decisions — gives Pete a vivid, non-abstract example for workshops on managing AI-driven organizational change without triggering resistance.
