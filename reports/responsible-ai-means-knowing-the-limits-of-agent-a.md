## Summary
MIT Sloan Management Review and BCG's annual study explores how organizations are navigating responsible AI implementation, specifically focusing on the limits of agent autonomy and the governance frameworks needed to manage AI decision-making within organizations.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The tension between granting autonomy to AI agents and maintaining human oversight mirrors classic organizational challenges around delegation, trust, and control. Leaders must design governance structures that balance efficiency gains from autonomous AI with accountability and ethical guardrails, paralleling research on autonomy and supervision in human teams.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should establish clear boundaries for AI agent autonomy that mirror how they design human authority structures—defining decision rights, escalation protocols, and accountability frameworks. Building organizational capability for responsible AI requires the same intentionality as developing human talent governance, with explicit policies about what decisions can be delegated to AI and which require human judgment.
