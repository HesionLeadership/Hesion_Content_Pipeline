# Morning Briefing — September 23, 2026

## This Week's Themes
The dominant thread today is the gap between what organizations say and what they actually do — showing up in culture values (Story 3), culture rituals (Story 4), and management behavior (Story 5). A second strong theme is leadership's own psychological blind spots: anxiety sabotaging transformation (Story 1) and stress mismanagement rooted in poor diagnosis rather than poor intent (Story 2). AI adoption pressure is emerging as a fresh org psych issue, showing up in both the IT/AI team conflict story (Story 10) and the "bots create hidden error risk" story (Story 6) — a signal this will be a growing beat. Finally, a talent-resilience cluster (mentorship, PTO coverage, culture scaling) points to organizations under-investing in structural redundancy and development, wanting shortcuts to what actually requires design work.

## Top Story
Story 1 (MIT Sloan Review, Leadership Anxiety Derails Transformation) is the standout — a rare four-year ethnographic study with real data (760 hours, 300+ interviews) showing that leaders themselves are often the reason transformations fail, not employee resistance. This flips the usual change management narrative and gives Pete a research-backed hook for his core audience: CHROs and COOs need to manage leadership psychology, not just employee buy-in.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate
Story 5 (5 Toxic Management Behaviors) — it's concrete, relatable, and every reader has a manager story that fits one of the five. Hook: "Your best people aren't quitting over pay. They're quitting because their manager took credit for their work and never had the hard conversation."

## Best Newsletter Candidate
Story 2 (HBR, Work Stress Isn't Always About Workload) has the deepest theoretical foundation — it maps directly onto Kahn et al.'s 1964 role stress theory, giving Pete a chance to write a substantive piece distinguishing ambiguity, conflict, and overload, and showing why most wellness programs fail by treating all three as the same problem. Plenty of room for frameworks, diagnostic tools, and case examples in 500+ words.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
Story 9 (Danny Meyer/Shake Shack) is the strongest live-audience example — a recognizable brand and leader, concrete practices (hiring for emotional skills, employees-before-customers), and a clear illustration of person-organization fit and culture erosion during scale. Easy to build a 10-minute case study around.
