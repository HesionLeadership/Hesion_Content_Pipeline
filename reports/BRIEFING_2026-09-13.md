# Morning Briefing — September 13, 2026

## This Week's Themes
The dominant theme this week is **leader self-awareness as infrastructure**—not as a soft skill, but as a structural requirement for organizational performance. Multiple stories converge on the idea that leaders' unexamined psychological patterns (anxiety, loyalty demands, feedback blind spots) actively sabotage the outcomes they're trying to produce. A strong secondary theme is **identity and trust in the age of AI**: three stories tackle how AI is disrupting employee identity, eroding trust through surveillance, and requiring new transparency frameworks for adoption—collectively painting a picture of organizations struggling to integrate AI without destroying the human psychological contract. A third thread runs through **culture as behavioral infrastructure**: the stories on rituals, storytelling, and team dynamics all argue that culture lives in micro-practices and narratives, not in strategy decks. Finally, **systemic equity** surfaces through the negotiation research, reminding us that even well-intentioned organizations embed class-based bias into their most routine processes.

## Top Story
**How Leadership Anxiety Derails Transformation** (MIT Sloan Review) — This is the week's most important read for Hesion's audience. A four-year ethnographic study with nearly 760 hours of observation reveals what most change management frameworks ignore: leaders don't fail at transformation because they lack strategy or buy-in—they fail because their own anxiety triggers controlling or avoidant behaviors that systematically undermine the change they championed. For CHROs and COOs managing transformation portfolios, this reframes the problem entirely: the biggest risk to your change initiative isn't resistance from the middle—it's unmanaged emotion at the top. The practical implication is that organizations need psychological infrastructure for leaders (peer coaching, structured reflection, real psychological safety at the executive level), not just for frontline employees.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate
**AI Surveillance Is Destroying the Workplace** (Fast Company) — This story has the ideal LinkedIn formula: a provocative, counterintuitive claim backed by solid research, and it hits a nerve with every CHRO and COO navigating hybrid work and productivity anxiety. The self-determination theory angle gives it intellectual weight without being academic. **Suggested hook:** *"Companies that monitor every keystroke are getting exactly what they're measuring—and losing everything that actually matters."*

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**There's a New Workforce Crisis and It Isn't Burnout** (Fast Company) — This is the deepest org psych story of the week and deserves a full-length treatment. The shift from behavioral disengagement (quiet quitting) to identity withdrawal is a meaningful conceptual escalation that most HR leaders haven't grasped yet. Pete could unpack the identity threat mechanism in detail: when AI strips away the visible skills around which employees built their professional selves, you don't get laziness—you get existential withdrawal that no engagement survey will detect and no perks program will fix. There's rich material here to connect to role redesign, the C-suite perception gap, and why traditional engagement interventions are pointed at the wrong problem. This is a 700-word piece waiting to happen.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**Dethroning Loyalty** (MIT Sloan Review) — This is a powerful workshop illustration because every leader in the room will initially bristle at the idea that loyalty is problematic—and that's exactly the point. It perfectly illustrates the distinction between earned commitment and demanded loyalty, and it lands hard during economic downturns when authoritarian instincts quietly intensify. Use it as a provocative opening: *"Raise your hand if loyalty is one of your core leadership values"*—then walk through how loyalty demands suppress dissent, enable poor decisions, and create the exact cultural fragility that makes organizations vulnerable during stress. It's a concrete, emotionally resonant way to teach psychological safety and constructive dissent.
