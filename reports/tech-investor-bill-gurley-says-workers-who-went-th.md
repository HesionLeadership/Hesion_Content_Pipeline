# Tech investor Bill Gurley says workers who went through the ‘college conveyor belt’ and chased safe jobs are at high risk of AI automation

**Source:** Fortune  
**Published:** Sat, 21 Nov 2026 14:24:02 +0000  
**URL:** [https://fortune.com/article/tech-investor-bill-gurley-says-college-grads-at-high-risk-of-ai-automation/](https://fortune.com/article/tech-investor-bill-gurley-says-college-grads-at-high-risk-of-ai-automation/)  
**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
Venture capitalist Bill Gurley argues that workers who passively followed a 'safe' career path without genuine passion or purpose are most vulnerable to AI-driven job disruption, while those who are engaged and AI-savvy in their roles will be more resilient. He also criticizes how the college admissions process pressures students into narrow career tracks before they can discover what truly interests them.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This connects to classic work on 'calling' vs. 'job' orientations (Wrzesniewski et al.) and intrinsic motivation theory—employees who experience their work as meaningful tend to be more engaged, adaptable, and resistant to disengagement-driven turnover or obsolescence, which Gurley frames here as resistance to AI disruption.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs should treat employee engagement and purpose-alignment not just as retention tools but as resilience strategies: actively helping employees find meaning and ownership in their roles (via job crafting, internal mobility, or skill development) may better prepare the workforce for technological disruption than compliance-based reskilling programs alone.
