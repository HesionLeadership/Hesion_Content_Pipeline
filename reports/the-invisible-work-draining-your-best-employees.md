# The Invisible Work Draining Your Best Employees

## Summary
The article explores how invisible cognitive labor—such as tracking deadlines, anticipating problems, managing processes, and coordinating across teams—disproportionately burdens top performers and leads to burnout, disengagement, and turnover.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The concept of 'mental load' or cognitive labor maps directly onto research on organizational citizenship behavior (OCB) and role overload, where high performers absorb unrecognized, unrewarded tasks that accumulate into chronic stress and inequitable workload distribution.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should audit and redesign work structures to make invisible cognitive labor visible, formally distributed, and recognized—rather than simply reducing task volume—to protect high performers from burnout and reduce turnover risk.
