## Summary
An MIT Sloan/BCG expert panel on responsible AI debates whether preserving trustworthy human judgment is the defining challenge as AI capabilities grow, with 65% agreeing, 19% disagreeing, and 15% neutral. The piece argues organizations must deliberately design systems that identify where human judgment matters and build in opportunities to exercise it, rather than assuming human oversight alone guarantees trustworthiness.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story directly engages skill atrophy, deskilling, and erosion of professional judgment through automation reliance—core topics in organizational psychology research on human-automation interaction, cognitive offloading, and expertise development through deliberate practice.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should proactively map decision points where human judgment is mission-critical (high-stakes, novel, or low-precedent situations) and intentionally design workflows that preserve skill-building opportunities and accountability there, rather than defaulting to full automation or token human-in-the-loop checkpoints that create accountability without actual judgment capacity.
