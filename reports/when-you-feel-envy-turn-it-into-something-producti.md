## Summary
Executive coach Stacy Kim argues that workplace envy, often seen as purely toxic, can be reframed as useful information about what people truly want in their careers. She distinguishes 'malicious' envy (focused on tearing others down) from 'benign' envy (focused on emulating admired qualities), and introduces a SIRN framework (Similar target, Intangible desire, Relevant comparison, and a fourth element not yet covered in this excerpt) to help individuals and leaders convert envy into motivation rather than resentment.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This connects to social comparison theory and emotion regulation research—showing how an emotion typically viewed as destructive can reveal unmet needs, intrinsic motivators, and growth opportunities if leaders create psychologically safe conditions for discussing it.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should treat visible signs of employee envy (over promotions, recognition, assignments) not as a problem to suppress but as diagnostic data about unmet career aspirations; building a culture where people can openly discuss admiration/envy (rather than hiding or weaponizing it) can turn rivalry into mentorship, learning, and better talent retention conversations.
