## Summary
The article examines two competing hypotheses about generative AI's effect on sales performance: one suggesting AI narrows gaps by giving low performers templates and coaching, another suggesting it widens gaps by freeing top performers to focus on high-value work where they already excel. The piece explores which effect dominates in real jobs involving bundles of tasks, with implications for hiring, training, and team structure.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This touches on individual differences in skill acquisition and technology adoption—specifically how existing competencies and self-regulation may interact with AI tools to either compound advantages (Matthew effect) or compensate for weaknesses, a well-studied dynamic in human capital and training transfer research.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should audit whether AI deployment is being used as a leveling tool for development or as leverage for already-strong performers, and intentionally design training and workflow integration to match their strategic goal (either narrowing or deliberately widening performance gaps).
