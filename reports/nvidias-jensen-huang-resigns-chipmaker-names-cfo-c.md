## Summary
Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned after admitting to a romantic affair with an alleged Chinese spy, triggering a national security controversy; CFO Colette Kress becomes interim CEO while the board scrambles to find a permanent successor amid a lack of clear succession planning.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The crisis exposes the risks of founder-centric, highly centralized leadership structures—Huang ran an unusually flat organization with ~60 direct reports and no designated successor, leaving the company structurally unprepared for a sudden leadership transition despite having formal governance power to remove him.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should treat succession planning as a continuous governance requirement, not a contingency reserved for retirement—even (especially) for high-performing founder-CEOs, since concentrated decision authority and absent deputy structures amplify organizational fragility when personal conduct issues or unexpected departures occur.

## Strength Score
**7/10** — While the scandal itself is a personal/political matter outside scope, the organizational dynamics—absence of succession planning, span-of-control risk from 60 direct reports, and governance mechanisms enabling board action against a founder—are substantive and well-documented org psych and corporate governance issues, not merely incidental details.
