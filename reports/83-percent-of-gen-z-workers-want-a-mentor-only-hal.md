# 83 Percent of Gen-Z Workers Want a Mentor. Only Half Have One

**Published:** Sat, 12 Sep 2026 05:00:00 -0400

## Summary
83% of Gen-Z workers desire workplace mentorship, but only about half currently have a mentor. The story explores why most workplace mentorship programs fail—citing lack of structure, poor matching, and insufficient organizational commitment—and offers expert recommendations for building effective mentoring relationships.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
Mentorship serves as a critical mechanism for newcomer socialization, career development, and psychological safety, particularly for younger employees navigating early-career identity formation. The gap between desire and availability signals a systemic organizational failure in developmental support structures.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should treat mentorship not as an informal perk but as a structured talent development initiative—investing in intentional matching, training mentors, setting expectations, and measuring outcomes to close the mentorship gap and improve Gen-Z retention and engagement.
