# Gen Z believes using AI is making their colleagues dumb and lazy—but may paradoxically see it as key to their own promotion, Wharton says

**Source:** Fortune  
**Published:** Sun, 15 Nov 2026 14:28:58 +0000  
**URL:** [https://fortune.com/article/why-does-gen-z-believe-using-ai-is-making-colleagues-dumb-lazy/](https://fortune.com/article/why-does-gen-z-believe-using-ai-is-making-colleagues-dumb-lazy/)  
**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
A Wharton-led survey of nearly 2,500 U.S. young adults (18-28) finds Gen Z increasingly uses AI at work despite majority concerns it makes people lazier (79%) and less smart (62%), with researchers attributing this paradox to present-bias and the better-than-average effect. Experts suggest employers should embrace ambivalence rather than ban AI, focusing on distinguishing 'crap' (outsourceable friction) from 'craft' (skill-building effort) to avoid a looming critical-thinking gap in talent pipelines.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story illustrates classic cognitive biases—present bias/temporal discounting and the better-than-average effect—shaping technology adoption behavior, plus a generational tension between individual performance incentives and collective skill-building norms.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs should design AI governance around task type (routine/friction vs. skill-building/craft) rather than blanket bans, and should anticipate that early-career critical-thinking and skill development may erode without deliberate scaffolding, creating future talent-pipeline risk.
