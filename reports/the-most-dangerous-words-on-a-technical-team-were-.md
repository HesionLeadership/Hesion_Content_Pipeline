# The Most Dangerous Words on a Technical Team? ‘We’re Aligned’

**Source:** Inc  
**Published:** Sat, 05 Sep 2026 12:00:00 -0400  
**URL:** [https://www.inc.com/neil-thompson/your-team-doesnt-need-more-expertise-it-needs-shared-understanding/91398391](https://www.inc.com/neil-thompson/your-team-doesnt-need-more-expertise-it-needs-shared-understanding/91398391)

## Summary
The article argues that the phrase 'we're aligned' on technical teams is dangerous because it creates an illusion of shared understanding when team members may actually have very different mental models of what was agreed upon. True alignment requires explicitly surfacing assumptions, defining terms, and confirming understanding beyond surface-level agreement.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This maps directly to research on shared mental models and the illusion of consensus—teams often mistake verbal agreement for cognitive alignment, leading to coordination failures, rework, and interpersonal conflict when mismatched expectations surface during execution.

## Leadership Lesson
Leaders should institutionalize practices that pressure-test alignment rather than accepting it at face value. This means requiring teams to articulate decisions in writing, using techniques like 'playback' (having each person restate what was agreed upon in their own words), and creating psychological safety for people to say 'I'm not sure we mean the same thing.' CHROs and COOs should train managers to treat stated alignment as a hypothesis to be verified, not a conclusion.
