## Summary
The article explores how corporate boards are increasingly deploying AI tools—from summarizing documents to acting as devil's advocates or activist-investor simulators—with some companies (Kazakhstan's Samruk-Kazyna, Abu Dhabi's IHC) even giving AI formal or symbolic board seats, while governance experts warn about groupthink, lack of accountability, and unclear data policies.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story highlights classic group decision-making dynamics—groupthink, devil's advocate roles, and bias mitigation—and examines whether AI can fill the social-psychological function of a dissenting voice without triggering the interpersonal discomfort that normally suppresses dissent in small, high-status groups.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should recognize that AI's value in high-stakes group settings lies in structurally enforcing dissent and challenge (reducing groupthink), not in replacing human judgment or accountability; leaders should pair AI's analytical rigor with deliberate processes that preserve trust-building and legal/ethical accountability, which research shows AI currently cannot replicate.
