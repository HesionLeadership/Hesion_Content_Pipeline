# What happened when Meta tried to collect employee data

**Source:** Fast Company  
**Published:** 2026-09-11T20:30:00  
**URL:** [https://www.fastcompany.com/91606241/what-happened-when-meta-tried-to-collect-employee-data](https://www.fastcompany.com/91606241/what-happened-when-meta-tried-to-collect-employee-data)

## Summary
Meta's attempt to collect employee keystroke and mouse movement data for AI training through its Model Capability Initiative (MCI) sparked an internal revolt, with over 1,800 employees signing a petition against the mandatory program. After a data privacy breach revealed collected data—including private conversations—was accessible company-wide, Meta suspended the program, though CTO Bosworth maintained that dissenting employees 'made the entire company worse.'

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This case illustrates a profound breakdown in procedural justice and organizational trust: a siloed AI division imposed a mandatory surveillance program without employee input, triggering collective resistance. Leadership's dismissive response to legitimate concerns (Bosworth blaming dissenters) exemplifies how defensive leadership amplifies rather than resolves trust violations.

## Leadership Lesson
Before implementing programs that fundamentally alter the employee-employer psychological contract—especially surveillance or data collection—leaders must involve affected employees in the design process, provide genuine opt-out mechanisms, and treat pushback as valuable signal rather than insubordination. Dismissing organized dissent as harmful to the company erodes trust further and validates employees' worst fears about leadership intent.

## Strength Score
**9/10** — This is an exceptional org psych case study touching on multiple core constructs: procedural justice (mandatory program with no opt-out), psychological contract violation (surveillance of work behavior), organizational trust erosion, collective voice and employee activism, silo effects in decision-making, and leadership response to dissent. Bosworth's framing of dissenters as making the company worse is a textbook example of how leaders can deepen organizational conflict through attribution errors.

## Original Story Summary
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">When employees at Meta learned that the company would start <a href="https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/boards-policy-regulation/meta-start-capturing-employee-mouse-movements-keystrokes-ai-training-data-2026-04-21/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">collecting their data</a> to train <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/artificial-intelligence" title="AI">AI</a> models, there was a swift backlash. Many of them <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91542004/meta-is-using-mouse-tracking-software-on-employees-now-theyre-pushing-back" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">openly pushed back</a> on Meta’s plan to capture keystrokes and mouse movements, circulating an online petition and urging their colleagues to sign. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Within just two months—and after an internal leak—Meta has <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91563851/meta-hits-pause-on-tracking-employee-keystrokes-to-train-ai-after-internal-leak" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">put an end</a> to the program, at least for now. A <a href="https://www.businessinsider.com/meta-ai-program-prompted-internal-revolt-program-suspended-2026-9" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">new report</a> from <em>Business Insider</em> chronicles what happened internally as Meta shared its plans with employees, revealing just how quickly the program was shelved over employee concerns and a breach in data privacy. (Meta declined to comment when reached by <em>Fast Company</em>.)</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The idea to collect employee data reportedly came from the researchers in Meta’s AI division, who are largely siloed from the rest of the company. One of the researchers who spearheaded the program was Rylan Schaeffer, who reported to the company’s AI chief. When the program—which the company called the Model Capability Initiative (MCI)—was announced, it drew immediate outrage from employees, especially when they learned it was mandatory and nobody could opt out.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">According to <em>Business Insider</em>, when a cybersecurity engineer raised concerns about data privacy and said security seemed like “an afterthought,” Meta CTO Andrew Bosworth went on the defense, saying, &#8220;That isn&#8217;t remotely a fair characterization! How would any of this end up in &#8216;the open&#8217;? This is tightly controlled data.”</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Many employees were reportedly worried that the program would extract passwords and other sensitive data, like credit card information or immigration forms. Some of them also wondered whether MCI would put them out of a job and enable the company to replace employees with AI. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">According to <em>Business Insider</em>, the petition from employees who were opposed to the program eventually got more than 1,800 signatures. Workers even <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91542004/meta-is-using-mouse-tracking-software-on-employees-now-theyre-pushing-back" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">used physical flyers</a> to garner support and remind their colleagues that protesting their working conditions is protected activity under the National Labor Relations Act. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In June, Meta decided people could opt out of the program for up to 30 minutes if they needed to handle anything sensitive, but employees kept pushing for the company to drop MCI altogether. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The data privacy concerns turned out to be prescient: Toward the end of June, a Meta engineer sounded the alarm after finding that the data gathered by the program—even from private conversations—was accessible to the entire company. As one employee reportedly put it: &#8220;I swear we&#8217;re probably the richest yet most incompetent company to exist in tech right now.” </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Meta suspended the program almost immediately, according to <em>Business Insider</em>, and said workers would be able to opt out if it’s ever reintroduced. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">There seem to be conflicting accounts of whether the data Meta collected from employees was used in any meaningful way. Employees told <em>Business Insider</em> that one executive claimed the data was not actually used for AI training, while Bosworth reportedly said it proved more useful than anticipated.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In leaked audio <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WHhJ-GIF7Lg" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">obtained by <em>More Perfect Union</em></a>, Bosworth has maintained that the employees who were openly opposed to MCI “made the entire company worse.” </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But the blowback Meta faced from its workforce—not to mention the public scrutiny—indicates that the risks far outweigh the benefits of trying to implement such a controversial program. As <em>Fast Company</em> <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91530650/meta-tracking-employees-ai-training-legal-not-ethical" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">has reported</a>, it invites complex questions about the ethics of collecting employee data, even if it is technically legal to do so on company devices.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The vocal outcry from Meta employees also points to more public dissent from white-collar employees, who are often not the target of this type of workplace monitoring. In the lead-up to a sweeping round of layoffs in May, many Meta employees expressed their discontent through memes and websites counting down to the announcement, <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/08/technology/meta-ai-employees-miserable.html" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank"><em>The New York Times</em> reported</a>.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">As the AI boom continues to drive layoffs across the tech industry—and amid growing fears among the workforce that they could be replaced by AI—some tech workers seem newly emboldened to air their grievances about their working conditions. </p>
