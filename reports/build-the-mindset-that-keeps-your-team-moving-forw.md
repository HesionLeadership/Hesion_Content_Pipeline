## Summary
Researcher Michelle Gielan discusses 'resilient optimism'—a mindset where leaders acknowledge difficult realities while maintaining belief that their actions can improve outcomes. She offers tactics like reframing stress as a signal, triaging overwhelming workloads, and structuring meetings to surface progress and constructive input from teams.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This connects to research on leader affective displays and sensemaking: leaders act as emotional and cognitive reference points for teams, shaping whether employees interpret setbacks as controllable (high agency/self-efficacy) or fixed (learned helplessness).

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should train leaders to pair honest problem acknowledgment with explicit agency-building language and meeting structures (e.g., surfacing progress, inviting constructive voices) rather than defaulting to either false positivity or pure crisis framing.
