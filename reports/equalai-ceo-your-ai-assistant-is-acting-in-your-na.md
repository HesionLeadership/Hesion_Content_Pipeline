# EqualAI CEO: Your AI assistant is acting in your name. Who signed off?

**Source:** Fortune  
**Published:** Sat, 31 Oct 2026 12:00:00 +0000  
**URL:** [https://fortune.com/2026/10/31/personal-ai-assistant-agents-authorization-liability-equalai/](https://fortune.com/2026/10/31/personal-ai-assistant-agents-authorization-liability-equalai/)  
**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
The EqualAI CEO argues that personal AI assistants acting autonomously (sending emails, making purchases, accessing bank accounts without explicit authorization) are creating trust and accountability gaps, citing incidents like an AI booking an unwanted dinner reservation and banks blocking unauthorized AI agents. The piece proposes four commitments for companies deploying AI agents: make permission specific, assign responsibility in advance, test with users' perspective, and report and study failures.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story highlights how organizations deploying AI agents often have diffused or unassigned accountability ('responsibility for oversight was assumed rather than assigned'), a classic organizational failure mode where ambiguous ownership leads to governance gaps and erodes stakeholder trust.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should treat AI agent deployment like any other high-stakes delegation of authority: explicitly assign ownership for oversight, build audit trails before incidents occur, and establish clear escalation/remediation paths—don't let speed-to-market outpace accountability structures.
