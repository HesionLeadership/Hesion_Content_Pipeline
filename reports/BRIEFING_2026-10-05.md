# Morning Briefing — October 05, 2026

## This Week's Themes

Trust erosion and its repair is the dominant thread this week: **‘We need to trust our employees’: This billionaire CEO went from counting cars in the parking lot past 6pm to embracing hybrid work** (Fortune), **Amazon responds to data center backlash, says it no longer uses NDAs** (TechCrunch), and **OpenAI's safety firings raise awkward questions** (Fortune) all show leaders grappling with the gap between control-based management and genuine psychological safety. A second theme is leadership succession and shared-power structures, visible in **David Ellison just brought in a co-CEO to run his new empire: Meet Ynon Kreiz** (CNBC Business), **Goldman Sachs CEO succession planning faces one big problem** (CNBC Business), and **Mattel names Roger Lynch as CEO, replacing Ynon Kreiz** (CNBC Business). A third theme is in-group bias and tribalism within professional cultures, seen in **The AI industry 'hates crypto people'—but they may need them all the same** (Fortune) and **What a McKinsey partner's commentary on women CEOs reveals** (Fast Company). Finally, organizational identity under pressure recurs in **Chick-fil-A wants to stay a family business even as it expands in the U.S. and abroad** (CNBC Business) and **Sean Parker is rebuilding Stability AI around music** (TechCrunch), both stories of leaders anchoring to core values while scaling or recovering.

## Top Story

**‘We need to trust our employees’: This billionaire CEO went from counting cars in the parking lot past 6pm to embracing hybrid work** (Fortune). This is the clearest, most actionable org psych story of the day: a billionaire CEO publicly admits his surveillance-based management was wrong and pivots to output-based trust. It gives CHROs a concrete before/after narrative to use when challenging blanket RTO mandates in their own organizations.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate

**How to be tough, not a jerk — and why most people get toughness wrong** (Fortune) — its five-practice framework is inherently listicle-friendly and taps into a tension every executive secretly worries about: am I too soft or too harsh? Suggested hook: "Most leaders think toughness means being harder on people. The best leaders know it means something else entirely."

## Best Newsletter Candidate

**OpenAI's safety firings raise awkward questions** (Fortune). This story has the layered complexity a 500+ word newsletter needs: it's not just a termination story, it's a case study in the collision between confidentiality obligations and psychological safety. The firings of three safety researchers for allegedly sharing information externally — paired with a separate resignation calling the culture "broken" — lets Pete unpack a core CHRO dilemma: what happens when employees don't trust internal channels enough to use them? The piece offers room to explore whistleblower policy design, the legal-versus-cultural optics of "defensible but retaliatory-looking" terminations, and how regulatory pressure changes the stakes of ambiguous information-sharing policies. This is rich enough to connect to broader themes of trust, control, and escalation that Pete's audience deals with constantly, and it's based on full article text, so every claim is grounded.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example

**OpenAI safety employee resigns, claiming the company's ‘culture is broken’** (TechCrunch). This is a textbook illustration of High-Reliability Organization (HRO) theory in action — Robinson's direct comparison of OpenAI's "iterative deployment" approach to the redundancy-and-caution norms of nuclear plants and airports gives Pete a vivid, real-world contrast to use when teaching audiences why fast-iteration cultures can be structurally incompatible with safety-critical work.
