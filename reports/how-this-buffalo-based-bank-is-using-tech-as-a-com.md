# How this Buffalo-based bank is using tech as a competitive edge

**Source:** Fast Company  
**Published:** 2026-09-02T11:00:00  
**URL:** [https://www.fastcompany.com/91596458/michael-wisler-m-t-bank-pacesetters-2026](https://www.fastcompany.com/91596458/michael-wisler-m-t-bank-pacesetters-2026)

## Summary
M&T Bank CIO-turned-SEVP Michael Wisler transformed the 170-year-old bank's technology strategy by insourcing IT talent (now 80%+ in-house), building internal development programs, investing in data infrastructure, and deploying AI copilots to 15,000+ employees—while also investing in the Buffalo tech ecosystem through nonprofit partnerships and accelerators.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The shift from outsourced contractors to in-house technology teams reflects research on how organizational commitment and knowledge retention improve when firms invest in internal talent development rather than relying on contingent workers. The insourcing strategy also enables stronger organizational identity and culture alignment, which are prerequisites for successful large-scale digital transformation.

## Leadership Lesson
When undertaking a major capability transformation, leaders should invest simultaneously in building internal talent pipelines (including nontraditional pathways), consolidating fragmented operations for scale, and embedding the organization in its local talent ecosystem—treating workforce development as both a competitive strategy and a community obligation.

## Original Story Summary
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In 2018, Michael Wisler became chief information officer at&nbsp;<a href="https://www.mtb.com/personal" rel="noopener" target="_blank">M&amp;T Bank</a>,&nbsp;a now-170-year-old financial institution based in Buffalo, after spending years in technology leadership roles at Capital One. Wisler soon worked to invest in M&amp;T’s in-house information technology capabilities and talent, reducing the bank’s reliance on outside tech contractors, speeding the rate at which the bank can respond to changes in technology and customer needs, and building more resilient systems. It was part of a transformation to treat technology, and the experts who work with it, as a&nbsp;true&nbsp;competitive advantage of the bank.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That required investing in ways to attract skilled employees, including a technology development program that helps recruit new college graduates. Connections with universities in the region and across the country help woo students looking to start their careers, and nontraditional pathways attract additional talent that might otherwise be overlooked. Today, more than 80% of M&amp;T’s tech and data teams work directly for the bank, including at two technology centers that M&amp;T has built in downtown Buffalo. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“We&#8217;ve done some tremendous things,” Wisler says. “We&#8217;re way more <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/productivity" title="Productivity">productive</a>. We&#8217;re way more resilient.”&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Since 2025, Wisler has served in a new position as M&amp;T’s senior executive vice president for technology and operations. His role includes overseeing both the technology that supports roughly 22,000 employees and the core functionality that enables the bank to serve its millions of customers online and across more than 900 branches in 12 Eastern states and the District of Columbia. He’s worked to streamline and unify facilities like call centers, historically split up between different areas of business, and ensure operations teams are equipped to take advantage of technology and practices that grow more efficient at scale. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“As we got larger, we have a higher appreciation for scaled efficiency and consistency, where we can really bring to bear vast, predictable, capable, scalable capabilities,” Wisler says.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">As the bank explores using <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/artificial-intelligence" title="AI">artificial intelligence</a> for tasks like enhancing technical and operational efficiency with digital copilots, applying agentic AI to cybersecurity and fraud detection, and generally anticipating and meeting customer needs, that investment in computing and data infrastructure and talent continues to pay off.  </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“You can’t win in AI if you don’t know where your data is, if&nbsp;you can’t trust your data, if it’s not democratized and available to everyone,” says Wisler.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Already,&nbsp;<a href="https://www.americanbanker.com/news/how-m-t-bank-ensures-data-quality-as-it-implements-gen-ai" rel="noopener" target="_blank">the bank has deployed AI copilots</a>&nbsp;to more than 15,000 employees to help with tasks from analyzing call center conversations to drafting reports, used AI for generating code, and harnessed the technology&nbsp;to quickly spot&nbsp;customer&nbsp;needs&nbsp;and portfolio risk.&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“We can either help solve a problem that the customer&nbsp;may&nbsp;not&nbsp;see that they have, or need they may not see yet, or be drawn to a risk that the faster that we can get to that, the earlier we can mitigate that,” says Wisler.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">M&amp;T continues to explore options around both building out AI solutions in house and harnessing tools from outside vendors, including AI features of general-purpose enterprise platforms and technology <a href="https://newsroom.mtb.com/2026-08-19-M-T-Bank-and-RDC-AI-Recognized-with-Editors-Choice-Award-at-2026-Banking-Tech-Awards-USA" rel="noopener" target="_blank">specifically built for the needs of banks</a>.  </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">As one of upstate New York’s largest employers, M&amp;T also invests in the Buffalo technology ecosystem, including through connections with <a href="https://www.techbuffalo.org/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">TechBuffalo</a>, a nonprofit that engages young people in technology and connects them with local employers, and <a href="https://43north.org/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">43North</a>, an accelerator that invests $5 million per year into the local tech ecosystem. Wisler serves on the board of both organizations. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“Not only do we have an obligation to the institution to amass the talent so that we can meet the outcomes that we want to,” he says, “we have an obligation to this community, and to this region, to do our part to create it.” </p>
