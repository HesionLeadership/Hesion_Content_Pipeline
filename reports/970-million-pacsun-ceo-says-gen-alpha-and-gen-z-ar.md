# $970 million Pacsun CEO says Gen Alpha and Gen Z are ‘greatly misunderstood’—so she made a board of 12- to 22-year-olds to give her advice

**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
Pacsun CEO Brieane Olson created a Youth Advisory Council of 12- to 22-year-olds, plus a 300-member ambassador program, to give Gen Z and Gen Alpha direct input on business decisions, from store design to competitive strategy, aiming to counter stereotypes that young people are disengaged.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story illustrates stakeholder voice and participatory decision-making as mechanisms for engagement, drawing on procedural justice theory—giving affected parties input into decisions increases buy-in and perceived fairness, even when those parties are external customers rather than employees.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs can apply this by formalizing structured feedback channels (advisory councils, rotating cohorts) for underrepresented or stereotyped groups, pairing voice with context ('answering the whys') to increase trust and actionable insight rather than tokenistic consultation.

## Strength Score
**6/10** — This is a legitimate organizational psychology angle around participatory governance and stakeholder voice, but it centers on customer/consumer engagement rather than internal workforce management, limiting its direct applicability to traditional employee-leadership dynamics that CHROs typically oversee.
