# Morning Briefing — July 25, 2026

## Top Story
**Gallup CEO says fixing the workplace may be harder than colonizing Mars** (Fortune). Four in five workers globally are disengaged — back to 2020 levels — despite billions spent on engagement programs over the past decade. This is the number that should be on every CHRO's desk this morning: it means the standard playbook (pulse surveys, wellness stipends, pizza Fridays) is categorically failing. For Hesion's audience, this is a mandate to reframe engagement as a leadership systems problem — manager capability, job design, and structural accountability — not an HR programming problem.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate
**AI surveillance is destroying the workplace** (Fast Company). This story has the perfect tension for LinkedIn: it pits a practice that *feels* like accountability against research proving it backfires. The self-determination theory angle gives Pete credible depth without being academic.

**Suggested hook:** "The fastest way to kill performance isn't micromanagement. It's letting an algorithm do the micromanaging for you."

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**For women, being creative at work comes with a hidden cost** (Fast Company). This deserves 500+ words because it sits at the intersection of three themes Pete's audience cares about simultaneously: innovation strategy, psychological safety, and DEI — but frames them as structurally linked rather than separate initiatives. The insight that organizations are actively *demanding* creativity traits (risk-taking, assertiveness, independence) while *punishing* women who display them is a genuine paradox worth unpacking. Pete can layer in role congruity theory, connect it to the DBS Bank innovation KPI story (Story 3) as a contrast case, and land on concrete structural interventions — anonymous idea pipelines, blind innovation reviews, experimentation norms — that make psychological safety for creativity equitable by design rather than by intention.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**Stripe's organizational culture as an "idea factory"** (Fast Company). This is the rare case study that illustrates the *combination* of high performance standards and psychological safety — the exact quadrant most leaders struggle to occupy. Pete can use Stripe's writing culture (forcing clarity, reducing politics), leader participation in operational work (reducing information distortion), and the 268-alumni-founder stat as proof that cooperative rigor produces both business results and human flourishing. Perfect for illustrating the concept: **psychological safety is not the absence of pressure — it's the presence of trust under pressure.**

## Stories Worth Watching
- **"Job-hugging" trend is cracking — 50% now looking to leave** (Inc). Strong supporting data point for the Gallup story above; fear-based retention is about to unwind. Useful stat for client conversations about proactive stay strategies.
- **IBM CEO admits culture was too slow to change** (WSJ). Good legacy-org cautionary tale, but wait for the full interview — the headline alone doesn't reveal enough about *what* they're doing differently now.
- **Research: Why some junior employees work well with AI — and others don't** (HBR). Interesting challenge to the "just train everyone on AI tools" assumption. The metacognitive and dispositional factors angle could pair well with Pete's leadership development work — worth reading the underlying study.
- **DBS Bank makes innovation a KPI worth 20% of performance reviews** (MIT Sloan). Strong structural intervention example. Best used alongside the women-and-creativity story to ask: "If you mandate innovation but don't audit who gets rewarded for it, what have you actually changed?"
- **The invisible labor of likability at work** (Fast Company). Solid companion piece to the creativity/gender story — same structural dynamic (unrecognized contributions, gendered expectations), different lens. Useful for deepening a newsletter or workshop but not strong enough to lead on its own.

## Skip These
