## Summary
Travis Kalanick, ousted as Uber CEO in 2017 amid scandals involving sexism, corporate espionage, and ruthless tactics, has returned to Silicon Valley prominence with a new robotics venture and the backing of influential VCs like Andreessen Horowitz, who frame his ouster as 'corporate cancel culture' rather than deserved accountability. Kalanick himself expresses no regret, with allies arguing that shifting cultural politics (including the Trump era) have made Silicon Valley more receptive to 'founder's mode' leadership and forceful, rule-bending builders.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This story illustrates how organizational and cultural context shapes the rehabilitation of leaders associated with toxic behavior—what was once seen as disqualifying misconduct (fostering harassment culture, corporate espionage, retaliation against critics) is being reframed as necessary toughness given a shift in the broader ecosystem's values and incentive structures.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should recognize that accountability for toxic leadership is not permanent or universally enforced—it is contingent on prevailing cultural and political winds, meaning governance structures (not just cultural sentiment) are necessary to ensure leader behavior is held to consistent standards regardless of shifting narratives about 'founder mode' or 'cancel culture.'
