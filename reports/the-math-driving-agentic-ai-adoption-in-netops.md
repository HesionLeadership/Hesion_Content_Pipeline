# The Math Driving Agentic AI Adoption in NetOps

## Summary
A 2026 Omdia/Cisco study of 1,000 IT and NetOps leaders finds that over half of organizations already use agentic AI to act (not just advise) on network operations tasks, driven by alert volumes far exceeding human capacity (4,000+ daily alerts vs. ~20 resolvable per engineer). The piece explores how teams are redesigning governance to grant AI agents graduated autonomy, the risks of inaction versus AI error, and a perception gap between practitioners (who see AI increasingly acting autonomously) and budget-holding executives (who still see it mainly as advisory).

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story illustrates trust calibration in human-agent teaming—authority is granted incrementally based on demonstrated reliability, much like onboarding a new employee—and highlights how toil and alert fatigue function as retention risks for scarce technical talent, not just efficiency problems.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should treat autonomy-granting (to AI or employees) as an earned, staged process with built-in verification and transparency ('show your work') rather than an all-or-nothing governance decision, and should recognize that chronic low-value toil erodes retention of skilled staff even when headcount and performance metrics look stable.
