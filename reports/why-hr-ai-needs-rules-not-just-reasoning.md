# Why HR AI Needs Rules, Not Just Reasoning

**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
The article argues that AI deployed in high-stakes HR workflows (payroll, compliance, hiring) must be grounded in enterprise systems, permissions, and audit trails rather than operating as unbounded 'lawless AI.' It proposes a checklist for CHROs evaluating AI tools, including: operates within enterprise permissions, understands role/policy/workflow context, routes through proper approvals, keeps an audit trail, draws from trusted systems of record, and maintains human judgment and accountability.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The piece touches on organizational trust theory—specifically how employees and leaders calibrate trust in decision-making systems based on perceived legitimacy, transparency, and accountability structures, and how ambiguity (e.g., 'whose numbers are correct') undermines confidence in automated recommendations.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should treat AI governance as an extension of existing organizational trust infrastructure (approval chains, audit trails, systems of record) rather than a bolt-on feature, ensuring human accountability remains explicit wherever AI influences high-stakes decisions.

## Strength Score
**6/10** — The article has a legitimate organizational psychology angle around trust, accountability, and human-in-the-loop decision-making, supported by survey data on confidence in AI-assisted systems. However, it reads partly as vendor-adjacent thought leadership (tied to Workday) and lacks deep psychological theory or rigorous research citations beyond one survey, limiting its depth as an org psych case study.
