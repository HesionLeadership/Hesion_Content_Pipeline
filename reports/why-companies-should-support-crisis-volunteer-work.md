## Summary
Employees who volunteered during Hurricane Harvey's aftermath, with company support and pay, reported gaining resilience, adaptability, and creative problem-solving skills they carried back into their daily work. HBR research across multiple crises suggests crisis volunteering builds capabilities valued in modern workplaces.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story illustrates experiential learning theory—employees develop resilience and adaptive capacity through real-world adversity exposure, and crisis volunteering may function as an informal skill-building intervention analogous to stress inoculation training.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should consider formalizing paid crisis-volunteer programs not just as CSR or goodwill gestures, but as low-cost, high-impact leadership development tools that build resilience and adaptability—skills difficult to teach in traditional training settings.
