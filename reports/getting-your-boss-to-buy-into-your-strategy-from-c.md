## Summary
An executive coaching podcast features Muriel Wilkins working with 'Elizabeth,' a newly promoted marketing executive, whose CEO dismissed her growth-oriented vision for the department by lumping marketing in with other 'shared services' functions that won't receive additional investment. The coaching conversation reveals that Elizabeth had assumed alignment with her boss's vision without ever explicitly confirming it or sharing her full roadmap with key stakeholders.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story illustrates the 'false consensus' trap in organizational strategy—Elizabeth projected her own philosophical shift onto her boss without verifying shared understanding, building elaborate plans on unconfirmed assumptions rather than establishing explicit alignment through direct communication and stakeholder engagement.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should coach high-potential leaders to treat strategic alignment as an active, ongoing process requiring explicit confirmation—not an assumption inferred from a promotion or vague signals. Leaders attempting to reposition their function's strategic value must proactively socialize their vision with key stakeholders (not just their own team) before investing resources, and must ask direct clarifying questions rather than interpreting silence or ambiguity as agreement.
