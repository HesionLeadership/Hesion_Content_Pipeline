# Morning Briefing — October 21, 2026

## This Week's Themes
A dominant theme this week is the erosion of human judgment and skill in the face of AI: **What happens when people have to jump in to clean up AI's mess?** (Fast Company), **Why companies may be liable for AI errors** (Fast Company), **Team Dynamics Weaken When Employees Turn to AI instead of Colleagues** (HBR), and **The Defining Challenge of Responsible AI Is Designing Systems That Preserve Human Judgment When and Where It Counts** (MIT Sloan Review) all point to deskilling, automation bias, and weakened team coordination as AI adoption accelerates. A second theme is the shifting psychological contract around work and loyalty, seen in **'Parallel work': How holding down multiple jobs at once shed its taboo** (Fast Company) and **The hidden cost of being the 'reliable one' at work** (Fast Company), both touching on how employees are renegotiating boundaries, exclusivity, and reward for dependability. A third theme is leadership communication under uncertainty and institutional trust, visible in **ConnectOne Bank CEO: what Kevin Warsh's 'quieter Fed' can teach CEOs about communicating uncertainty** (Fortune), **Harvard Business School's former dean has studied CEOs for three decades. He says the role remains remarkably unchanged** (Fast Company), and **Flock CEO defends surveillance technology amid national backlash: 'We shouldn't pause progress'** (TechCrunch). A smaller but related thread is meaning versus metrics—**Recognition is a talent strategy** (Fast Company), **Brands should lead with meaning instead of product details** (Fast Company), and **Move slow and make things: What Japanese craftsmanship can teach business about quality, patience, and expertise when AI makes speed cheap** (Fortune)—all pushing back against short-termism in how organizations value people and brands.
