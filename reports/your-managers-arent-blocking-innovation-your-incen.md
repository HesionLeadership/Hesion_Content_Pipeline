# Your Managers Aren’t Blocking Innovation. Your Incentives Are, and They’re Working Exactly as Designed

## Summary
The article argues that innovation stalls in organizations not because of a cultural problem or resistant middle managers, but because incentive structures explicitly reward managers for protecting existing processes, stability, and predictable outcomes rather than experimentation and risk-taking.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This directly addresses the principal-agent problem and reinforcement theory in organizational design—when reward systems are misaligned with strategic goals, rational actors will optimize for what they're measured on, not what leadership rhetorically espouses.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should audit whether their performance management systems, bonus structures, and promotion criteria actually reward innovative behavior or instead penalize the risk and short-term inefficiency that innovation requires. Realigning incentives at the manager layer—not just declaring a culture of innovation—is the structural lever that drives change.
