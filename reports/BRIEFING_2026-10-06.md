## This Week's Themes
Three threads dominate this week. First, AI is forcing a redesign of organizational structures and trust mechanisms, not just tools—seen in **People will be your competitive advantage** (Fast Company), **The Real Reasons Why Digital Projects Fail** (MIT Sloan Review), **How Leaders Talk About AI Predicts Adoption** (HBR), and **AI Is Making Verification the Bottleneck for Companies** (HBR). Second, psychological safety is under strain across multiple organizations, from **OpenAI's safety firings raise awkward questions** (Fortune) and **OpenAI safety employee resigns, claiming the company's 'culture is broken'** (TechCrunch) to **Factory CEO just accused his VC board adviser of spying for Cognition** (TechCrunch) and repeated cuts in **Disney laying off around 300 employees in latest cuts under new CEO Josh D'Amaro** (CNBC Business). Third, burnout and recovery are recurring concerns, tying together **Optimism Is Now an Essential Leadership Skill** (HBR), **Research: Sleep Deprivation Is a Leadership Problem** (HBR), **Corgi Says Its 7-Day Workweek Is Voluntary. Experts Say That Answers the Wrong Question** (Inc), and **The rise of the 'two-job job': 61% of workers say they're now doing more than one role** (Fortune). A smaller but notable thread is shared/co-leadership structures, with Skydance's leadership rollout appearing in three separate stories this week.

## Top Story
**Optimism Is Now an Essential Leadership Skill** (HBR) stands out because it grounds a soft-sounding concept in hard data—an 11-year, 25,000+ person dataset using the validated LOT-R scale linking optimism to productivity, sales, promotions, and lower burnout. For CHROs, it reframes optimism as a coachable competency tied to self-efficacy, not a personality trait you either have or don't.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate
**Getting Your Boss to Buy into Your Strategy (from "Coaching Real Leaders")** (HBR) works well because every leader has lived some version of Elizabeth's mistake—assuming alignment instead of confirming it. Suggested hook: "A newly promoted exec built an entire growth vision—without ever checking if her boss actually agreed to it."

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**The Real Reasons Why Digital Projects Fail** (MIT Sloan Review) has the deepest org psych angle for a long-form piece. It's built on real research (564 physicians, EHR systems) that separates two distinct mechanisms—data quality and system usability—both of which get wrongly lumped into "user resistance to change." This is a classic attribution error that costs organizations real remediation dollars, and it gives Pete a chance to walk CHROs/COOs through a proper diagnostic framework rather than a generic change-management pep talk—exactly the kind of mechanism-level analysis that sustains a 500+ word piece.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**Why Sam Altman takes his hardest questions to Nikesh Arora** (Fortune) is a strong live illustration of the psychological safety vs. accountability tension. Arora's "brutal honesty, high-stakes framing" model (aerospace comparison) gives Pete a concrete case to contrast with fear-based cultures—useful for a workshop segment on calibrating candor without tipping into toxicity.
