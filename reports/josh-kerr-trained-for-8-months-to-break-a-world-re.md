## Summary
Runner Josh Kerr used data-driven restraint—deliberately holding back in training—to break a decades-old world record, arguing that most organizations obsessively measure output but ignore recovery and restraint as performance signals.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The story illustrates that sustainable high performance depends not just on effort and output metrics but on strategic recovery and restraint—a principle directly applicable to employee burnout prevention and workload management in organizations.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should build recovery and restraint into performance systems rather than rewarding maximum output alone. Measuring and managing cognitive and physical load—not just productivity—can prevent burnout and sustain long-term organizational performance.
