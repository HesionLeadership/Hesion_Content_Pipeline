# Morning Briefing — August 09, 2026

## This Week's Themes
The dominant theme this week is **the structural failure of leadership development** — organizations consistently promoting, rewarding, and evaluating leaders on the wrong things (Stories 3, 6, 10). Closely related is **psychological safety as infrastructure, not aspiration** — multiple stories expose how safety is unevenly distributed across gender (Story 5), undermined by fear-based management (Story 10), and eroded by HR's dual-agency problem (Story 8). A third pattern is **innovation as an organizational design problem, not a personality trait** — whether it's founders bottlenecking creativity (Story 1), DBS embedding innovation into KPIs (Story 7), or women being penalized for the very creative behaviors organizations claim to want (Story 5). Finally, **feedback and communication quality** keeps surfacing: vague feedback like "be more strategic" (Story 2), the stories organizations tell themselves about culture (Story 4), and the filtering of truth as hierarchy increases (Story 6). The throughline: most leadership dysfunction is systemic, not individual — and CHROs and COOs are uniquely positioned to redesign the systems.

## Top Story
**"Why we still hate HR—and 100 business leaders on how to fix it" (Fast Company)** — This one hits Hesion's audience squarely in the chest. The article exposes a structural contradiction that most CHROs live inside but rarely name aloud: HR cannot simultaneously be the employee advocate and the management enforcer without destroying trust in both directions. With 86% of workers reporting fear of their HR department, this isn't a perception problem — it's a design problem. For Pete's CHRO clients, this is either a wake-up call or a conversation starter they've been waiting for permission to have.

## Best LinkedIn Post Candidate
**"Nobody knows what it means to 'be more strategic'" (Fast Company)** — This has the highest shareability because every mid-career professional has received this feedback and felt confused by it. It's instantly relatable, mildly provocative, and offers a clean framework.
**Hook:** *"'Be more strategic' is the most expensive three words in corporate America — because they sound like feedback but function like a Rorschach test."*

## Best Newsletter Candidate
**"For women, being creative at work comes with a hidden cost" (Fast Company)** — This deserves 500+ words because the org psych angle is layered and counterintuitive. Most organizations are pushing hard on innovation *and* equity simultaneously without realizing the two agendas can collide. The double bind — where the traits organizations now prize most (risk-taking, assertiveness, independence) map onto stereotypically masculine behaviors — means blanket calls for "more innovation" can actually widen gender gaps. Pete could unpack role congruity theory, connect it to real audit mechanisms (whose ideas get funded, amplified, rewarded?), and offer structural interventions that go beyond "encourage everyone to speak up." This is the kind of nuanced analysis that positions Hesion as thinking two levels deeper than the headline.

## Best Workshop/Keynote Example
**"How a brilliant founder can become their company's creative bottleneck" (Fast Company)** — This is a perfect real-world illustration of the shift from **directive to participative leadership** and the concept of **learned helplessness in teams**. The Lauren Templeton case study gives Pete a named, concrete example: a high-performing founder who inadvertently trained her team to stop generating ideas. The practical interventions (speak last, separate ideation from evaluation, distribute institutional context) are immediately actionable and map cleanly to a workshop exercise where leaders audit their own meeting behaviors. It also pairs well with the DBS Bank story (Story 7) as a contrasting "what good looks like" case.

## Stories Worth Watching

- **Company Culture Is Built One Story at a Time (Inc)** — Good framework for culture work; the "audit the stories already being told" advice is practical and could become a diagnostic tool in consulting engagements.
