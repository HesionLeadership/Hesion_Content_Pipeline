# OpenAI safety employee resigns, claiming the company’s ‘culture is broken’

**Source:** TechCrunch  
**Published:** Sat, 03 Oct 2026 16:30:01 +0000  
**URL:** [https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/03/openai-safety-employee-resigns-claiming-the-companys-culture-is-broken/](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/03/openai-safety-employee-resigns-claiming-the-companys-culture-is-broken/)  
**Content basis:** Full article text

## Summary
David Robinson, a longtime OpenAI safety employee, resigned and wrote in The Atlantic that the company's culture is 'broken,' arguing its 'iterative deployment' trial-and-error approach guarantees periodic failures that grow more dangerous as AI systems become more capable. He argues AI companies should operate like nuclear plants or airports with redundancy and careful planning, noting he never worked with colleagues who had experience in high-reliability industries.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
This is a direct application of High-Reliability Organization (HRO) theory—Robinson explicitly contrasts OpenAI's fast-iteration culture with the redundancy-and-caution norms of nuclear power and aviation, and notes he lacked colleagues with cross-industry safety expertise.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs in high-stakes industries should audit whether organizational speed-to-market incentives are structurally at odds with safety culture, and ensure hiring/staffing deliberately imports expertise from high-reliability domains (aviation, nuclear, finance) rather than relying solely on internal iteration.
