## Best Newsletter Candidate

**When a Short Seller Came for Samsonite Over Accounting Flaws** (HBR) has the deepest org psych angle and the most room to unpack in 500+ words. The case isn't just about one luggage company—it's a vivid illustration of diffusion of responsibility and authority bias in governance: boards, co-investors, and Big Four auditors all assumed someone else had done the real diligence, which let a CEO's alleged faked doctorate and hidden related-party deals slide for years. A newsletter can walk through exactly how that assumption chain forms (prestige signals substituting for verification), why it's psychologically seductive (it reduces cognitive load and diffuses blame if something goes wrong), and what it costs when a short seller—an outsider with no reputational stake in protecting the narrative—finally asks the obvious questions. Pete can connect this directly to his CHRO/COO audience: credential verification and related-party disclosure shouldn't be treated as optional extras handled by legal, but as baseline governance hygiene that HR and ops leaders should actively champion in board processes. The piece also offers a natural "narrative vs. numbers" framework—how good storytelling (a charismatic CEO, a respected board, brand-name investors) can mask red flags that would be obvious if anyone stopped to check. That tension between narrative comfort and numerical scrutiny is exactly the kind of psychological mechanism Pete's audience needs named and operationalized, making this the story with the most substantive runway for a long-form piece.
