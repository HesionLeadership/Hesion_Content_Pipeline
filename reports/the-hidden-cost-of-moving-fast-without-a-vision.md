# The hidden cost of moving fast without a vision

**Source:** Fast Company  
**Published:** 2026-08-28T15:39:29  
**URL:** [https://www.fastcompany.com/91584971/the-hidden-cost-of-moving-fast-without-a-vision-management-advice-speed](https://www.fastcompany.com/91584971/the-hidden-cost-of-moving-fast-without-a-vision-management-advice-speed)

## Summary
The article argues that organizational speed without a clear, shared vision creates hidden costs including decision fatigue, incoherent innovation, and erosion of employee belief. It distinguishes between motion (mere activity) and momentum (shared belief moving in the same direction), contending that vision serves as 'leadership infrastructure' and an operating system for decision-making.

## Organizational Psychology Angle
The piece directly addresses how absence of shared organizational vision leads to goal misalignment, decision fatigue, and disengagement—core constructs in organizational psychology. The distinction between motion and momentum maps onto research on collective sensemaking and the role of superordinate goals in sustaining intrinsic motivation.

## Leadership Lesson
CHROs and COOs should treat vision not as a static statement but as an actively maintained decision-making filter that is repeatedly translated across all levels of the organization. Before investing in speed-enhancing processes, tools, or restructuring, leaders should audit whether their teams can clearly articulate where the company is headed and why—if they can't, accelerating execution will amplify misalignment rather than resolve it.

## Original Story Summary
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">On the surface, being a CEO and being an evangelical preacher don&#8217;t appear to have much in common. One builds companies. The other builds congregations.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Look closer, though, and the similarities are striking.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Both spend their days asking people to believe in something they cannot yet see. Both stand before a room, describe a future that does not exist, and then ask everyone to organize their lives around making it real. Both rely on charisma, conviction, repetition, and a certain sanctioned irrationality. After all, what is a startup but a congregation with stock options?</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Leadership, at its core, is an act of faith.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The future has always belonged to leaders who could see beyond the horizon and persuade others to walk toward it. Steve Jobs saw a computer as an extension of human creativity. Melanie Perkins imagined a world where anyone could design, not just designers. Howard Schultz saw a neighborhood gathering place inside a cup of coffee.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">At Motto, we spend our days helping leadership teams define and operationalize vision. We&#8217;ve learned that the strongest organizations aren&#8217;t necessarily the fastest. They&#8217;re the clearest. Their people understand where they&#8217;re headed and why it matters.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That work has taught us something that feels almost heretical in today’s business climate—speed is wildly overrated when no one but the founders can explain where the company is going.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-the-worship-of-speed">The Worship of Speed</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Business has fallen in love with velocity. Move faster. Ship faster. Hire faster. Pivot faster. Adopt <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/artificial-intelligence" title="AI">AI</a> faster. Every meeting becomes an action plan. Every action plan becomes a dashboard.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Speed has its place, of course. In a volatile market, hesitation can be fatal. Companies that move too slowly get lapped, acquired, ignored, or buried under a pile of better-funded competitors with fewer doubts and better distribution.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But speed without vision has a strange way of multiplying the very problems it promises to solve. A company with no shared picture of the future does not become more focused when it accelerates. It becomes more frantic. Meetings multiply. Priorities collide. Teams start sprinting in different directions while everyone congratulates themselves on the pace.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That is the hidden cost. The faster a visionless company moves, the more expensive its confusion becomes.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-the-hidden-costs-leaders-don-t-see">The Hidden Costs Leaders Don&#8217;t See</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The cost rarely appears on a balance sheet. It shows up in quieter, more expensive ways.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Decision fatigue.</strong> Without a shared vision, every important decision becomes another debate. Leaders revisit the same questions. Teams wait for approval instead of exercising judgment. The organization feels busy while quietly slowing itself down.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Innovation without coherence.</strong> Ideas multiply, but they don&#8217;t compound. Every team pursues its own version of progress until the company starts to feel like a collection of departments rather than a unified business.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>The erosion of belief.</strong> This is the cost most leaders miss. People don&#8217;t leave simply because a company moves fast. They leave because they no longer believe the movement is taking them somewhere worth going.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That&#8217;s the real difference between motion and momentum. Motion is activity. Momentum is shared belief moving in the same direction.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-when-activity-replaces-conviction">When Activity Replaces Conviction</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We see this pattern all the time. A company hits a new stage of growth. The founder’s original fire still burns somewhere, but the company has outgrown the informal myth that once held it together. New executives arrive with new language. New teams form around new priorities. The market shifts. The product expands. The culture starts speaking in many dialects at once.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Nobody calls it a vision problem at first. They call it a communication problem. Or an alignment problem. Or a process problem. That equals more meetings, more tools, more operating cadences, more OKRs, more town halls.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Sometimes those things help. Often, they create a better-organized version of the same drift.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The real issue is deeper: the company can no longer answer, with enough force and clarity, the simplest question in business.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Where are we going, and why should anyone care?</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">When that answer goes soft, organizations compensate with faster motion. Activity becomes anesthesia. Busyness lets everyone avoid the more terrifying possibility that the company is working very hard on a future no one has truly chosen.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-the-problem-isn-t-your-vision-statement">The problem isn&#8217;t your vision statement</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The word “vision” has suffered from years of corporate abuse. It has been stretched into bland declarations, workshopped into oblivion, and printed in fonts large enough to suggest importance without actually creating any.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But real vision is not corporate poetry. It is leadership infrastructure.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A strong vision tells people what future the company is trying to create. A useful vision also tells them what to ignore. That second part matters. Without a clear vision, everything can look like an opportunity. Every trend becomes tempting. Every customer request becomes urgent. Every competitor’s move becomes a reason to panic.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Vision gives a company judgment. It becomes the filter through which leaders make tradeoffs and teams decide what deserves their energy.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This is why the best visions are not merely aspirational. They are the company&#8217;s <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/91473272/why-you-should-treat-your-brand-as-an-operating-system-brand-strategy">operating system</a>. They show up in <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/section/hiring" title="Hiring">hiring</a>, product decisions, customer experience, culture, and brand. They shape what gets funded, what gets canned, and what gets protected when pressure hits.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-visionary-companies-move-differently">Visionary companies move differently</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The companies that move fast with vision feel different inside. Not calmer, exactly. Often, they are intense, demanding, full of audacity and ambition. But the energy has a direction.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">They repeat the future until people can use it. Not once a year at an offsite, but constantly, in language and behavior that becomes common property.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">They make tradeoffs visible. People understand why one bet matters more than another, which keeps speed from turning into chaos.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">They connect the daily work to the larger mission. The engineer, the designer, the salesperson, and the new hire can all explain how their work ladders up to the future being built.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">And perhaps most importantly, they treat vision as a living discipline rather than a founder’s private hallucination. It must be translated, challenged, clarified, and carried by more than one person.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-the-real-advantage">The real advantage</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">AI will make speed cheaper. Automation will make output easier. More teams will be able to produce more things in less time.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That means speed alone will forgo some of its magic.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The real advantage will belong to leaders who can make the future vivid enough to organize belief around it. Leaders who can integrate vision, culture, and brand so the company knows not only what it is doing, but what it is becoming.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Because people do not give their best years to a roadmap. They give them a future that feels worthy of the sacrifice.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Speed matters. But only when it has somewhere meaningful to go.</p>
