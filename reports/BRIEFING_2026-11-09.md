# Morning Briefing — November 09, 2026

## This Week's Themes

Three threads dominate this week. First, **governance under pressure when leadership becomes personal**: **Before news broke of his affair with a suspected Chinese spy, Nvidia's Huang told Fortune his company "could be of great service" to China** (Fortune) and **Nvidia is investigating Jensen Huang. But its board may have no interest in challenging its leader** (Fast Company) both expose how founder-centric companies struggle to activate real oversight when the CEO's personal conduct collides with strategic risk. Second, **leadership churn and mismatched timelines**: **Goodyear Tire CEO Mark Stewart steps down as company continues turnaround plan** (CNBC Business) shows how boards lose patience with transformation work before it can mature. Third, **trust and autonomy as adoption levers**, seen in **Why Leaders Need to Rethink Their Approach to AI Oversight** (HBR), **'Shock absorbers': Korn Ferry says managers are carrying AI change that executives aren't ready to lead** (Fortune), and **AI won't pay off for the S&P 493 until CEOs rebuild the work** (Fortune) — all pointing to the same gap between rolling out AI and redesigning the human systems around it. Counterpoint stories like **A CEO gave his employees Fridays off. Three years later, revenue is up about 160%** (Fortune), **Coffee giant Dutch Bros made all of its executives, including the CEO, do a two-week bootcamp where they learned how to pour drinks and run stores** (Fortune), and **Meet the 23-year-old college dropout already making six figures as Walmart's youngest U.S. store manager** (Fortune) show the upside case: organizations that build trust, flatten hierarchy, and create visible mobility paths outperform on retention and culture. Underlying nearly everything is **The 'charismatic' leader trap: Why organizations shouldn't depend on one individual, no matter how appealing** (Fast Company) — power concentration and succession fragility as the common failure mode.
